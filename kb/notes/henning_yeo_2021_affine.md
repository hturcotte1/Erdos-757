## What is known about it without the PDF

**Abstract (verbatim, Springer):**
> "A subset $T$ of vertices in a hypergraph $H$ is a transversal if $T$ has a nonempty intersection with every edge of $H$. The transversal number $\tau(H)$ of $H$ is the minimum size of a transversal in $H$. A hypergraph $H$ is 3-uniform if every edge of $H$ has size 3. Let $H$ be a 3-uniform hypergraph with $n_H$ vertices and $m_H$ edges. Tuza (Discrete Math 86:117–126, 1990) and Chvátal and McDiarmid (Combinatorica 12:19–26, 1992) showed that $4\tau(H)\le n_H+m_H$. Chvátal and McDiarmid also showed that $6\tau(H)\le 2n_H+m_H$. The linear hypergraphs achieving equality in these bounds were characterized by the authors (Henning and Yeo in J Graph Theory 59:326–348, 2008; Discrete Math 313:959–966, 2013). In this paper, we show that these bounds can be improved if we impose some structural properties on $H$. We show that if $H$ does not contain a subhypergraph isomorphic to the affine plane AG(2,3) of order 3 with two vertices deleted, then $17\tau(H)\le 5n_H+3m_H$. [...] total domination [...] if $G$ is a graph of order $n$ with minimum degree at least 3 that contains no 4-cycles and no specified graph on 12 vertices as a subgraph, then $\gamma_t(G)\le\frac8{17}n$."

**Main theorem as cited by Ma–Tang (arXiv:2602.23282v2, Thm 5.3, citing [HY21, Theorem 7]):** *Let $H$ be a 3-uniform **linear** hypergraph that contains no subhypergraph isomorphic to $F_7$. Then $17\tau(H)\le5n_H+3m_H$.* [published, via Ma–Tang]
- The abstract does not say "linear", but the title does, and Ma–Tang state it for linear $H$. Treat linearity as a hypothesis. (The abstract's mention of "linear hypergraphs achieving equality" in the older bounds suggests that the theorem is formulated for linear $H$ throughout.)
- "Subhypergraph" here means a partial hypergraph (not necessarily induced); this matches Gyárfás–Lehel's use for $H_0=F_7$. Ma–Tang apply it with $F_7$ = AG(2,3) minus two points $=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$.
- Which two points are removed does not matter: any two points of AG(2,3) are equivalent under the affine group, so AG(2,3) minus two points is a single hypergraph $F_7$ ($n=7$ points, $12-4-4+1=5$ lines remain: the 4 lines through each removed point are deleted, the line through both counted once). ## Independent facts I computed instead [verified-here unless noted]
Scripts: `experiments/20261013-2257-paper-digest/chk_fibonacci_F7_AG.py`, `chk_L13_L8.py` (exact brute force); `hy_tight_search.py` is a crude random search (heuristic).
- **$F_7$ is exactly the minimal obstruction to $17\tau\le5n+3m$:** $\tau(F_7)=3$, $n=7$, $m=5$: $17\cdot3=51>50=5\cdot7+3\cdot5$. $F_8$ = AG(2,3) minus one point ($n=8,m=8,\tau=4$): $68>64$. AG(2,3) ($n=9,m=12,\tau=5$): $85>81$. So the $F_7$-freeness hypothesis is needed, and the theorem is sharp in the sense that every hypergraph in the 4-τ-equality family ($F_7$, $F_8$ in Thm 2 of 1802.01825) violates it.
- **Slack $\delta(H)=5n+3m-17\tau$ is additive over connected components**, so tight examples ($\delta=0$) would have to be connected. Single edge: $\delta=1$ ($n=3,m=1,\tau=1$).
- $L(13)=\{\{i,i+1,i+3\}\}\subset\mathbb Z_{13}$ (Gyárfás–Lehel's extremal piece for $\varepsilon\le1/26$) is linear and $F_7$-free with $n=m=13$, $\tau=6$: $\delta=104-102=2$. It is not an AP hypergraph ($m=n>n-2$).
- Random-greedy search (60 s each, maximal linear $F_7$-free hypergraphs, then 0–3 random edge deletions; weak) found minimum $\delta=1$ at $n=9$ ($m=8$, $\tau=4$, edges `(4,5,7),(0,1,4),(6,7,8),(2,5,6),(1,2,8),(2,3,7),(0,3,6),(3,5,8)`), $\delta=3$ at $n=10,11$, $\delta=6$ at $n=12$, $\delta=8$ at $n=13$. [heuristic] No $\delta=0$ example was found, and this says nothing about whether one exists.
- Known AP-hypergraph slacks: $A_{\rm base}$ has $\delta=4$; 12-point sets $\delta=5$ (kb/00_START_HERE.md §6). Since $m\le n-2$ for AP hypergraphs, $D=\delta+3(n-m)\ge6$ always.

## Exactly what transfers to #757 and how
- It is the **only** published inequality giving $9/17$: $17\tau\le5n+3m$ with $m\le n-2$ $\Rightarrow\ 17\tau\le8n-6\Rightarrow h\ge(9n+6)/17$.
- To go beyond $9/17$ one needs either (i) more forbidden configurations (any realizability obstruction for AP hypergraphs besides $F_7$) fed into a Henning–Yeo-type bound with an $F_7$-free-and-$X$-free hypothesis, or (ii) a different inequality exploiting that AP hypergraphs have $m\le n-2$ and distinct midpoints (the HY inequality uses $m$ only through the coefficient 3). Whether the HY bound is tight in the range $m\le n-2$ is unknown to me, and it is the key question for deciding whether the HY approach can be improved at all.
- The slack identity $D=\delta+3(n-m)$ in kb/00_START_HERE.md §3 is a restatement of HY; knowing the minimum $\delta$ among *realizable* hypergraphs is the real target.
