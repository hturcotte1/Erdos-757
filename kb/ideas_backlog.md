# Ideas backlog (speculative; nothing here is established)

Pick from here when a track stalls. Move an idea to `kb/dead_ends.md` or `results/LEDGER.md` once tested.

1. **Harmonic-measure view of saturated sets.** Values are hitting probabilities of a random walk on the midpoint map. Potential theory, electrical networks and Markov-chain tools might constrain how many APs can coexist without forcing a parallelogram. **Status 2026-10-15: untested** (no experiment uses it; the harmonic description is only recorded in START_HERE §6).
2. **Lattice constructions.** Translation-invariant AP structures in $\mathbb Z^2$ or $\mathbb Z^3$ with long-range edges, projected generically (Freiman invariance makes this legal). One-dimensional chains are dead (D5), so try 2D tilings whose AP hypergraph has independence density below 4/7. **Status: untested** (Track U4 never started; the 1-D periodic case is dead, D5). Still open.
3. **Expander-like hypergraphs.** Low $\alpha$ needs expansion. Look for realizable "almost-designs": partial Steiner triple systems with $m\approx n$ whose AP realizations avoid parallelograms. **Status: untested as stated.** The nearest thing run: simulated annealing over saturated midpoint maps at n=16 and 18 (D16), which never reached $\alpha\le9$ [heuristic].
4. **CEGAR loop.** SAT proposes a hypergraph; linear algebra refutes it with a forced relation; the refutation becomes a learned clause. The learned clauses are themselves data for the forbidden-configuration library (L2). **Status: TRIED, no record.** Realizability-CEGAR is implemented (e757/cpsat_model.py, e757/realizability.py with Farkas certificates) and its learned cores are the forbidden library (K32, K33: 44 cores for n<=7, 287 for n<=8) [verified-here]. Witness sweeps at n=16,18,20,22,23,25 were UNKNOWN (D15, D18). Open sub-idea: cores for 9 and 10 vertices (Q7).
5. **LP-tested discharging.** Generate candidate local weights automatically: an LP over all small realizable configurations finds the best weights, then you try to prove the inequality. **Status: TRIED, no proof.** LP fit over 1,333,217 realizable hypergraphs gave the frontier $\tau\le(33/119)n+(3/17)m$ (valid on all rows) and $\tau\le3n/7$ tight only at A_base [heuristic: experiments/20261014-0020-lp-fit]; the inductive fit (K39) shows the best realizable constant is exactly $3/7$ and abstract data force $31/70$ (D17). Open: a discharge for the dominant local type (a vertex on three full traces).
6. **Flag algebras / SDP** on local configurations of realizable hypergraphs. The answer is a hereditary-family ratio, which fits the method. **Status: untested.**
7. **Stability of Henning–Yeo.** Show that near-equality forces many copies of a specific small configuration, then show those cannot all be realized. **Status: partial.** Henning–Yeo 2021 could not be obtained (LOG 2026-10-13T23:20Z), so equality cases are unknown. Derived here: degree bound in Gyárfás–Lehel-tight saturated hypergraphs (K44); falsifier found no counterexample to the GL-tight structure lemmas on about 80,000 instances (kb/notes/gl_tight_structure.md) [derived-here/heuristic]. Open: obtain the paper (scout, or a human).
8. **Variants over other groups:** $\mathbb Z_p$, $\mathbb F_p^k$, and $\mathbb F_3^k$ (where midpoints are not unique). These show which hypotheses matter. **Status: untested.**
9. **Fractional relaxations.** The fractional transversal number of realizable hypergraphs, and LP duality certificates as proof sketches. **Status: untested.**
10. **Program evolution.** LLM-written generator programs scored by exact $h/n$ (FunSearch/AlphaEvolve style), restricted to the feasible sizes in START_HERE §4. **Status: partly tried.** Annealing of midpoint maps (e757/anneal.py) scored by exact h: no $h\le9$ at n=16 (D16).
