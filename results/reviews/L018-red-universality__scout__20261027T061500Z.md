## Verdict by item

### (2a) Edmonds-polytope bound nu(G) >= 2|E|/7 for subcubic G (Theorem 2, Step 2.4): KNOWN (classical)
- It is the Delta = 3 case of the Chvatal-Hanson extremal theorem. Quoted in Hou, Qiu, Liu, arXiv:1610.00815, Lemma 2.1 (text read here, file pdftotext of https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.00815): "Lemma 2.1 ([3]). For any graph G with maximum degree Delta >= 1 and matching number nu >= 1, we have e(G) <= f(nu, Delta) = nu*Delta + floor(Delta/2) * floor(nu / ceil(Delta/2)) <= nu(Delta+1)." Reference [3] there: V. Chvatal, D. Hanson, "Degrees and matchings", J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 20(2) (1976) 128-138, doi:10.1016/0095-8956(76)90004-6.
- For Delta = 3: e(G) <= 3 nu + floor(nu/2) <= 7 nu / 2, i.e. nu >= 2e/7 (for e(G) >= 1; trivially for e = 0). So Theorem 2's bound nu(G) >= (2/7) n_2 is exactly the Chvatal-Hanson bound; the Edmonds-polytope proof in Step 2.4 is a different (short, fractional) proof of the same inequality, valid and not claimed new in the file as a graph theorem, but the file should cite Chvatal-Hanson (1976) as the source of the inequality. Secondary confirmation: Ma/Tang-style statement of the same theorem in arXiv:2405.18446 ("Two lower bounds for maximum matching", intro: "Chvatal and Hanson [1] providing a sharp estimate for the maximum number of edges in a graph not containing a matching of a specific size") and Hou et al. above.
- Related, NOT the same: Henning and Yeo, "Tight lower bounds on the matching number in a graph with given maximum degree", arXiv:1604.05020 (J. Graph Theory 2018): for connected graphs with maximum degree <= k, k odd, bounds alpha'(G) >= gamma n + beta m - K; for k = 3 this gives (reading the abstract formula; a derived value, not stated for k = 3 in the page text I saw) alpha' >= n/9 + 2m/9 - 1/9. Also Biedl, Demaine, Duncan, Fleischer, Kobourov, "Tight bounds on maximal and maximum matchings", Discrete Math 285 (2004) 7-15 (doi:10.1016/j.disc.2004.05.003), and Haxell-Scott arXiv:1406.7227 (which answers negatively whether nu >= (3n+n_2)/9 for subcubic graphs). I could not extract from these the verbatim 2m/7 statement; the Chvatal-Hanson citation above is the verified reference.
- The identity tau(H) = m - nu(G) for Delta(H) <= 2 (Step 2.2, dual graph) is Henning-Yeo, Electron. J. Combin. 24(2) (2017) P2.50, Prop. 9 (already in kb/literature/papers/henning_yeo_ejc_2017.pdf and kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md section 3.1 item 4, section 4 item 4). Theorem 2 (11 tau <= 3n + 2m for Delta <= 2) is the I11-analogue of the K10 route already in the project; the dual-graph argument is known technique, the I11 specialisation with the stronger slack bound 7 slack >= 21 n_0 + n_2 appears to be new but is a routine corollary of dual graph + Chvatal-Hanson.

### (2b) Gadget lemma Theorem 6(b') (minimality applied to the induced subhypergraph F_1 + v): no specific prior statement found; standard technique
- It is an instance of the generic "minimal counterexample, apply the hypothesis to an induced subhypergraph, compare tau and the potential" step used throughout Thomasse-Yeo, Combinatorica 27 (2007) 473-487 (Thm 1 and Thm 3 proofs, HAL copy in kb/literature/sources/henning_yeo/thomasse_yeo_2007_combinatorica.txt), Henning-Yeo arXiv:1802.01825 Thm 21 and the deficiency method. Nothing in these sources states the particular lemma (a hub vertex of degree 3 in a minimal counterexample cannot have a component of H - v containing two of its three pairs), which is specific to I11 and to the potential 3n + 2m - 11 tau. Searches of arXiv/web for such a lemma returned nothing. Verdict: no prior statement found; the technique is not novel, the lemma is problem-specific.
- Caveat: the Henning-Yeo 2021 paper (Graphs Combin. 37, 867-890; the source of the 17 tau <= 5n + 3m proof template) could NOT be read (see kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md section 1: paywalled, no arXiv version). If its proof contains an analogous hub-vertex gadget lemma, it could not be checked. Hence PARTIAL, not CLEAR.

### (1) Theorem 4 (non-locality of Conjecture RU on class C): no direct prior statement found; the mechanism is folklore
- Searched: "no local proof / discharging / reducible configurations / large girth / transversal" (web), Henning-Yeo and Thomasse-Yeo texts on disk (kb/literature/sources/henning_yeo/, kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md), arXiv listing queries. No paper states a "no bounded-radius reducible-configuration (discharging) proof exists for a transversal inequality" obstruction. Henning-Yeo and Thomasse-Yeo proofs are minimal-counterexample arguments whose reducible configurations are all of bounded size, but none discusses an impossibility result for bounded-radius configurations.
- Nearest known phenomena (analogies, not the same statement):
  - Locality limits for independent sets in graphs of large girth: Gamarnik-Sudan, "Limits of local algorithms over sparse random graphs", Ann. Probab. 45(4) (2017), arXiv:1304.1831 (local algorithms cannot find near-maximum independent sets in random d-regular graphs for large d); Lauer-Wormald, "Large independent sets in regular graphs of large girth", arXiv:1308.0266 (notes the large-girth locality statement is for large degree and "appears to say nothing about small r, say r = 3"); Hoppen-Wormald line of work on large-girth cubic graphs.
  - The Thomasse-Yeo/Henning-Yeo proofs of 4 tau <= n + m use only bounded-size reducible pieces for the general class; for Delta <= 2 the Henning-Yeo EJC 2017 paper abandons local reduction in favour of the dual-graph matching bound, which is a global argument.
  - Erdos-Sachs 1963 (cubic graphs of arbitrary girth) is the standard source of the large-girth input; Theorem 4 uses it correctly as the only external input.
- The Theorem 4 statement (every (Red) pair (Z,T), i.e. 11|T| <= 3|Z| + 2e(Z), has |Z| >= girth/2, with the forest lemma value(Z) <= 11 nu(G[W]) - 3|E_G(W)| < 0 for forests, Lemma 2.2) is, to my searching, new as a statement, but the underlying idea (large-girth graphs look like trees locally, so any bounded-radius argument only sees tree configurations, where a potential inequality that needs a cycle to be tight cannot be certified) is a standard one. The conclusion "no bounded-radius discharging proof of I11 on class C" should be phrased as an obstruction for proofs that use only reducible configurations of a given bounded size (as the file does: "P-tree-r is not r-reducible"), not as a statement about all proofs. Note also Remark 4.1: the obstruction is for the pendant-vertex configuration; it does not preclude local arguments centred elsewhere (e.g. degree-3 vertices), as the file says.
