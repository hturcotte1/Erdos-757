### Theorem 3 and Proposition 6 (own code, sharing nothing with realize_sdr.py, rexact.py, e757/ or judge/)
**Method** (`realize_generic.py`):
- A depth-first search over **all** midpoint assignments: each edge picks any of its 3 vertices as middle term, 3^m leaves before
  pruning. The node is the exact solution space V (Fractions) of the chosen equations x_a + x_c = 2 x_mid, stored as
  vectors r_v with x_v = <r_v, lambda> for free parameters lambda.
- Test at each node: the generic point of V is bad iff some r_i = r_j, or some 4-set has <= 4 classes of difference vectors modulo
  sign.
  - The set of non-(4,5) points of V is a finite union of linear subspaces, since |x_i - x_j| = |x_k - x_l| iff
    x_i - x_j = +-(x_k - x_l).
  - So it is all of V iff a relation holds identically.
  - Pruning is therefore sound: subspaces inherit identities.
  - A leaf with a good generic point is an actual (4,5)-set carrying the edges as 3-APs.
- **Why the enumeration is complete:** every 3-AP has exactly one middle term, one of its three points. So every realization lies
  in V for one of the 3^m assignments.
- This needs neither the injectivity of the midpoint map (K5), nor the extremes being the non-midpoints, nor the maximum
  principle, nor K3/K6. It decides exactly the statement of Theorem 3 and Proposition 6 from the definition.
