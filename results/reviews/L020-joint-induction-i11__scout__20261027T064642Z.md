### (1) Building long near-extremal families of linear 3-graphs by repeated gluing at a vertex: PARTIAL (technique known; this particular family and its slack bookkeeping not found)

What exists [published]:
- Henning-Yeo, "Transversals in 4-uniform hypergraphs", arXiv:1504.02650 (local text kb/literature/sources/henning_yeo/henning_yeo_1504.02650_4uniform_3regular.txt, Definition 1 and Lemma 9). The extremal ("bad") family B for tau <= n/4 + m/6-type bounds is DEFINED by operations (A)-(D). Quote of operation (D): "Given any B1, B2 in B, such that Bi contains a 2-edge {ui, vi}, for i = 1, 2, define B as follows. Let V(B) = V(B1) u V(B2) u {x} and let E(B) = E(B1) u E(B2) u {{u1,v1,x},{u2,v2,x},{u1,v1,u2,v2}} \ {{u1,v1},{u2,v2}}." So gluing two pieces through a NEW vertex x joined to a pair in each piece is exactly the shape of L020's "add v with edges {v}+P_j" (pattern (e) of Theorem 3, and the gluing in Theorem 1 where v = 28 attaches an (8,5,3) graph and a (20,15,8) graph). Lemma 9 (vi)-(viii) is the same package of properties L020/L017/L019 use as "free choice / pair richness / triple coverage": "(vi) For all s in V(B) there exists a tau(B)-set containing s. (vii) For all s,t in V(B) there exists a tau(B)-set containing both s and t if and only if {s,t} is not an (A)-pair. (viii) Let {s1,t1},{s2,t2} and {s3,t3} be three subsets of V(B). Then there exists a tau(B)-set in B intersecting all of these three sets." Also Lemma 9(ii): "tau(B) = tau(B1) + tau(B2)" under (D), and (iii) gives tau(B) as an exact linear function of n and the edge counts (the family is exactly tight). The L020 notions "NC pair" = non-co-transversal pair = HY's "(A)-pair" (only these pairs fail (vii)); "admissible triple is covered" = HY (viii). The proof technique ("minimal counterexample; track a linear potential; bad pieces glued at a vertex; counters of how many pieces an edge touches") is the Thomassé-Yeo / Henning-Yeo method; see kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md section 3.1 items 2, 3, 6.
- Thomasse-Yeo, Combinatorica 27 (2007) 473-487 (local text): Claim 2 "split a vertex", Claim 6 dummy vertex omega; minimal counterexample w.r.t. degree sequence. Not a family construction, a reduction toolkit.
- Henning-Lowenstein, arXiv:1401.4851 (local text, Theorem 1): for k = 2 or k >= 4 the extremal connected hypergraphs for tau <= (n + (k/2) m)/(3k/2)-type bound are exactly E_k and the generalized triangle T_k (no gluing families). They mention (p. 2) that for k = 3 "there are three infinite families of extremal connected hypergraphs, as well as two special hypergraphs, one of order 7 and the other of order 8" (Henning-Yeo JGT 59 (2008) 326-348; DM 313 (2013) 959-966): those papers are paywalled and I could not read how the infinite 3-uniform families are built (probably also by vertex-gluing operations; NOT verified). Those families are for 4 tau <= n + m and are non-linear, so they are not the objects of L020.
- Henning-Yeo 2021 (Graphs Combin. 37, 867-890, the 17 tau <= 5n + 3m paper): full text still not obtainable (Springer abstract only; no arXiv version; same status as kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md section 1), so an overlap with its internal extremal/near-extremal constructions cannot be excluded. Its abstract/Ma-Tang's citation say nothing on extremal families.
- Web search (this run) for "gluing operations", "bad hypergraphs", "extremal families linear 3-uniform transversal": nothing beyond the above.

How L020 differs [derived-here]:
- The graph class is C (linear, F_7-free, e(S) <= |S|-2), the inequality is I11 (weights 3,2,11), which does not appear in any source I found (also found by the L017 and L019 scouts); the objects are "near-tight" graphs with Delta <= 3 (slack s <= 1), not the extremal families of 4 tau <= n + m or of the 4-uniform bounds.
- In HY (D) the glued vertex x joins ONE pair from each of two 2-edge pieces into a 4-edge-and-3-edge structure; L020's gluing uses a degree-3 vertex v attached to three pairs, with e-/b-/f-configurations from two components, and the tracked invariant is the slack (s(F1)+s(F2)-2 for the e-pattern; same slack for Theta in Observation 7). The new content is (i) that near-tight graphs (not tight ones) exist at all sizes up to 29 in this class by this operation, giving the refutation of Conjecture T'' at n = 29 (45 classes; computer-verified, unrefereed); (ii) the chains (8,5,3)->(15,11,6)->(22,17,9)->(29,23,12) and (12,10,5)->(19,16,8)->(26,22,11) via the triangle operation Theta (computer, unrefereed); (iii) the exact classification (Theorem 3(iv),(v)) of how a degree-3 vertex deletion splits a near-tight graph, with the deficiency/Hall argument (3.6) that echoes HY's Claims D/E of arXiv:1802.01825 Thm 21 (kb/notes/hy_proof_technique.md 3.1 item 3).
- Recommendation: describe Theta and the pattern-(e) gluing as an instance of the Henning-Yeo "bad hypergraph" vertex-gluing operations (arXiv:1504.02650, Def. 1(D), Lemma 9(vi)-(viii)) in the class C at the I11 weights; do not present vertex-gluing of near-extremal pieces as a new technique. The refutation of T'' and the specific chains are project-specific.
- Caveat: HY2021's own treatment of near-extremal pieces (it must prove 17 tau <= 5n + 3m, whose slack-1 pieces are built from single edges) could contain similar constructions; unreadable.

### (2) Theorem 2: 7 s >= 21 n_0 + n_2 for Delta <= 2 linear 3-graphs: KNOWN (a restatement of Chvatal-Hanson plus the Henning-Yeo dual-graph identity), except for the packaging as slack

- Henning-Yeo, EJC 24(2) (2017) P2.50 (local PDF kb/literature/papers/henning_yeo_ejc_2017.pdf). Quote, Proposition 9 (p. 6): "If H in H_k and G_H is the dual graph of H, then alpha'(G_H) = |E(H)| - tau(H)." Here H_k = connected, linear, k-uniform, Delta = 2... (the paper's class H_k: "connected, linear, k-uniform hypergraphs with maximum degree 2"; L020 uses only linearity and Delta <= 2 via L017 Thm 7 Steps 4.1-4.3, and handles isolated vertices/ degree-1 vertices separately; the identity itself is the same: tau = m - nu(G*)). The paper says the identity is "well-known (see, for example, [7])" and gives a short proof. With n = n_0 + n_1 + n_2 and 3m = n_1 + 2 n_2 one gets s = 3n + 2m - 11 tau = 3 n_0 + 11 nu(G*) - 3 n_2 [derived-here, I re-derived this].
- The bound needed is nu(G*) >= 2|E(G*)|/7 for simple graphs of maximum degree <= 3 (L020 Lemma 2.1). This is the case d = 3 of the Chvatal-Hanson theorem: a graph with maximum degree <= Delta and matching number <= nu has at most f(nu, Delta) = nu*Delta + floor(Delta/2)*floor(nu/ceil(Delta/2)) <= nu(Delta+1) edges. [published: V. Chvatal, D. Hanson, Degrees and matchings, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 20 (1976) 128-138; formula seen as "Lemma 2.1" quoted in several citing papers, e.g. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18446 and the search results listed below; proof by Balachandran-Khare, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611842, "a new proof to obtain the exact upper bound for the number of edges", also credits Sauer et al. for d = m]. For Delta = 3: |E| <= 3 nu + floor(nu/2) <= 7 nu/2, i.e. 7 nu >= 2|E|. Equality extremal graph: K_4 with one edge subdivided (5 vertices, 7 edges, nu = 2), exactly what L020 C6 finds as the equality case. So Lemma 2.1 is a restatement of Chvatal-Hanson (d = 3); its Tutte-Berge proof is an independent short proof, not a new result. (I did not re-derive the CH formula from the original paper, which I could not access; I relied on the formula as quoted in the citing literature and on L020's own check of all 5,389 graphs with at most 10 vertices.)
- Consequence: Theorem 2's first inequality 7 s >= 21 n_0 + n_2 follows from Prop. 9 + Chvatal-Hanson in two lines (L020 step 2.2 is exactly that). The "connected" part (s >= (m-1)/7, n <= 2m+1, hence m <= 8 and n <= 17 for s <= 1) uses connectivity of the dual graph, a standard fact. Note it is NOT a consequence of HY P2.50 Thm 14 for k = 3 (18 tau <= 4n + 4m + 2, from the matching bound alpha'(G) >= (n + 2m - 1)/9 for connected graphs with Delta <= 3, as I derived by setting k = 3 in the formulas of Thm 3/14); that inequality has different weights from I11, so the two do not imply each other directly [derived-here, not checked in detail].
- Recommendation: state Theorem 2 as "a corollary of Henning-Yeo Prop. 9 [EJC 24(2) P2.50] and the Chvatal-Hanson edge/matching bound for Delta = 3"; keep Lemma 2.1's proof as a self-contained alternative; drop novelty claims for it. L018 Theorem 2 (same bound) is likewise known.
