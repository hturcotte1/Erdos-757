## Additional independent arguments for three of the sparse top cells

Not needed by the certificate, but independent of every program (they confirm Steps 4.1-4.3 by pure mathematics):
- Lemma (hypertree bound). If H is a connected 3-graph without two edges sharing two vertices, with n vertices and m
  edges, then tau(H) <= floor((2m+1)/3). Proof: the incidence graph has cyclomatic number c = 3m - (n+m) + 1 = 2m - n + 1;
  replacing, c times, a vertex v of an edge e on a cycle by a new degree-1 vertex gives a Berge-acyclic 3-graph H' with
  n + c = 2m + 1 vertices; a cover of H' maps onto a cover of H (new vertex -> v), so tau(H) <= tau(H') = nu(H') (Koenig
  property of Berge-acyclic hypergraphs: induct on a leaf edge e meeting the rest in one vertex v; tau(H') <= 1 +
  tau(H' - v) = 1 + nu(H' - v) <= nu(H')) <= floor((2m+1)/3).
  (27,13,10,2): tau <= floor(27/3) = 9 < 10; (27,14,10,3): tau <= floor(29/3) = 9 < 10. Both cells are empty for every
  Delta.
- (27,18,11,2): all 27 degrees equal 2 (3m = 54 = 2n). The graph G* on the 18 edges of H, with one link per vertex of H,
  is cubic, simple (linearity) and connected, and tau(H) = rho(G*) = 18 - nu(G*) (Gallai). tau = 11 forces nu(G*) = 7, so
  by Tutte-Berge some S has q = odd(G* - S) = |S| + 4 (S is non-empty since G* is connected of even order). Each odd
  component sends an odd number of edges to S; with q1 components sending exactly one, 3|S| >= q1 + 3(q - q1) gives
  q1 >= 6, and such a component has at least 5 vertices, so 18 >= |S| + 30: impossible. The cell is empty.
- (27,19,11,3) and (27,25,12,3) have no such short argument (the bound gives 13 and 17); they rest on the computation
  (Step 4.4, 4.5), which I reproduced (R10, R11).
