## Verdict by item

1. FOLDING LEMMA (Theorem 5): PARTIAL.
   - The operation in the graph case (2-uniform: x of degree 2, edges {x,y},{x,z}, y,z non-adjacent; delete x, identify y and z; tau drops by exactly 1, i.e. alpha drops by 1) is the classical "vertex folding" / "degree-2 folding" of Chen, Kanj, Jia for vertex cover / maximum independent set. KNOWN as a statement. L019's Theorem 5(c) tau(K°) = tau(K) - 1 is exactly its hypergraph analogue, under the extra hypothesis that x is co-transversal (automatic in graphs by an exchange argument, not automatic for 3-graphs, which is why L019 assumes it; in a minimal counterexample it holds by Theorem 1).
   - For 3-uniform (linear) hypergraphs, with the choice "one vertex y from the first edge and one vertex z from the second edge, identify, delete x, and read off tau(K) <= tau(K°) + 1 with equality when x is co-transversal", I found NO statement in the transversal literature I could reach: not in Thomassé-Yeo 2007, Henning-Löwenstein 2014, Henning-Yeo (arXiv:1504.02650, 1802.01825 Sec 7, EJC 25(4) P4.27), Bujtás-Henning-Tuza-Yeo EJC 21(2) P2.24, nor in web search of hitting-set (kernelisation / branching) literature. The proof (Theorem 5(b),(c)) is a five-line exchange argument, essentially the folding correctness proof; the novelty is at most (i) the observation that for 3-graphs the move is exact when x is co-transversal, and (ii) Theorem 5(d)-(e): the exact criterion for when the folded hypergraph stays in the class C (linearity iff no common neighbour; F_7 through the merged vertex; saturated set containing y,z), which is specific to this project's class C and has no precedent.
   - Opposite-direction relative in the transversal literature: Thomassé-Yeo "splitting a vertex" (Claim 2 of their Thm 3) splits x into two vertices, each in one edge (any transversal of the split hypergraph is a transversal of the original). Folding is the converse-type move (identify, rather than split) with a different purpose (it lowers n by 2 and m by 2, raising s by exactly 1). Thomassé-Yeo's "dummy vertex omega" (Claim 6) is the analogue of L019's gadget vertex w, not of the fold.
   - Conclusion: do not present the fold as a new operation; cite it as the hypergraph form of the degree-2 vertex folding of Chen-Kanj-Jia (graph case) and credit only Theorem 5(d)-(e) and the slack bookkeeping as project-specific.

2. PAIR-PAIR COVERAGE AND GADGET LEMMAS (Theorems 2-3, Lemma 0.7): KNOWN in technique, new only in application.
   - The gadget K+w(Q_1..Q_k), the identity tau(K+) = tau(K) iff some minimum transversal meets all Q_j (L019 0.7), and "delete a co-transversal vertex set and track 3|Z| + 2|boundary| - 11 (tau drop)" are the standard potential / minimal-counterexample bookkeeping of Thomassé-Yeo and Henning-Yeo ("+x - y - z - t" accounting; Claim B "no X-transversal of weight |T| meets all of E*(X)" of 1802.01825 Sec 7). Theorem 1 (slack floor s >= -2, Delta = 3 at the first open size) and Theorem 2(i)-(iv) are direct consequences of that bookkeeping at the I11 weights; I found no earlier statement for the weights (3,2,11), and I found no source stating I11 itself anywhere (consistent with the L017 scout report).
   - Theorem 3 (any two of three pairs are jointly covered) is a one-line consequence of Theorem 1 + gadget edge-deletion; I did not find it as a stated lemma anywhere. It is an application of the standard technique, not a new technique. Edge-criticality statements (tau(K minus g) = tau(K) - 1, Theorem 2(iv)) are the standard "edge-critical / 3-critical hypergraph" notion; a 2025 arXiv paper on 3-critical 3-graphs (arXiv:2512.24850, R.
