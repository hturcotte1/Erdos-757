## Statement
Let $A$ be a 12-point (4,5)-set with $h(A)=7=f(12)$ (an extremal 12-point set; by L002 these are the 54,243 affine classes of the census, all with $m=10$). For every pair of distinct reals $x,y\notin A$ such that $B=A\cup\{x,y\}$ is a (4,5)-set, $h(B)=9$.
Consequently no 14-point (4,5)-set with $h\le8$ (an extremal 14-point set, $f(14)=8$) contains an extremal 12-point subset: every 12-point subset of every extremal 14-point set has $h=8$. For the 2,910 saturated extremal 14-point classes this was already observed directly (K47: all 91 two-point deletions keep $h=8$); the new content is that the same holds for every non-saturated ($m=11$) extremal 14-point set, should one exist (open question Q11). As a by-product, every (4,5) one-point extension of an extremal 12-point set has $h=8$ (consistent with $f(13)=8$).
The statement says nothing about extensions by three or more points.

## Proof
All properties involved are affine invariants and $A\mapsto A\cup\{x,y\}$ commutes with affine maps, so one integer representative per class suffices. Fix a class $A$ (scaled by 4 so that the midpoints and endpoints of pairs of $A\cup\{x\}$ below are integers; the T2 line intersection points are handled with exact Fractions). $h(B)\le h(A)+2=9$ trivially (a Sidon subset of $B$ meets $A$ in a Sidon subset of $A$). The content is $h(B)\ge9$, shown by exhibiting a 3-AP-free 9-subset (Sidon by K4, since $B$ is a (4,5)-set by hypothesis) or by computing $\alpha$ exactly, in every case of the L001 analysis. Let $P_x$ be the finite set of reals not in $A$ forming a 3-AP with two points of $A$. Any 3-AP of $B$ that is not a 3-AP of $A$ contains $x$ or $y$; if it contains exactly one of them, that point is in $P_x$.
- Case T0 (no new AP): $S\cup\{x,y\}$ is 3-AP-free for a maximum Sidon subset $S$ of $A$; $h(B)=9$.
- Case T1 ($x\in P_x$ up to $x\leftrightarrow y$; $A\cup\{x\}$ a (4,5)-set: 21 to 46 admissible $x$ per class, 1,887,566 in total). T1a ($y$ in no new AP): $h(B)=h(A\cup\{x\})+1$, and $h(A\cup\{x\})=8$ for every admissible $x$ (computed exactly), so $h(B)=9$. T1b ($y$ in a new AP, hence $y$ a midpoint or endpoint of a pair of $A\cup\{x\}$): every candidate pair was tested exactly for the (4,5) property; for each of the 51,264,727 (4,5) pairs a 3-AP-free 9-subset was found and checked to be Sidon, so $h(B)=9$.
- Case T2 ($x,y\notin P_x$, some new AP, necessarily $\{a,x,y\}$ with $a\in A$ in one of 3 roles: $3\cdot12=36$ lines per class). At a generic point of a line the AP hypergraph of $B$ is $H(A)$ plus the single edge $\{a,x,y\}$; its independence number was computed exactly for all $54{,}243\times36=1{,}952{,}748$ line hypergraphs: always 9. Non-generic points of a line either put $x$ or $y$ into $P_x$ (Case T1) or lie on a second line; the pairwise intersection points of the 36 lines (19,247,288 in total over all classes after removing $x=y$ and points with $x$ or $y$ in $A$; 19,961,424 before) were tested exactly and none gives a (4,5)-set in any class, as linearity (K6) forces (two APs $\{a,x,y\}$, $\{a',x,y\}$ would share the pair $\{x,y\}$).
The cases are exhaustive, so $h(B)=9$ for every (4,5) two-point extension of every class. $\square$

Remark (where the computation is load-bearing). Case T1a also follows from $f(13)=8$ (Henning–Yeo, K8): a 13-point (4,5)-set has $h\ge8$. Case T2 also follows from theory: a T2 line gives $\alpha=8$ only if $a$ lies in every maximum Sidon subset of $A$ (the identity $\alpha(H+\{a,x,y\})=\max(\alpha(H)+1,\alpha(H-a)+2)$ proved in Lemma E of kb/notes/extension_lemmas.md and its Remark on the other roles), and the intersection points are excluded by K6. So only the 51,264,727 T1b pairs rest on the computation alone. Lemma U (Henning–Yeo applied to $H(A)-a$, linear and $F_7$-free by K6, K7) requires $17\tau\le5(n-1)+3(m-\deg a)$, i.e. $85\le55+30-3\deg a$ at $(12,10,\tau=5)$, impossible since every vertex of a saturated set has degree $\ge1$ (the second smallest point is the midpoint of an AP whose left endpoint is $\min A$). So no T2 line can be deficient; the sweep confirms it (no class has a vertex in every maximum Sidon subset; 48 to 63 maximum Sidon subsets per class).
