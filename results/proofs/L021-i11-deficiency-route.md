## Statement

**Conventions.** As in L017: 3-graphs $H$ with $n=|V(H)|$, $m=|E(H)|$, transversal number $\tau(H)$, degrees $d_H$, $\Delta(H)$; $H-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(H)\setminus Z$; $H\setminus e$ deletes the edge $e$ and keeps all vertices; $\partial_H(Z)$ is the set of edges meeting $Z$; a set is *co-transversal* if some minimum transversal contains it. $E_1$ is the single edge $(3,1,1)$. $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau$ (the I11 slack). $C$ is the class of L007–L016 (linear, $F_7$-free, $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for all $|S|\ge2$), and
$$C_3:=\{H\in C:\Delta(H)\le3\}.$$
$C$ and $C_3$ are closed under deleting vertices and deleting edges (L017 0.2; deleting cannot raise a degree).

**Definition 1.** For a rational $\varepsilon\ge0$ let $J_\varepsilon$ be the inequality $11\tau\le(3+\varepsilon)n+2m$. ($J_0$ is I11.)

**Theorem 1 (what suffices for goal (B)).** (a) If $J_\varepsilon$ holds for every member of $C_3$, it holds for every member of $C$. (b) If $J_\varepsilon$ holds on $C$ for some $0\le\varepsilon<3/17$, then $f(n)\ge((6-\varepsilon)n+4)/11$ for every $n\ge2$ and $c_*\ge(6-\varepsilon)/11>9/17$. For $\varepsilon=1/6$: $c_*\ge35/66=0.53030\ldots>9/17=0.52941\ldots$ ($35\cdot17=595>594=9\cdot66$).

**Definition 2 (the key potential, $\varepsilon=1/6$; integer form).** For a 3-graph $H$ put
$$A(H):=27n+35m-132\tau=12\Big(s(H)+\tfrac{n}{6}-\tfrac{11}{12}(n-m)\Big).$$
(In the normalisation of the spec: $11\tau\le\frac94n+\frac{35}{12}m+{\rm def}(H)$ with ${\rm def}=\mathrm{DEF}/12$; the vertex coefficient is $3+\varepsilon-t$ and the edge coefficient $2+t$ with $t=1-\varepsilon/2=11/12$.) Let $L$ be a finite list of connected members of $C_3$ with $A<0$; for $F\in L$ its *weight* is $W(F):=-A(F)>0$. An *$L$-family* of $H$ is a set $X$ of pairwise vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs of $H$, each isomorphic to a member of $L$ ("members" of $X$); $V(X)$ is the union of their vertex sets; $E^*_H(X)$ is the set of edges of $H$ that meet $V(X)$ and lie inside no member of $X$. Put
$$\mathrm{DEF}_H(X):=\sum_{F\in X}W(F)-35\,|E^*_H(X)|,\qquad \mathrm{DEF}(H):=\max_X\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\ \ (X=\emptyset\text{ allowed, so }\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge0),$$
$$P(H):=A(H)+\mathrm{DEF}(H).\qquad\textbf{KT}^L:\ P(H)\ge0\ \text{for every }H\in C_3 .$$
**The list $L^*$** (Theorem 7(i)): $E_1$ ($W=16$); the two classes of cell$(8,5,3,3)$ ($W=5$); the four classes of cell$(10,7,4,3)$ ($W=13$); the 19 classes of cell$(15,11,6,3)$ ($W=2$); the six classes of cell$(17,13,7,3)$ ($W=10$): 32 graphs, all with at most 17 vertices (edge lists in experiments/prover/L021/closure_results.json). Note: of the 13 connected tight classes with $\Delta\le3$ named in the spec, 11 are in $L^*$; the two $(14,12,6)$ classes have $A=+6$ and are not special; no saturated piece ($m=n-2$) with $n\ge11$ is special, since then $A=12s+2n-22>0$.

**Theorem 2 (KT implies $J_{1/6}$).** If every member of $L$ satisfies $J_{1/6}$ (for instance $L=L^*$, by L016), then KT$^L$ implies $J_{1/6}$ on $C_3$, hence (Theorem 1) on $C$, hence $c_*\ge35/66$. More precisely $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\le11(n-m)$ for every $H\in C$.

**Theorem 3 (the Henning–Yeo claims A–F).** Let $L$ satisfy (L1) every vertex of every member of $L$ lies in a minimum transversal of it, and (L2) $W(F)\le35$ for all $F\in L$. Let $H$ be a *minimal counterexample* to KT$^L$: $H\in C_3$, $P(H)<0$, and $P(H')\ge0$ for every $H'\in C_3$ with $n(H')+m(H')<n(H)+m(H)$. Then:
(A) $H$ is connected, $n\ge3$, and $H$ has no isolated vertex;
(B) for every nonempty $L$-family $X$ of $H$ there are no minimum transversals $T_F$ of the members $F\in X$ whose union meets every edge of $E^*_H(X)$;
(C) $H$ is not isomorphic to a member of $L$;
(E) $|E^*_H(X)|\ge|X|+1$ for every nonempty $L$-family $X$;
(F) $\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\le\sum_{F\in X}W(F)-35(|X|+1)<0$ for every nonempty $X$; hence $\mathrm{DEF}(H)=0$ and $A(H)=P(H)<0$.

**Theorem 4 (free hub: vertices of a minimal counterexample).** Let $L$ satisfy (L1) and $W(F)\le16$ for all $F\in L$ (true for $L^*$), and $H$ be as in Theorem 3.
(a) Every vertex $v$ is co-transversal: $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)-1$.
(b) For every vertex $v$ of degree 3: $A(H-v)=A(H)$ ("free hub"), $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\ge-A(H)\ge1$, and an $L$-family $X_v$ of $H-v$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_{H-v}(X_v)=\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)$ satisfies, with $e_v:=|E^*_{H-v}(X_v)|$ and $k_v:=$ the number of edges at $v$ meeting $V(X_v)$, exactly one of
 (D1) $|X_v|=1$, $e_v=0$, $k_v\in\{2,3\}$: the member $F$ is a connected component of $H-v$, met by $k_v\ge2$ of the three edges at $v$, and no minimum transversal of $F$ meets every set $e\cap V(F)$ ($e$ an edge at $v$ meeting $F$); $A(H)\ge-W(F)$;
 (D2) $|X_v|=2$, $e_v=0$, $k_v=3$: both members are components of $H-v$ and every edge at $v$ meets one of them; $A(H)\ge-(W_1+W_2)$;
 (D3) $|X_v|=3$, $e_v=1$, $k_v=3$, $W_1+W_2+W_3\ge36$; $A(H)\ge-(W_1+W_2+W_3-35)$.
 In each case (B) holds for $X_v$ in $H$: no union of minimum transversals of its members meets all edges of $E^*_H(X_v)=E^*_{H-v}(X_v)\cup\{$the $k_v$ edges at $v$ meeting $V(X_v)\}$. In particular, if $H$ has a vertex of degree 3 (always, when $L\supseteq\{E_1,(8,5,3)\#0\}$, by Theorem 6), then $-32\le A(H)\le-1$.

**Theorem 5 (edges).** For $H$ as in Theorem 3 and every edge $e$: either $\tau(H\setminus e)=\tau(H)-1$, or $H\setminus e$ has an $L$-family $X'$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_{H\setminus e}(X')\ge35-A(H)>35$ one of whose members $F'$ satisfies $e\subseteq V(F')$ (so $H[V(F')]=F'+e$).

**Theorem 6 (KT for maximum degree 2).** If $L$ contains $E_1$ and the class $(8,5,3)\#0$ of $L^*$ (edges $012,156,247,346,357$), then $P(H)\ge0$ for every linear 3-graph $H$ with $\Delta(H)\le2$. In particular KT$^{L^*}$ holds for all members of $C_3$ with $\Delta\le2$, and a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ has $\Delta=3$.

**Theorem 7 (computations; [verified-here as computations, unrefereed]).**
(i) Among the connected members of $C_3$ with at most 24 vertices, the graphs $F$ with $A(F)+\mathrm{DEF}^{\rm proper}_{L^*}(F)<0$ (DEF over $L^*$-families of proper induced subgraphs) are exactly the 32 members of $L^*$; every other connected member with $A<0$ and at most 24 vertices (328 graphs in 12 non-empty cells in all, 296 of them outside $L^*$) has $A+\mathrm{DEF}=0$ exactly. The stored lists for 25 and 26 vertices that exist give the same picture (they contain no graph with $A<0$).
(ii) KT$^{L^*}$ holds for every member of $C_3$ with at most 24 vertices. Hence a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ has $n\ge25$.
(iii) $L^*$ satisfies (L1) and $W\le16\le35$.
(iv) KT$^{L^*}$ holds for all 27,525 connected linear 3-graphs with $\Delta\le2$ and at most 12 edges (a check of Theorem 6; 7 of them have $A<0$, all with $A+\mathrm{DEF}=0$).
(v) (Pure hub assemblies.) There is no $H\in C_3$ not isomorphic to a member of $L^*$ with a vertex $v$ of degree 3 such that $H-v$ is the disjoint union of two members of $L^*$ and $\tau(H)=\tau(H-v)+1$. **Proposition 9 (Claim H, conditional).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ and $X$ a nonempty $L^*$-family minimising $|E^*(X)|-|X|$ and, among those, maximising $|X|$. Suppose $|E^*(X)|=|X|+1$. Let $\partial(X)$ be the set of vertices outside $V(X)$ lying on edges of $E^*(X)$. **Conjecture KT*** [conjecture]: KT$^{L^*}$ holds on $C_3$. By Theorem 2 it implies $J_{1/6}$ on $C$ and $c_*\ge35/66$ (goal (B)). Evidence: Theorem 7(i),(ii),(iv), Theorem 6, and Theorems 3–5 (structure of a hypothetical minimal counterexample).

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 0.1–0.7.) $n,m,\tau$ are additive over disjoint unions and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts; $\tau(H)-|Z|\le\tau(H-Z)\le\tau(H)$; $\tau(H\setminus e)\in\{\tau(H)-1,\tau(H)\}$; in a linear 3-graph two edges meet in at most one vertex, so the co-vertices of $v$ in different edges are distinct; $C$, $C_3$ are hereditary (vertex and edge deletion).
0.2 For $H\in C$: $n\ge m$, and $n\ge m+2$ if $n\ge2$ ((P3) with $S=V(H)$; $m=0$ if $n\le2$).
0.3 (Edge partition.) For an $L$-family $X$ of $H$ with $R=H-V(X)$: $E(H)=E(R)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{F\in X}E(F)\sqcup E^*_H(X)$, so $m(H)=m(R)+\sum m_F+|E^*_H(X)|$, $n(H)=n(R)+\sum n_F$. (An edge missing $V(X)$ is in $R$; one meeting $V(X)$ is inside a member or in $E^*$; members are disjoint, so an edge lies inside at most one.)
0.4 (Families in induced subgraphs.) If $H_0$ is an induced subgraph of $H$, every $L$-family of $H_0$ is an $L$-family of $H$ (an induced subgraph of $H_0$ is induced in $H$).
0.5 (Union of families.) If $X$ is an $L$-family of $H$, $R=H-V(X)$ and $Y$ an $L$-family of $R$, then $X\cup Y$ is an $L$-family of $H$ and $E^*_H(X\cup Y)=E^*_H(X)\sqcup E^*_R(Y)$; hence $\mathrm{DEF}_H(X\cup Y)=\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)+\mathrm{DEF}_R(Y)$. *Proof.* By 0.4 and disjointness $X\cup Y$ is a family. An edge meeting $V(X)$ and inside no member of $X$ is inside no member of $Y$ (those miss $V(X)$); an edge missing $V(X)$ is an edge of $R$, and it is in $E^*_H(X\cup Y)$ iff it is in $E^*_R(Y)$. $\square$
0.6 (Additivity of DEF and $P$.) If $H=H_1\sqcup H_2$ then $\mathrm{DEF}(H)=\mathrm{DEF}(H_1)+\mathrm{DEF}(H_2)$ and $P(H)=P(H_1)+P(H_2)$. *Proof.* Members are connected, so each lies in $H_1$ or $H_2$; $X=X_1\sqcup X_2$, every edge lies in one part, so $E^*_H(X)=E^*_{H_1}(X_1)\sqcup E^*_{H_2}(X_2)$ and the maximum splits. $A$ is additive by 0.1. $\square$
0.7 (Weights.) For $L^*$, computed exactly (Theorem 7(iii)): $W(E_1)=132-81-35=16$; $W(8,5,3)=396-216-175=5$; $W(10,7,4)=528-270-245=13$; $W(15,11,6)=792-405-385=2$; $W(17,13,7)=924-459-455=10$.

### Step 1. Proof of Theorem 1
1.1 (a) Suppose $J_\varepsilon$ fails on $C$ and let $H\in C$ be a violator with the fewest vertices. If $\Delta(H)\le3$ then $H\in C_3$, contrary to the hypothesis. Otherwise let $d(v)=d\ge4$. $H-v\in C$ (0.1) has fewer vertices, so $11\tau(H-v)\le(3+\varepsilon)(n-1)+2(m-d)$, and $\tau(H)\le\tau(H-v)+1$; hence $11\tau(H)\le(3+\varepsilon)n+2m+(8-\varepsilon-2d)\le(3+\varepsilon)n+2m-\varepsilon$, so $H$ satisfies $J_\varepsilon$: a contradiction. $\square$
1.2 (b) Let $A$ be an $n$-point (4,5)-set, $n\ge2$, $H=H(A)$. Then $H\in C$ (K5–K7), $m\le n-2$ (K5) and $h(A)=n-\tau(H)$ (K4). $J_\varepsilon$ gives $11\tau\le(3+\varepsilon)n+2(n-2)$, so $h(A)\ge n-((5+\varepsilon)n-4)/11=((6-\varepsilon)n+4)/11$. Hence $f(n)/n>(6-\varepsilon)/11$ and $c_*=\inf_nf(n)/n\ge(6-\varepsilon)/11$ (K1). Finally $(6-\varepsilon)/11>9/17\iff102-17\varepsilon>99\iff\varepsilon<3/17$. $\square$

### Step 2. Proof of Theorem 2
2.1 For $F\in L$: $W(F)\le11(n_F-m_F)\iff132\tau_F\le38n_F+24m_F\iff66\tau_F\le19n_F+12m_F$, which is $J_{1/6}$ for $F$. For $L^*$ it holds because every member has at most 17 vertices and satisfies I11 (L016), and $J_{1/6}$ is weaker than I11.
2.2 Let $H\in C$ and $X$ a nonempty $L$-family, $R=H-V(X)$. By 0.3, $n-m=\sum_F(n_F-m_F)+(n(R)-m(R))-|E^*(X)|$. With 2.1 and $n(R)\ge m(R)$ (0.2; $R\in C$ or $R$ empty):
$$\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\le11\sum_F(n_F-m_F)-35|E^*|=11(n-m)-11(n(R)-m(R))-24|E^*|\le11(n-m).$$
For $X=\emptyset$, $0\le11(n-m)$ by 0.2. So $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\le11(n-m)$.
2.3 If KT$^L$ holds for $H\in C_3$: $132\tau\le27n+35m+11(n-m)=38n+24m$, i.e. $66\tau\le19n+12m$, which is $J_{1/6}$ ($11\tau\le\frac{19}{6}n+2m$). Theorem 1 finishes. $\square$
2.4 *Remark (why the shift).* With the spec's literal coefficients $(3,2)$ the tight pieces have weight $0$, so the deficiency would vanish identically, and giving them positive weight only weakens I11 (the induction hypothesis becomes weaker, not stronger). Henning–Yeo's special pieces are *violators* of their base inequality; the analogue here is the shifted inequality $11\tau\le(3+\varepsilon-t)n+(2+t)m$, whose violators include the tight pieces. The choice $t=1-\varepsilon/2$ makes the deletion of a degree-3 vertex with a drop of $\tau$ exactly neutral: $27+3\cdot35=132$ (Henning–Yeo's key theorem has the same feature: $6+3\cdot13=45$). The edge constant of DEF equals the edge coefficient ($35$), which makes edges joining pieces cost-neutral (Step 3.2) — this is why trees of pieces and hypertrees with a perfect matching are equality cases, not special pieces (Theorem 7(i), Sanity checks). $\varepsilon>0$ is needed: at $\varepsilon=0$ ($t=1$) the weights are $W=12(n-m-s)$, the $(17,13,7)$ pieces have $W=48>36=12c$, violating (L2), and every tight or slack-1 graph with $n-m\ge2$ is special, so the special family would contain all graphs of Conjecture T″ (unbounded as far as is known). At $\varepsilon=1/6$ only sparse pieces are special: $A=12s-9n+11m$, so $A<0\iff12s<9n-11m$, which (with $s\ge0$) forces $m<9n/11$.

### Step 3. Proof of Theorem 3
3.1 (A) If $H=H_1\sqcup H_2$ with both parts nonempty, $P(H)=P(H_1)+P(H_2)$ (0.6) and both parts lie in $C_3$ with smaller $n+m$, so $P(H)\ge0$: contradiction. The empty graph has $P=0$; $K_1$ has $P=A=27>0$. So $H$ is connected with $n\ge2$, hence has an edge, $n\ge3$, and no isolated vertex.
3.2 (B) Suppose $X\ne\emptyset$ and minimum transversals $T_F$ ($F\in X$) whose union meets every edge of $E^*(X)$. Let $R=H-V(X)\in C_3$ (smaller $n+m$, as $V(X)\ne\emptyset$) and $T_R$ a minimum transversal of $R$. By 0.3, $T_R\cup\bigcup T_F$ is a transversal of $H$, so $\tau(H)\le\tau(R)+\sum\tau_F$, and by 0.3
$$A(H)\ge A(R)+\sum_FA(F)+35|E^*(X)|=A(R)-\sum_FW(F)+35|E^*(X)|=A(R)-\mathrm{DEF}_H(X).$$
Let $Y$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(R)$. By 0.5, $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_H(X\cup Y)=\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)+\mathrm{DEF}(R)$. Adding, $P(H)\ge A(R)+\mathrm{DEF}(R)=P(R)\ge0$: a contradiction. $\square$
3.3 (C) If $H\cong F\in L$, take $X=\{H\}$: $E^*(X)=\emptyset$, contradicting (B). $\square$
3.4 (E) Choose a nonempty $X^*$ minimising $|E^*(X^*)|-|X^*|$ and suppose $|E^*(X^*)|\le|X^*|$. Let $B$ be the bipartite graph between $E^*(X^*)$ and $X^*$, $e\sim F$ iff $e\cap V(F)\ne\emptyset$. If $B$ has a matching saturating $E^*(X^*)$, pick for each matched pair $(e,F)$ a vertex $x_e\in e\cap V(F)$; by (L1) (invariant under isomorphism) some minimum transversal $T_F$ contains $x_e$ (each $F$ is matched at most once); for unmatched $F$ take any $T_F$. The union meets every edge of $E^*(X^*)$, contradicting (B). Otherwise, by Hall's theorem, some nonempty $S\subseteq E^*(X^*)$ has $|N_B(S)|<|S|$. Put $X'=X^*\setminus N_B(S)$. If $X'=\emptyset$, then $|X^*|=|N_B(S)|<|S|\le|E^*(X^*)|$, contrary to the assumption. So $X'\ne\emptyset$. An edge of $E^*(X')$ meets $V(X')$, is inside no member of $X'$, and is inside no member of $N_B(S)$ (it meets $V(X')$, which is disjoint from them), so $E^*(X')\subseteq E^*(X^*)$; it is not in $S$ (edges of $S$ meet only members in $N_B(S)$). Hence $|E^*(X')|-|X'|\le|E^*(X^*)|-|S|-|X^*|+|N_B(S)|<|E^*(X^*)|-|X^*|$, contradicting the choice of $X^*$. So $\min_X(|E^*(X)|-|X|)\ge1$. $\square$
3.5 (F) For $X\ne\emptyset$: $\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\le\sum W-35(|X|+1)\le(35-35)|X|-35<0$ by (E) and (L2). The maximum is therefore attained by $X=\emptyset$: $\mathrm{DEF}(H)=0$, and $A(H)=P(H)<0$. $\square$

### Step 4. Proof of Theorem 4
4.1 (Bound for DEF after deleting a vertex.) Let $v$ have degree $d$ and $X'$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)$, $X'\ne\emptyset$. By 0.4, $X'$ is an $L$-family of $H$, and $E^*_H(X')=E^*_{H-v}(X')\sqcup K$, $K$ the edges at $v$ meeting $V(X')$ (an edge of $H$ meeting $V(X')$ inside no member either contains $v$ — then it is in $K$, and it is inside no member as $v\notin V(X')$ — or is an edge of $H-v$). So $\mathrm{DEF}_{H-v}(X')=\mathrm{DEF}_H(X')+35|K|\le\sum W-35(|X'|+1)+35|K|$ by (F), and with $W\le16$, $|K|\le d$, $|X'|\ge1$:
$$\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\le\max\big(0,\,-19|X'|-35+35d\big)\le\max(0,35d-54).\tag{4.1}$$
4.2 (a) $H$ has no isolated vertex, so $1\le d(v)\le3$. If $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)$ then $A(H)=A(H-v)+27+35d$. $H-v\in C_3$ is smaller, so $A(H-v)\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\ge-\max(0,35d-54)$ by (4.1), and $A(H)\ge27+35d-\max(0,35d-54)\ge62>0$, contradicting (F). So $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)-1$ (0.1). $\square$
4.3 (b) For $d(v)=3$, (a) gives $A(H-v)=A(H)-27-105+132=A(H)$. As $P(H-v)\ge0$: $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\ge-A(H)\ge1$ ($A$ is an integer, negative by (F)). So $X_v\ne\emptyset$ and
 (i) $\sum_{X_v}W-35e_v=\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\ge1$, hence $35e_v\le16|X_v|-1$;
 (ii) $E^*_H(X_v)=E^*_{H-v}(X_v)\sqcup K$ with $|K|=k_v\le3$ (4.1), so (E) gives $e_v+k_v\ge|X_v|+1$.
If $|X_v|=j\ge4$: (ii) gives $e_v\ge j-2$, and (i) gives $35(j-2)\le16j-1$, i.e. $19j\le69$: impossible. $j=1$: (i) gives $e_v=0$, (ii) $k_v\ge2$. $j=2$: $35e_v\le31$, $e_v=0$, $k_v\ge3$. $j=3$: $35e_v\le47$, $e_v\le1$, $k_v\ge4-e_v$, so $e_v=1$, $k_v=3$, and (i) gives $\sum W\ge36$. In (D1)/(D2), $e_v=0$ means that no edge of $H-v$ leaves $V(F)$ (resp. $V(F_1)\cup V(F_2)$) except the edges inside the members, and members are connected, so they are components of $H-v$. The lower bounds on $A(H)$ are $A(H)\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)=-(\sum W-35e_v)$; the largest value of $\sum W-35e_v$ is $16+16=32$ (D2 with two copies of $E_1$), so $A(H)\ge-32$. The statement about (B) is Theorem 3(B) for the $L$-family $X_v$ of $H$ (0.4). In (D1), $E^*_H(F)=K$, and a minimum transversal $T$ of $F$ meets an edge $e\in K$ iff it meets $e\cap V(F)$ (as $v\notin V(F)$). $\square$

### Step 5. Proof of Theorem 5
5.1 $H\setminus e\in C_3$ and $n+m$ drops by one, so $P(H\setminus e)\ge0$. If $\tau(H\setminus e)=\tau(H)$ then $A(H\setminus e)=A(H)-35$ and $\mathrm{DEF}(H\setminus e)\ge35-A(H)>35$. ### Step 6. Proof of Theorem 6 (maximum degree 2)
6.1 By 0.6 and $A(K_1)=27>0$ it suffices to treat a connected $H$ with $m\ge1$ and $\Delta\le2$. Let $G$ be its dual graph (L017 4.1): $V(G)=E(H)$, one $G$-edge per vertex of degree 2; $G$ is simple and connected, $\Delta(G)\le3$, $N:=|V(G)|=m$, $M:=|E(G)|=n_2(H)$. The edge $x$ of $H$ contains exactly $3-d_G(x)$ vertices of degree 1, so $n_1=3N-2M$, $n=3N-M$, and $\tau(H)=N-\nu(G)$ (L017 4.2; Henning–Yeo EJC 2017 Prop. 9). Hence
$$A(H)=27(3N-M)+35N-132(N-\nu(G))=132\,\nu(G)-16N-27M.$$
(Every such $H$ lies in $C_3$: linearity is $G$ simple; $F_7$ has a vertex of degree 3; for a set $U$ of $H$-edges, the vertex set spanned has $3|U|-|E(G[U])|\ge|U|+2$ vertices when $|U|\ge1$, because $|E(G[U])|\le\min(3|U|/2,\binom{|U|}2)\le2|U|-2$ for $|U|\ge1$ — used only for the computation 7(iv).)
6.2 (Tutte–Berge.) Choose $U\subseteq V(G)$ with $2\nu(G)=N+|U|-o(G-U)$; let $C_1,\dots,C_q$ be the components of $G-U$, $c_i=|V(C_i)|$, $e_i=|E(C_i)|$. Every $G$-edge lies in some $C_i$ or meets $U$, and at most $3|U|$ edges meet $U$, so $M\le3|U|+\sum e_i$; $N=|U|+\sum c_i$. Therefore
$$A(H)=66(N+|U|-o)-16N-27M\ \ge\ 35|U|+\sum_i\theta_i,\qquad\theta_i:=50c_i-27e_i-66[c_i\text{ odd}].$$
6.3 (Values of $\theta_i$.) $C_i$ is simple and subcubic, so $e_i\le\min(\binom{c_i}2,\lfloor3c_i/2\rfloor)$. $c_i=1$: $\theta_i=-16$. $c_i=2$: $\ge73$. $c_i=3$: $\ge150-81-66=3$. $c_i=4$: $\ge200-162=38$. $c_i=5$: $e_i\le7$; $e_i\le6$ gives $\theta_i\ge22$; $e_i=7$ forces degrees $(3,3,3,3,2)$, whose complement in $K_5$ is a path with two edges plus a disjoint edge, so $C_i$ is $K_4$ with one edge subdivided ($K_4^s$), and $\theta_i=-5$. Even $c_i\ge6$: $\ge50c_i-\frac{81}2c_i>0$. Odd $c_i\ge7$: $e_i\le(3c_i-1)/2$, $\theta_i\ge(19c_i-105)/2\ge14$. So $\theta_i\ge0$ except $\theta_i=-16$ for singleton components and $\theta_i=-5$ for $K_4^s$ components. Let $o_1,o_5$ be their numbers.
6.4 (A family.) Let $X$ consist of the edge $x$ of $H$ for each singleton component $\{x\}$ (an induced copy of $E_1$; $H$ is linear), and, for each $K_4^s$ component $B$ with subdivision vertex $u_0$, the induced subgraph of $H$ on the seven vertices given by the $G$-edges of $B$ and the third vertex $p$ of the $H$-edge $u_0$ (the $K_4$-vertices of $B$ have degree 3 inside $B$, so they have no other $G$-edges; $p$ is a degree-1 vertex of $H$ or the $G$-edge from $u_0$ to $U$). This subgraph contains exactly the five $H$-edges of $B$ (any other $H$-edge has at most one vertex, $p$, in the set), and it is isomorphic to $(8,5,3)\#0$ (its dual is $K_4^s$ with the degree-1 vertex at the subdivision vertex: for $(8,5,3)\#0$ the edges $012,156,247,346,357$ give the dual $a\!-\!b,a\!-\!c,b\!-\!d,b\!-\!e,c\!-\!d,c\!-\!e,d\!-\!e$, which is $K_4$ on $\{b,c,d,e\}$ with $bc$ subdivided by $a$, and $0$ is the degree-1 vertex of $a$). Members coming from different components of $G-U$ are non-adjacent in $G$, hence vertex-disjoint in $H$. An $H$-edge outside the members meeting $V(X)$ contains a $G$-edge from a member to its complement, whose other end is in $U$ (components of $G-U$ have no edges to each other), or contains $p$, which then lies on an $H$-edge of $U$. So $E^*_H(X)\subseteq U$ and
$$\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\ge16o_1+5o_5-35|U|.$$
6.5 Adding 6.2–6.4: $P(H)\ge35|U|-16o_1-5o_5+16o_1+5o_5-35|U|=0$. If $X=\emptyset$ the same holds with $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge0$ and no negative $\theta_i$. The last sentence of the theorem follows from Theorem 3(A) and 4.3: a minimal counterexample is connected, and if it had $\Delta\le2$ it would satisfy $P\ge0$. $\square$

### Step 8. Proof of Proposition 9
8.1 (H.1.) For every $e\in E^*(X)$ there are minimum transversals $T_F$ ($F\in X$) whose union meets every edge of $E^*(X)\setminus\{e\}$. *Proof.* Let $B$ be the bipartite graph of 3.4 and $B-e$; its sides have $|X|$ elements each. If $B-e$ has no perfect matching, Hall gives a nonempty $S\subseteq X$ with $|N_{B-e}(S)|<|S|$. As in 3.4, $E^*_H(S)=N_B(S)$ (an edge meeting $V(S)$ inside no member of $S$ is inside no member of $X$), so $|E^*_H(S)|\le|N_{B-e}(S)|+1\le|S|$, contradicting (E). With a perfect matching of $B-e$ and (L1) the claim follows as in 3.4. $\square$
8.2 (The replacement.) Let $R=H-V(X)$ and $H'=R+e'$ as in (H$_3$) or (H$_2$) (in (H$_1$) put $e'=\partial(X)\cup\{z_1,z_2\}$ with two new vertices). Let $T'$ be a minimum transversal of $H'$. New vertices lie on $e'$ only, so we may assume $T'$ contains a vertex $u\in e'\cap\partial(X)$; $u$ lies on some $e_u\in E^*(X)$. With 8.1 for $e_u$, $T'\setminus\{\text{new}\}\cup\bigcup T_F$ is a transversal of $H$ (edges of $R$ by $T'$, of members by $T_F$, of $E^*(X)$ by $u$ or the $T_F$). With $n(H)=n(H')-\nu+\sum n_F$ ($\nu$ new vertices), $m(H)=m(H')-1+\sum m_F+|X|+1$:
$$A(H)\ge A(H')+\sum A(F)+35|X|-27\nu=A(H')-\sum W+35|X|-27\nu\ge A(H')+19|X|-27\nu .$$
8.3 ($\mathrm{DEF}(H')\le16$.) Let $Y$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(H')>0$; then $35|E^*_{H'}(Y)|<\sum_YW\le16|Y|$, so $|E^*_{H'}(Y)|\le|Y|-1$. *Case 1: no member of $Y$ contains $e'$.* A member containing a new vertex would contain $e'$ (its only edge; members are connected with $\ge3$ vertices), so members lie in $R$ and are induced there ($e'\not\subseteq$ their vertex set); by 0.4 $Q:=X\cup Y$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$, and $E^*_H(Q)\subseteq E^*_H(X)\cup E^*_R(Y)$, $E^*_R(Y)\subseteq E^*_{H'}(Y)$; so $|E^*_H(Q)|\le|X|+1+|Y|-1=|Q|$, contradicting (E). *Case 2: $e'\in E(R')$ for a member $R'$ of $Y$, $|Y|\ge2$.* $Q:=X\cup(Y\setminus\{R'\})$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$; an edge of $R$ meeting $V(Y\setminus\{R'\})$ inside none of its members is inside no member of $Y$, so it is in $E^*_{H'}(Y)$; hence $|E^*_H(Q)|\le|X|+1+|Y|-1=|Q|+1$ with $|Q|>|X|$; by (E) $|E^*(Q)|-|Q|=1=|E^*(X)|-|X|$, contradicting the maximality of $|X|$. So $Y=\{R'\}$ and $\mathrm{DEF}(H')\le W(R')\le16$.
8.4 In each of (H$_3$), (H$_2$), (H$_1$), $H'\in C_3$ (for (H$_1$): $e'$ has two new vertices of degree 1, so it creates no $F_7$ (minimum degree 2) and no pair inside two edges, and $e_{H'}(S)\le e_R(S\setminus\{z_1,z_2\})+1\le|S|-3$ for $S\supseteq e'$; degrees: $u$ lost at least one edge of $E^*(X)$ and gained $e'$), and $n(H')+m(H')<n(H)+m(H)$, so $A(H')\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(H')\ge-16$. By 8.2, $P(H)=A(H)\ge19|X|-16-27\nu$, which is $\ge3$ for $\nu=0$, $\ge14$ for $\nu=1$, $|X|\ge3$, and $\ge6$ for $\nu=2$, $|X|\ge4$: contradiction with (F). If none of the exceptions of GAP G2 occurs, the optimal $X$ has $|E^*(X)|-|X|\ge2$, hence so has every nonempty family; then in 4.3 (ii) reads $e_v+k_v\ge|X_v|+2$, which with (i) leaves only $j=1$, $e_v=0$, $k_v=3$. $\square$

## Where the induction closes and where it does not

**Closes (proved above, for a minimal counterexample $H$ to KT$^{L^*}$):** connectivity, no isolated vertex (3.1); Claim B (3.2); $H\notin L^*$ (3.3); Hall: $|E^*(X)|\ge|X|+1$ for all $X$ (3.4); $\mathrm{DEF}(H)=0$ (3.5); every vertex co-transversal (4.2); the free hub and the three local configurations (D1)–(D3) at every degree-3 vertex, with the Claim-B (Hall-deficiency) condition and $-32\le A(H)\le-1$ (4.3); edge-criticality up to chords of pieces (Step 5); $\Delta(H)=3$ because KT holds for $\Delta\le2$ (Step 6); $n\ge25$ (7.3); the pure hub assemblies are excluded (7.6), which is the check of the Hall step against the seven Hall-deficient patterns of L017 Theorem 4: over pieces of $L^*$, only pattern (b) with $E_1\sqcup E_1$ ($=F_7$) and pattern (e) with $(8,5,3)\sqcup(8,5,3)$ ($=$ the six $(17,13,7)$ classes, which are themselves pieces) raise $\tau$; pattern (a) with one piece cannot occur at $n\ge25$; and a τ-raising degree-3 hub over pieces always has $A(H)=\sum A(F_i)<0$ (the "potential balance" of the spec is exactly zero for degree 3, $-35$ for degree 2 and $-70$ for degree 1 when $\tau$ rises, $+132$ more when it does not).

**Does not close (GAPs).**
- GAP: G1 (impure configurations). (D1) with $H-v=F\sqcup G'$, $G'\ne\emptyset$; (D2) with $H-v\supsetneq F_1\sqcup F_2$; and (D3). Here $v$ attaches a special component of $H-v$ to the rest of the graph; the free-hub accounting is exactly balanced ($A(H)\ge A(G')-W(F)$ for $Z=V(F)\cup\{v\}$, $T=T_F\cup\{v\}$), so a contradiction needs a reduction that gains $W(F)+\mathrm{DEF}(G')$, which none of the elementary reductions tried in experiments/prover/L021/README.md provides. These configurations are locally possible: e.g. $F=E_1=\{x,y,z\}$ with edges $\{v,x,a\},\{v,y,b\}$ satisfies Claim B, and the attachment data (attach\_output.txt) show many uncoverable 2- and 3-attachments for $E_1$, $(8,5,3)$, $(10,7,4)$, $(15,11,6)$ (single-vertex parts clustered on few edges).
- GAP: G2 (Claim H). Proposition 9 gives $|E^*(X)|\ge|X|+2$ except when, for the optimal $X$ with $|E^*(X)|=|X|+1$: $|\partial(X)|\ge3$ and every 3-set $e'\subseteq\partial(X)$ gives $(H-V(X))+e'\notin C_3$ (an edge of $H-V(X)$ containing two vertices of $e'$, an $F_7$ through $e'$, or a set $S\supseteq e'$ with $e_{H-V(X)}(S)=|S|-2$); or $|\partial(X)|=2$ with $|X|\le2$ or the replacement outside $C_3$; or $|\partial(X)|=1$ with $|X|\le3$; or $\partial(X)=\emptyset$ (then $H=H[V(X)]$, an assembly of at most finitely many pieces joined by $|X|+1$ edges). Henning–Yeo meet the same obstruction (their Claims H.4–H.5 treat a non-linear replacement separately); for $C$ the replacement may also violate $F_7$-freeness or (P3), and the minimality hypothesis only covers $C_3$.
- GAP: G3 (degree 2). Deleting a degree-2 vertex costs exactly $35$ in this regime ($A(H-v)=A(H)+35$), so Theorem 4 gives no structure at degree-2 vertices beyond co-transversality.
- GAP: G4 (finiteness of the special family). KT$^{L^*}$ is a statement with a fixed list; a minimal counterexample, if any, is a connected member of $C_3$ with $n\ge25$ and $A+\mathrm{DEF}^{\rm proper}<0$, i.e. an irreducible special piece outside $L^*$. The data (7.1–7.2) contain none up to 24 vertices; nothing is known beyond.
Consequently Conjecture KT* (hence $c_*\ge35/66$ by this route) is NOT proved. If G2 were closed, a minimal counterexample would be connected, $\Delta=3$, $n\ge25$, $\mathrm{DEF}=0$, $-16\le A\le-1$, and every degree-3 vertex $v$ would be the apex of a configuration (D1) with $k_v=3$: a special component $F_v$ of $H-v$ met by all three edges at $v$, no minimum transversal of $F_v$ meeting the three traces, and $H-v\ne F_v$. The natural next step is a global argument over these apexes (each $F_v$ is a component of $H-v$ with at most 17 vertices), which is not attempted here.
