## Statement

**Conventions** (L021). $C$ is the class of L007–L016 (linear, $F_7$-free, $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for $|S|\ge2$); $C_3=\{H\in C:\Delta(H)\le3\}$. $A(H)=27n+35m-132\tau$. $L^*$ is L021's list of 32 pieces ($E_1$ with $W=16$; two $(8,5,3)$ classes, $W=5$; four $(10,7,4)$ classes, $W=13$; nineteen $(15,11,6)$ classes, $W=2$; six $(17,13,7)$ classes, $W=10$; $W=-A$; names as in closure_results.json). An $L^*$-family $X$ of $H$, $V(X)$, $E^*_H(X)$, $\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)=\sum_{F\in X}W(F)-35|E^*_H(X)|$, $\mathrm{DEF}(H)=\max_X\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\ge0$, $P(H)=A(H)+\mathrm{DEF}(H)$ and KT$^{L^*}$ ($P\ge0$ on $C_3$) are as in L021 Definition 2. A **minimal counterexample** is an $H\in C_3$ with $P(H)<0$ such that $P(H')\ge0$ for every $H'\in C_3$ with $n(H')+m(H')<n(H)+m(H)$ (L021 Theorem 3). For a vertex $v$ of degree 3 we write $e_1,e_2,e_3$ for the edges at $v$; for $Y\subseteq V(H)\setminus\{v\}$ the *traces* are $Q_j=e_j\cap Y$; a co-vertex of $v$ outside $Y$ is *outer*. A **special component** of $H-v$ is a component of $H-v$ isomorphic to a member of $L^*$. A piece $F\in L^*$ is **rich** if every two vertices of $F$ lie in a common minimum transversal of $F$. A **b-configuration** of a 3-graph $G$ is a set of three distinct vertices of $G$-degree $\le2$, no two of which lie in a common minimum transversal of $G$. $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is $G$ with four new vertices $v,x,y,z$ and the edges $\{x,y,z\},\{v,x,a\},\{v,y,b\},\{v,z,c\}$.

Two hypotheses on a minimal counterexample $H$:
- **(E+)**: $|E^*_H(X)|\ge|X|+2$ for every nonempty $L^*$-family $X$ of $H$.
- **(AH)** (apex hypothesis; SYNTHESIS §3: "every degree-3 vertex $v$ is a (D1) apex with $k_v=3$"): for every vertex $v$ of degree 3, every $L^*$-family $X$ of $H-v$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_{H-v}(X)\ge1$ consists of a single member, which is a component of $H-v$ met by all three edges at $v$.

**Lemma 1 ((E+) gives (AH); the special component).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample. (a) (E+) implies (AH). (b) Under (AH), for every vertex $v$ of degree 3: $H-v$ has exactly one special component $F_v$; all three edges at $v$ meet $F_v$; the traces $Q_j=e_j\cap V(F_v)$ are nonempty and pairwise disjoint; no minimum transversal of $F_v$ meets $Q_1$, $Q_2$ and $Q_3$; $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)=W(F_v)$; and $-W(F_v)\le A(H)\le-1$, so $-16\le A(H)\le-1$.

**Lemma 2 (rich-member lemma).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample and $X$ a nonempty $L^*$-family of $H$ with $|E^*_H(X)|=|X|+1$. Then no member of $X$ is rich. By computation C2 (for the tight pieces also by hand, Step 0.6) the rich pieces are $(10,7,4)\#0$, $(10,7,4)\#3$, all six $(17,13,7)$ classes and $(15,11,6)\#4,\#5,\#14,\#15,\#16,\#17$; the non-rich ones are $E_1$, both $(8,5,3)$ classes, $(10,7,4)\#1,\#2$ (whose only non-co-transversal pairs are $\{0,1\},\{0,2\}$, where $\{0,1,2\}$ is the edge at the unique vertex $0$ of degree 1) and the other thirteen $(15,11,6)$ classes.

**Lemma A (minimal apex) [the judge's statement, SYNTHESIS §3, verbatim in content].** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ in which every degree-3 vertex $v$ is a (D1) apex with $k_v=3$ (precisely: (AH); by Lemma 1 this holds under (E+), which L021 Proposition 8 gives when the exceptions of GAP G2 do not occur). Choose $v$ and a special component $F_v$ of $H-v$ with $|V(F_v)|$ minimum (over all vertices of degree 3; $H$ has one, L021 Theorem 6; such a $v$, a *minimal apex*, need not be unique, and Lemma A, Lemma A+ hold for every choice).
(a) Every vertex $w$ of $F_v$ with $d_H(w)=3$ has $v\in V(F_w)$.
(b) If $n\ge67$, the minimal apex is an E1-hub: $F_v=E_1=\{x,y,z\}$, the edges at $v$ are $\{v,x,a\},\{v,y,b\},\{v,z,c\}$, $d_H(x)=d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$, and no minimum transversal of $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}$ contains two of $a,b,c$ ($\{a,b,c\}$ is a b-configuration of $G'$). Moreover $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G')$, $\tau(H)=\tau(G')+2$ and $A(G')=A(H)+16\in[0,15]$.

**Lemma A+ (the threshold 47) [new].** In the setting of Lemma A let $Z$ be the set of vertices of $F_v$ of $H$-degree 3. Exactly one of the following holds.
(α) $Z=\emptyset$; then $F_v=E_1$ and every conclusion of Lemma A(b) holds (for every $n$).
(β) Some $w\in Z$ is not a co-vertex of $v$; then $V(H)=\{v\}\cup V(F_v)\cup V(F_w)$ and $n\le31$.
(δ) Every vertex of $Z$ is a co-vertex of $v$, and $Z$ meets two different edges at $v$, say $w\in Z\cap e_j$, $w'\in Z\cap e_k$, $j\ne k$; then $V(H)=\{v\}\cup V(F_v)\cup V(F_w)\cup V(F_{w'})$ and $n\le46$.
(The fourth logical case, $\emptyset\ne Z$ contained in one edge at $v$, is impossible.) In particular the conclusion of Lemma A(b) holds for every $n\ge47$.

**Remark (sharpening of Lemma A+; [derived-here by the round-2 referee; computer-checked, unrefereed]).** An exhaustive local check over the 32 pieces and all admissible trace triples shows: case (γ) never occurs; (α) occurs only for $F_v=E_1$; (β) occurs exactly for pieces $F_v$ with a degree-3 vertex (so $\Delta(F_v)=3$ there); (δ) occurs only for $F_v=(8,5,3)\#0$, so in case (δ) $n\le37$ (not 46). This shrinks the W2 enumeration. Lemma A+'s statement above is unchanged (46 stays as the proved bound).

### Step 1. Proof of Lemma 1
1.1 (a) Assume (E+). Let $d(v)=3$ and let $X$ be an $L^*$-family of $H-v$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_{H-v}(X)\ge1$; put $j=|X|\ge1$ (the empty family has DEF 0), $e=|E^*_{H-v}(X)|$, $k$ = the number of edges at $v$ meeting $V(X)$, $k\le3$. Since $W\le16$: $35e\le\sum W-1\le16j-1$. By 0.1 and 0.3, $X$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$ with $|E^*_H(X)|=e+k$, so (E+) gives $e+k\ge j+2$, i.e. $e\ge j-1$. Then $35(j-1)\le16j-1$, so $19j\le34$ and $j=1$. Then $35e\le15$ gives $e=0$, and $e+k\ge3$ gives $k=3$. $e=0$ says that every edge of $H-v$ meeting $V(F)$ ($X=\{F\}$) lies inside $F$; $F$ is connected and induced, so $F$ is a component of $H-v$, met by all three edges at $v$. This is (AH).
1.2 (b) Assume (AH) and $d(v)=3$. *Existence:* $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)\ge1$ (0.3) is attained by a family, which by (AH) is a single special component met by all three edges. *Every special component is met by all three edges:* for a special component $F'$, the family $\{F'\}$ of $H-v$ has $E^*_{H-v}(\{F'\})=\emptyset$ (the edges of $H-v$ meeting a component lie in it), so $\mathrm{DEF}_{H-v}(\{F'\})=W(F')\ge2\ge1$ and (AH) applies. *Uniqueness:* two special components $F'\ne F''$ form a family of $H-v$ with $E^*=\emptyset$ and $\mathrm{DEF}=W(F')+W(F'')\ge1$ with two members, contrary to (AH). *Traces:* $Q_j\ne\emptyset$ as $e_j$ meets $F_v$; pairwise disjoint by 0.7. *Claim B:* the edges of $H$ meeting $V(F_v)$ are the edges of $F_v$ and $e_1,e_2,e_3$ (an edge of $H$ not containing $v$ is an edge of $H-v$, and if it meets the component $F_v$ it lies in it), so $E^*_H(\{F_v\})=\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}$; a minimum transversal of $F_v$ meets $e_j$ iff it meets $Q_j$ ($v\notin V(F_v)$); 0.2(B) gives the claim. *DEF:* every family with positive deficiency is $\{F_v\}$, so $\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)=W(F_v)$. *Bounds:* $P(H-v)\ge0$ by minimality, and $A(H-v)=A(H)$ (0.3), so $A(H)\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(H-v)=-W(F_v)\ge-16$; $A(H)\le-1$ by 0.2(F). $\square$

### Step 2. Proof of Lemma 2 (rich-member lemma)
