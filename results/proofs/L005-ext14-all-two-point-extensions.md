## Statement
Let $A$ be a 14-point (4,5)-set with $h(A)=8$ and $m(A)=12$ (every non-extreme point of $A$ is the midpoint of a 3-term AP in $A$; by L004 these are the 2,910 affine classes of the census). For every pair of distinct reals $x,y\notin A$ such that $B=A\cup\{x,y\}$ is a (4,5)-set, $h(B)=10$.
Consequently: no 16-point (4,5)-set with $h\le 9$ (a set beating $4/7$ at $n=16$, see START_HERE section 4) contains a saturated extremal 14-point subset; equivalently, in any 16-point record set every 14-point subset with $h=8$ has $m=11$ (the open case Q11; $m\ge11$ is Henning–Yeo, K8, as in L004). The statement says nothing about extensions by four or more points, and nothing about non-saturated 14-point sets with $h=8$.

## Proof
Every property involved (being a (4,5)-set, $h$, the AP hypergraph, $m$) is invariant under affine maps $x\mapsto qx+t$, and $A\mapsto A\cup\{x,y\}$ commutes with them, so it suffices to treat one integer representative of each of the 2,910 classes of L004. Fix a class $A$ (scaled by 4 so that all midpoints and endpoints of pairs of $A\cup\{x\}$ below are integers; the T2 line intersection points are handled with exact Fractions). $h(B)\le h(A)+2=10$ trivially, since a Sidon subset of $B$ meets $A$ in a Sidon subset of $A$. The content is $h(B)\ge 10$, shown by exhibiting a 3-AP-free 10-subset (or computing $\alpha$ exactly) in every case of the L001 analysis; by K4 (Sidon subsets of a (4,5)-set are exactly its 3-AP-free subsets, and $B$ is a (4,5)-set by hypothesis) such a subset is Sidon:

Let $P_x$ be the finite set of reals not in $A$ that form a 3-AP with two points of $A$ (midpoints $(a+b)/2$ and endpoints $2b-a$, $a\ne b\in A$). Any 3-AP of $B$ that is not a 3-AP of $A$ contains $x$ or $y$; if it contains exactly one of them, that point lies in $P_x$.
- Case T0 (neither $x$ nor $y$ in any new AP): $S\cup\{x,y\}$ is 3-AP-free for a maximum Sidon subset $S$ of $A$, so $h(B)=10$.
- Case T1 ($x\in P_x$ up to the symmetry $x\leftrightarrow y$; $A\cup\{x\}$ must be a (4,5)-set, 32 to 61 admissible $x$ per class, 141,153 in total). T1a ($y$ in no new AP): $h(B)=h(A\cup\{x\})+1$; computed exactly for all 141,153 single extensions: always 10 (i.e. $h(A\cup\{x\})=9$ for every admissible $x$). T1b ($y$ in a new AP): then $y$ forms an AP with two points of $A\cup\{x\}$, so $y$ ranges over the finite set of midpoints/endpoints of pairs of $A\cup\{x\}$; every candidate pair is tested exactly for the (4,5) property (every 4-subset containing $x$ or $y$); for each of the 4,970,208 (4,5) pairs a 3-AP-free 10-subset was found (checked to be Sidon), so $h(B)=10$.
- Case T2 ($x,y\notin P_x$ but some new AP exists; it must contain both $x$ and $y$, so it is $\{a,x,y\}$ with $a\in A$ in one of 3 roles): $(x,y)$ lies on one of $3\cdot14=42$ lines. At a generic point of such a line the AP hypergraph of $B$ is $H(A)$ plus the single edge $\{a,x,y\}$; its independence number was computed exactly for all $2{,}910\times42=122{,}220$ line hypergraphs: always 10. Non-generic points of a line either put $x$ or $y$ into $P_x$ (Case T1) or lie on a second line (a second edge containing both $x$ and $y$): the pairwise intersection points of the 42 lines (480 to 498 per class after removing $x=y$ and points with $x$ or $y$ in $A$; 1,444,980 in total; 498 for $A_{\rm base}$) were tested exactly (Fractions; these points have denominators dividing 3), and none of them gives a (4,5)-set in any class, as linearity (K6) forces: two APs $\{a,x,y\}$, $\{a',x,y\}$ with $a\ne a'$ would share the pair $\{x,y\}$.
These cases are exhaustive, so $h(B)=10$ for every (4,5) two-point extension of every class. $\square$

Remark (theory agrees; not load-bearing). Lemma U1 (kb/notes/extension_lemmas.md, falsifier-checked) shows by Henning–Yeo applied to $H(A)-a$ (linear and $F_7$-free by K6, K7) that no vertex of a $(14,12,\tau=6)$ hypergraph lies in every maximum independent set, which is exactly the condition for a T2 line to give $\alpha=9$; so U1 predicts that no T2 line is deficient (every T2 line hypergraph has $\alpha=10$), and the sweep confirms it: no class has such a vertex (the number of maximum Sidon subsets per class ranges from 137 to 150; for $A_{\rm base}$ it is 143 as in L001).
