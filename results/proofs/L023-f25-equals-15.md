### Step 3. Proof of Theorem 3
3.1 (Reduction to midpoint labellings.) Let $X$ be one of the $X_i$, $V=\{0,\dots,24\}$, $E$ its 23 edges, and suppose $A$, $\varphi$ are as in the statement; write $x_v=\varphi(v)$.
 (i) $\varphi(E)$ consists of 23 distinct 3-term APs of $A$, and $A$ has at most $|A|-2=23$ of them (K5, (P3) with $S=A$). So the APs of $A$ are exactly the images of the edges of $X$.
 (ii) For $e\in E$ let $\lambda(e)\in e$ be the vertex mapped to the midpoint of the AP $\varphi(e)$. Since the midpoint map of $A$ is injective (K5), $\lambda:E\to V$ is injective; since it never takes the value $\min A$ or $\max A$ (K5) and $|V\setminus\lambda(E)|=25-23=2$, the two vertices $u<w$ outside $\lambda(E)$ are mapped to $\min A$ and $\max A$.
 (iii) Replace $x$ by $(x-x_u)/(x_w-x_u)$ ($x_w\ne x_u$). This affine map preserves 3-term APs and their midpoints, injectivity, the weak Sidon property and the (4,5) property. Now $x_u=0$, $x_w=1$, and for every edge $e=\{a,\lambda(e),c\}$: $2x_{\lambda(e)}-x_a-x_c=0$. So $y=(x_v)_{v\ne u,w}$ solves the square system $L_\lambda$: one equation per edge, 23 unknowns (the vertices of $\lambda(E)=V\setminus\{u,w\}$), with $x_u=0$, $x_w=1$ substituted.
 (iv) $L_\lambda$ is non-singular. Suppose $z\ne0$, $z_u=z_w=0$, $2z_{\lambda(e)}=z_a+z_c$ for every edge. Replacing $z$ by $-z$ if necessary, $M=\max_vz_v>0$; let $S=\{v:z_v=M\}\subseteq\lambda(E)$. For $v\in S$ the unique edge $e=\{a,v,c\}$ with $\lambda(e)=v$ has $z_a+z_c=2M$, $z_a,z_c\le M$, so $a,c\in S$. Take $v\in S$ with $x_v$ maximal: $x_v=(x_a+x_c)/2$ with $x_a\ne x_c$, so $\max(x_a,x_c)>x_v$ with $a,c\in S$, a contradiction. Hence $y$ is **the** solution of $L_\lambda$.
