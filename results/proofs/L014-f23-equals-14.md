## Statement

**Theorem 1.** There is no $H\in C$ with $(n,m,\tau)=(23,21,10)$ and $\Delta(H)\le3$.

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L007–L012 Step 0.1.) $n,m,\tau,s$ are additive over components; a single vertex has $s=3$; $C$ is hereditary (every subhypergraph, in particular $H-v$ and every component, lies in $C$); for $H\in C$: $\tau\le m$, $\tau(H)-1\le\tau(H-v)\le\tau(H)$, $m\le n-2$ for $n\ge2$ ((P3) with $S=V$), $17\tau\le5n+3m$ (K8); a connected 3-graph with $m\ge1$ edges has $n\le2m+1$; deleting a vertex does not increase degrees; a component of a 3-graph is an isolated vertex or has at least 3 vertices.

0.2 (L011 Theorem 1.) Every $H\in C$ with at most 22 vertices has $s(H)\ge0$.

0.3 (L012 0.6.) If $H\in C$ has $m=n-2\ge1$ edges, then $H$ is connected and has no isolated vertex.

0.4 (L012 0.4, 0.5.) If $X\in C$ has $\Delta(X)\le\Delta$ and $w$ has degree $d$, the $d$ edges at $w$ are $\{w,a_i,b_i\}$ with pairwise disjoint pairs, no pair inside an edge of $X-w$, and every $a_i,b_i$ of $(X-w)$-degree $\le\Delta-1$ (an isolated vertex of $X-w$ is an admissible endpoint of degree 0). If $G\cong G_0$, every such extension of $G$ is isomorphic to such an extension of $G_0$; $\tau$, connectivity, degrees and membership in $C$ are isomorphism invariants.

0.5 (The engine, L012 Step 3.) For a key $(n,m,t,D)$, `engine.cell(n,m,t,D)` writes a list `cell(n,m,t,D)` (file `out/cells/cell_n_m_t_D<D>.txt`). If $(n,m)=(1,0)$ the list is the isolated vertex; if $m=0$, $t<1$, $t>m$, $n>2m+1$, $m>n-2$, $17t>5n+3m$ or $D<1$ it is empty (then $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ is empty by 0.1). Otherwise, with MD $=10$: if $m\le10$ the *direct route* (`enum_conn n m t D`, then `filt FOUND n tau t`, then one graph per canonical form); if $m\ge11$ the *recursion*: for every $d$ with $\lceil3m/n\rceil\le d\le D$ and $t'\in\{t-1,t\}$ with $S'=3(n-1)+2(m-d)-11t'\ge0$, every decomposition of $(n-1,m-d)$ with total slack $S'$ (`engine.decompositions`), every multiset of representatives of the component lists `cell(n_i,m_i,\tau_i,d)`, placed on consecutive vertex blocks, is an input $G$; `ext_d (n-1) t' t d (d-1)` checks $\tau(G)=t'$ exactly and lists every extension of $G$ by a new vertex of degree $d$ as in 0.4 with $\tau\ge t$ that is $F_7$-free and satisfies (P3) (a branch is cut as soon as a new edge lies in a copy of $F_7$); `filt` re-checks (P1)–(P3), keeps the extensions connected on all $n$ vertices and, for $t'=t$, those with $\tau=t$ exactly; one graph per canonical form is kept.

**Lemma E″ (correctness for $n\le23$).** For every key $(n,m,t,D)$ with $n\le23$, every graph of `cell(n,m,t,D)` lies in $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$, and every member of $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ is isomorphic to a graph of the list.
*Proof.* This is L012 3.2–3.4, by induction on $m$ for all $n\le23$ at once (the same argument, written out for general $n$, is L013 0.5, Lemma E′; it is repeated here so that nothing of L013 is used). The only place where I11 enters is L012 3.2(ii): each component $K$ of $X-w$ is a connected member of $C$ with at most $n-1\le22$ vertices, hence $s(K)\ge0$ by 0.2. (i) *Soundness*: every saved graph passed `filt` (linear, $F_7$-free, (P3), connected on $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, $\tau=t$ exactly on the direct route and in the branches with $t'=t$; for $t'=t-1$, `ext_d` gives $\tau\ge t$, and $\tau\le\tau(G)+1=t$), and $\Delta\le d\le D$ (components from lists with bound $d$, new vertex of degree $d$, endpoints of $G$-degree $\le d-1$). (ii) *Completeness*, $m\le10$: L007 Lemma D, and `filt` keeps exactly the members of $C$ with $\tau=t$. (iii) *Completeness*, $m\ge11$: let $X\in\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ and $w$ a vertex of degree $d=\Delta(X)$, so $\lceil3m/n\rceil\le d\le D$. $X-w\in C$ has $n-1$ vertices, $m-d$ edges, $\Delta\le d$ and $\tau(X-w)=t'\in\{t-1,t\}$ (0.1). Its components $K$ have types $(n_K,m_K,\tau_K)$ that are connected types ($n_K\ge3$, $\lceil(n_K-1)/2\rceil\le m_K\le n_K-2$, $1\le\tau_K\le m_K$, $17\tau_K\le5n_K+3m_K$, slack $s(K)\ge0$) or isolated vertices (slack 3), with slacks adding to $S'$; hence $S'\ge0$, every slack lies in $[0,S']$, and the types form one of the decompositions that `engine.decompositions` lists (checked against an independent brute force in every branch, C1). Each $K$ lies in $\mathcal C(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)$ with $m_K<m$ and $n_K\le n-1$, so by induction it is isomorphic to a graph of `cell(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)`; hence $X-w$ is isomorphic to an input $G$, and by 0.4 $X$ is isomorphic to an extension of $G$ by a vertex of degree $d$ with $\tau=t$, connected and in $C$, which `ext_d` lists (L012 3.5: every hit is produced; a branch is cut only when a new edge lies in an $F_7$, which every superset then contains) and `filt` keeps. (iv) *Reduction*: `canon` prints a relabelling of its input (nauty's canonical labelling of the incidence graph, the two colour classes kept), so keeping one printed graph per distinct output keeps an isomorphic copy of every graph that reaches it. $\square$

0.6 (Program limits.) `ext_d` stops with exit code 1 if $G$ has more than 31 vertices or $d>8$, with 3 if an input has more than 262,144 minimum covers and with 4 if it has more than 256 admissible pairs; its edge array holds 40 edges (here at most 21). `filt` stops (exit 3) at more than 40 edges and works with up to 32 vertices (here at most 23). `canon` stops (exit 3) if $n+m>64$ (here at most 44). `enum_conn` stops if $N>31$ or $M>31$.
