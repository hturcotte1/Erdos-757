## Statement

$C$ is the class of L007–L010: 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set with $|S|\ge2$; $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$ (the I11 slack).

**Theorem 1 (I11 up to 22).** Every $H\in C$ with $n\le22$ satisfies $11\,\tau(H)\le3n+2m$.

**Theorem 2.** Every 22-point (4,5)-set has $h\ge13$, and $C_{22}=B_{12}\cup(5000+15\cdot B_{10})$, with $B_{12}=\{0,269,385,467,501,511,521,523,535,538,549,575\}$ and $B_{10}=\{0,50,73,80,87,96,100,112,127,174\}$ (data/small_records.json), i.e.
$C_{22}=\{0,269,385,467,501,511,521,523,535,538,549,575,5000,5750,6095,6200,6305,6440,6500,6680,6905,7610\}$,
is a 22-point (4,5)-set with $h=13$. Hence $f(22)=13$ (Henning–Yeo alone gives $f(22)\ge12$), and no 22-point (4,5)-set beats $4/7$ (that needs $h\le\lceil88/7\rceil-1=12$, i.e. $(n,m,\tau)=(22,20,10)$; kb/00_START_HERE.md §4).

**Corollary 3 (L008–L011 together).** No (4,5)-set with at most 22 points beats $4/7$: for $n\le15$ and $n\in\{17,19,21\}$ this follows from K8 alone (no $(m,\tau)$ with $m\le n-2$, $n-\lceil4n/7\rceil+1\le\tau\le m$ and $17\tau\le5n+3m$, `decomp22.py` part (d) for $3\le n\le22$; for $n\le2$, $h=n$), and $f(16)=10$, $f(18)=11$, $f(20)=12$, $f(22)=13$ (L008, L009, L010, Theorem 2) equal $\lceil4n/7\rceil$. So **every (4,5)-set $A$ with $h(A)<\tfrac47|A|$ has at least 23 points.**

**Remark 4 ($n=21$).** Theorem 1 at $n=21$ gives $\tau\le\lfloor(63+2m)/11\rfloor\le\lfloor101/11\rfloor=9$ ($m\le19$), i.e. $h\ge12$, which is no new information: $f(21)\ge f(20)=12$ already, and K8 alone gives $\tau\le\lfloor162/17\rfloor=9$. What Theorem 1 adds at $n=21$ is that $\tau=9$ forces $m\ge18$ ($99\le63+2m$); the cells (21,18,9) (slack $63+36-99=0$) and (21,19,9) (slack 2) satisfy I11 and K8 ($153\le159$, $153\le162$) and are not excluded. Since $f$ is non-decreasing (delete a point), $12=f(20)\le f(21)\le f(22)=13$: **$f(21)\in\{12,13\}$ stays open**; $f(21)=12$ iff some 21-point (4,5)-set has $\tau=9$ and $m\in\{18,19\}$.

**Remark 5 (what I11 cannot exclude).** The method refutes violations of I11 only. A cell $(n,m,\tau)$ that beats $4/7$ ($h=n-\tau\le\lceil4n/7\rceil-1$), satisfies $m\le n-2$, $\tau\le m$, K8, and has I11 slack $\ge0$ is not excluded even by I11 for all $n$. For $n\le40$ these are exactly (`decomp22.py` part (d)): (23,21,10) $s=1$; (25,23,11) $s=0$; (30,27,13) $s=1$; (30,28,13) $s=3$; (32,29,14) $s=0$; (32,30,14) $s=2$; (34,32,15) $s=1$; (36,34,16) $s=0$; (37,33,16) $s=1$; (37,34,16) $s=3$; (37,35,16) $s=5$; (39,35,17) $s=0$; (39,36,17) $s=2$; (39,37,17) $s=4$; there are none for $n\le22$ and none for $n\in\{24,26,27,28,29,31,33,35,38,40\}$ (at $n=27,29,31,33,35,38,40$ K8 allows record cells, but all have negative I11 slack). The first, (23,21,10), is saturated ($m=n-2$) with $h=13<\tfrac47\cdot23$; excluding it needs an inequality strictly stronger than I11.

### Step 0. Facts used

0.4 (Degree lemma, L010 0.4.) If I11 holds on $C$ below $N$ vertices and $H\in C$ has $N$ vertices, $m$ edges, $\tau(H)=\tau$ and $s(H)<0$, then for every vertex $v$: $\tau(H-v)=\tau-1$ and $d(v)\le\lfloor(s+8)/2\rfloor$. (Each application in Step 2 writes the inequality out.)
0.5 (Enumeration and transfer, L008 Step 5, L009 0.5.) `enum_conn N M T0 D` outputs a labelled copy of every connected linear 3-graph with $N$ vertices, $M$ edges, $\tau\ge T_0$ and $\Delta\le D$, and nothing else (L007 Lemma D). If $G\cong G_0$, every extension of $G$ by a new vertex with three edges whose pairs are pairwise disjoint, not inside an edge of $G$, with endpoints of $G$-degree $\le2$, is isomorphic to such an extension of $G_0$, which `ext_indep5` (and `ext_vertex2`) generate; $\tau$, connectivity, the maximum degree and membership in $C$ are isomorphism invariants.
0.6 (Edges at a degree-3 vertex, L009 5.4.) If $X\in C$ has $\Delta(X)\le3$ and $d(w)=3$, the edges at $w$ are $\{w,c_j,d_j\}$ ($j=1,2,3$) with pairwise disjoint pairs (by (P1) two edges at $w$ meet only in $w$), no pair inside an edge of $X-w$ (that edge would meet $\{w,c_j,d_j\}$ in two vertices), and each $c_j,d_j$ of degree $\le2$ in $X-w$ (it lies in exactly one edge at $w$). A vertex of $X-w$ in no edge of $X-w$ (an isolated vertex) is allowed as an endpoint, with degree 0.
