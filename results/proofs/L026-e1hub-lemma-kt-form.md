## Statement

**Conventions.** As in L021 and L017. $C$ is the class of linear $F_7$-free 3-graphs with $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ (property (P3)); a set $S$ with $|S|\ge3$ and $e(S)=|S|-2$ is *saturated*. $C_3=\{G\in C:\Delta(G)\le3\}$; $C$ and $C_3$ are closed under deleting vertices and edges. $G-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(G)\setminus Z$; $\partial_G(Z)$ is the set of edges of $G$ meeting $Z$, $q_G(u,w)=|\partial_G(\{u,w\})|$; $\sigma_G(u,w)=1$ if some edge contains $u$ and $w$ ($u,w$ *adjacent*), else 0. A vertex set $S$ is *co-transversal* in $G$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains $S$; a pair that is not co-transversal is an *NC pair*; a vertex $u$ is *avoided* if it lies in no minimum transversal. $s(G)=3n+2m-11\tau$ (I11 slack). $A(G)$, $\mathrm{DEF}(G)$, $P(G)=A(G)+\mathrm{DEF}(G)$ are as in L021 Definition 2 with $L=L^*$ ($P(\emptyset)=0$, $P(K_1)=27$). An *optimal family* of $G$ is an $L^*$-family $X$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)=\mathrm{DEF}(G)$. $E_1$ is the single edge. $Y_9$ is the 3-graph with edges $\{1,2,3\},\{4,5,6\},\{7,8,9\},\{1,4,7\}$ ($A(Y_9)=-13$). For odd $p\ge3$, a *$p$-piece tree* is a connected 3-graph $G$ whose vertex set is the disjoint union of $p$ sets inducing members of $L^*$ such that every other edge of $G$ meets three of these sets.

A *b-triple* of $G$ is a set of three distinct vertices that is not an edge of $G$ and whose three pairs are NC pairs of $G$; a *b-configuration* is a b-triple whose vertices have $G$-degree $\le2$.

**Hub, fold, merge.** For distinct vertices $a,b,c$ of $G$: $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,x,y,z$ and the four *hub edges* $e_0=\{x,y,z\}$, $e_a=\{v,x,a\}$, $e_b=\{v,y,b\}$, $e_c=\{v,z,c\}$; $\{a,b,c\}$ is the *outer triple*. The *fold* $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,z$ and the edges $\{v,a,b\}$, $\{v,z,c\}$. For non-adjacent $u\ne w$, the *merge* $G/(u{=}w)$ replaces $u$ and $w$ by one new vertex $\zeta$ in every edge. An *E1-hub* of a 3-graph $H$ is $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ with $e_0,e_a,e_b,e_c\in E(H)$ and $d_H(x)=d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$; its *outer graph* is $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}$; if $\Delta(H)\le3$ then $H=G'\oplus(a,b,c)$.

**The KT form of the E1-hub lemma** (SYNTHESIS.md Section 3): *no minimal counterexample $H$ to KT$^{L^*}$ has an E1-hub whose outer triple is a b-configuration of $G'$.* **The I11 form**: *if $G\in C$ is connected, $\Delta(G)\le3$, $s(G)\le1$, $n(G)\ge27$, I11 holds for all members of $C$ with fewer than $n(G)+4$ vertices, $a,b,c$ have $G$-degree $\le2$, $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$ and $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C$, then $\{a,b,c\}$ is not a b-configuration of $G$.*

**Theorem 1 (hub arithmetic; no minimality).** Let $G\in C_3$ and let $a,b,c$ be distinct vertices of $G$-degree $\le2$ with $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$.
 (a) $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C_3$.
 (b) $\tau(G\oplus(a,b,c))=\tau(G)+1$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of $a,b,c$, and $\tau(G)+2$ otherwise. Hence $A$ changes by $+116$ or $-16$, and $s$ by $+9$ or $-2$.
 (c) $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)\in C_3$ iff no edge of $G$ contains both $a$ and $b$. Its transversal number is $\tau(G)$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains $c$ and one of $a,b$, and $\tau(G)+1$ otherwise; if $\{a,b,c\}$ is a b-configuration, $A(G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c))=A(G)-8=A(G\oplus(a,b,c))+8$ and $s(G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c))=s(G)-1$.
 Parts (a)–(c) hold verbatim with $C$ in place of $C_3$ and without the degree hypothesis (the degree bound is used only for "$\Delta\le3$").

**Lemma 2 (families in induced subgraphs; no minimality).** If $H_0$ is an induced subgraph of $H$ and $X$ an $L^*$-family of $H_0$, then $X$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$, $E^*_H(X)=E^*_{H_0}(X)\sqcup\{f\in E(H)\setminus E(H_0):f\cap V(X)\ne\emptyset\}$, and $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_{H_0}(X)-35\,|\{f\in E(H)\setminus E(H_0):f\cap V(X)\ne\emptyset\}|$.

**Lemma 3 (deficiency of a connected graph; no minimality).** If $G$ is connected then $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le16$; $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\ge11$ only if $G\cong E_1$, a $(10,7,4)$ member of $L^*$, or $Y_9$; so $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le13$ unless $G\cong E_1$, and $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le10$ if $n(G)\ge11$.

**Lemma 4 (avoided vertices; no minimality).** If $u$ is avoided in $G$, then $P(G)\ge P(G-u)+27$.

**Lemma 5 (bridge lemma).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$, $e\in E(H)$, and $V(H)=V_1\sqcup V_2$ ($V_1,V_2\ne\emptyset$) such that every edge other than $e$ lies inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$, and $e$ meets both. With $H_i=H[V_i]$, $Q_i=e\cap V_i$: $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$, equivalently $Q_1$ is avoided in $H_1$ and $Q_2$ in $H_2$.

**Lemma 6 (pendant-block lemma).** In the setting of Lemma 5, if $|Q_2|=1$ then $V_2=Q_2$.

**Corollary 7 (bridges are pendant).** If $H$ is a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ and $H\setminus e$ is disconnected, then $H\setminus e$ has exactly two components, one of them a single vertex $u$ with $d_H(u)=1$, and $e\setminus\{u\}$ is avoided in $H-u$.

**Theorem 8 (KT form: reduction to Case 1).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ with $n(H)\ge16$ and an E1-hub $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ whose outer triple is a b-configuration of $G'$. Then:
 (a) $G'$ is connected.
 (b) Each of $a,b,c$ lies in a minimum transversal of $G'$; for each pair, say $\{a,b\}$: $\tau(G'-a-b)=\tau(G')-1$ and $A(G'-a-b)=A(H)+94-35\,q_{G'}(a,b)$, $q_{G'}(a,b)\le4$.
 (c) $A(G')=A(H)+16$, $-10\le A(G')\le15$, $-26\le A(H)\le-1$; if some pair of $a,b,c$ is non-adjacent in $G'$, then $A(H)\ge-18$.
 (d) For each pair, say $\{a,b\}$, every component $K$ of $G'-a-b$ with $c\notin V(K)$ meets at least two edges of $\partial_{G'}(\{a,b\})$ or is a single vertex of $H$-degree 1; $\sum_KA(K)=A(H)+94-35q_{G'}(a,b)$.
 (e) If $q_{G'}(a,b)=4$, then $G'-a-b$ has exactly four components, none a single vertex, one containing $c$, and at least two of them isomorphic to $E_1$, a $(10,7,4)$ piece or $Y_9$.

**Lemma 9 (positive deficiency; no minimality).** Let $G$ be a connected 3-graph and $X$ an $L^*$-family of $G$ with $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)>0$. Then $V(X)=V(G)$, $p:=|X|$ is odd and $p\le11$, $|E^*_G(X)|=(p-1)/2$, every edge of $E^*_G(X)$ meets three distinct members of $X$, and $\sum_{F\in X}W(F)\ge35(p-1)/2+1$. Hence if $\mathrm{DEF}(G)>0$, then $G$ is isomorphic to a member of $L^*$ ($p=1$) or is a $p$-piece tree with $p\in\{3,5,7,9,11\}$, and $n(G)\le17p\le187$.

**Lemma 10 (boundary count).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$, $U\subseteq V(H)$, and $K_1,\dots,K_r$ ($r\ge1$) distinct components of $H[U]$ with $\mathrm{DEF}(K_i)>0$; let $p_i$ be the number of members of an optimal family of $K_i$, and $\partial$ the set of edges of $H$ that meet $V(K_1)\cup\dots\cup V(K_r)$ and lie inside none of the sets $V(K_i)$. Then $|\partial|\ge\sum_i(p_i+1)/2+1\ge r+1$. In particular, for one such component $K$ at least $(p+3)/2\ge2$ edges of $H$ meet $V(K)$ without lying inside $V(K)$.

**Lemma 11 (merge lemma; no minimality).** Let $G$ be a linear 3-graph and $u\ne w$ non-adjacent vertices.
 (a) $n(G/(u{=}w))=n(G)-1$, $m(G/(u{=}w))=m(G)$, $d(\zeta)=d_G(u)+d_G(w)$, other degrees unchanged.
 (b) $\tau(G)-1\le\tau(G/(u{=}w))\le\tau(G)$, with $\tau(G/(u{=}w))=\tau(G)$ iff $\{u,w\}$ is an NC pair of $G$.
 (c) If $G\in C$: $G/(u{=}w)$ is linear iff $u,w$ have no common neighbour; every copy of $F_7$ in $G/(u{=}w)$ contains $\zeta$; $G/(u{=}w)$ satisfies (P3) iff no saturated set of $G$ contains $u$ and $w$.
 (d) If $G\in C$, $\{u,w\}$ is an NC pair and $G/(u{=}w)\in C$, then $s(G/(u{=}w))=s(G)-3$ and $A(G/(u{=}w))=A(G)-27$.
**Corollary 11.1.** If $G\in C$, $s(G)\le2$, and every member of $C$ with $n(G)-1$ vertices satisfies I11, then every NC pair $\{u,w\}$ of $G$ is *blocked*: $u,w$ are adjacent, or have a common neighbour, or lie in a common saturated set, or $G/(u{=}w)$ contains a copy of $F_7$.

**Theorem 12 (KT form: Case 1*).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ with $n(H)\ge22$ and an E1-hub $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ whose outer triple is a b-configuration of $G'$. Then, in addition to Theorem 8:
 (a) $\mathrm{DEF}(G')=0$, so $A(G')=P(G')\ge0$.
 (b) Not all three pairs of $\{a,b,c\}$ are adjacent in $G'$.
 (c) For every pair, say $\{a,b\}$, that is non-adjacent in $G'$, the fold $H^\circ=G'\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)$ has $\mathrm{DEF}(H^\circ)=0$ and $P(H^\circ)=A(H)+8\ge0$. Consequently $-8\le A(H)\le-1$ and $8\le A(G')\le15$.
 (d) Every pair, say $\{a,b\}$, that is non-adjacent in $G'$ and has $d_{G'}(a)+d_{G'}(b)\le3$ is blocked in $G'$ in the sense of Corollary 11.1.
 (e) Let $a$ be an outer vertex with $d_{G'}(a)=2$ and $g_1,g_2$ its edges in $G'$. Then the component $C_{\rm hub}$ of $H-a$ containing $v$ has $\mathrm{DEF}(C_{\rm hub})=0$, and $H-a$ has exactly one component $K$ with $\mathrm{DEF}(K)>0$: $K$ is a component of $G'-a$ with $b,c\notin V(K)$, it is isomorphic to a member of $L^*$, both $g_1$ and $g_2$ meet $V(K)$, and no minimum transversal of $K$ meets both $g_1\cap V(K)$ and $g_2\cap V(K)$. In particular $a$ is a cut vertex of $G'$. Moreover some $g_i$ has a vertex outside $V(K)\cup\{a\}$, and if $g_i=\{a,k_i,r_i\}$ with $k_i\in V(K)$, $r_i\notin V(K)$ for both $i=1,2$, then $(H-V(K))+\{a,r_1,r_2\}\notin C_3$: $r_1,r_2$ are adjacent in $H-V(K)$, or the new edge lies in a copy of $F_7$, or some saturated set of $H-V(K)$ contains $a,r_1,r_2$. (This uses the computation C10: no member of $L^*$ contains a hub triangle, defined in 12.5; (f), (i), (j) use (e) and so depend on C10 too.)
 (f) (Corner pattern.) If $d_{G'}(a)=2$, $d_{G'}(b)=d_{G'}(c)=1$ and each of $b,c$ shares an edge with $a$, then $R:=G'-\{a,b,c\}$ is isomorphic to a $(17,13,7)$ member of $L^*$, $n(H)=24$ and $H$ is a connected tight member of $C_3$ with $(n,m,\tau)=(24,19,10)$. (Such $H$ do not exist by the unrefereed computations L021 Theorem 7(ii) and experiments/20261027-0300-tight-cells-d4, cell$(24,19,10,3)=\emptyset$.)
 (g) If $q_{G'}(a,b)=4$ for some pair, then of the four components of $G'-a-b$ (Theorem 8(e)) either all four are isomorphic to members of $L^*$, with total weight $46-A(H)\in[47,54]$ (so $n(G')\le70$), or exactly three are copies of $E_1$ and the fourth, $K_4$, has $\mathrm{DEF}(K_4)=0$ and $A(K_4)=A(H)+2\in\{0,1\}$ (so $A(H)\in\{-2,-1\}$).
 (h) If $q_{G'}(a,b)=3$ for some pair, then $\mathrm{DEF}(G'-a-b)\ge11-A(H)\ge12$, and the components $K$ of $G'-a-b$ with $\mathrm{DEF}(K)>0$ satisfy $\sum_K(p_K+1)/2\le3$ ($p_K$ the number of members of an optimal family of $K$): there are at most three of them, and if there is only one it is isomorphic to $E_1$, to a $(10,7,4)$ piece or to $Y_9$.
 (i) If some outer vertex, say $c$, has $d_{G'}(c)=2$ and the other two, $a$ and $b$, are non-adjacent in $G'$, then $n(H)\le73$. More precisely, $H-c$ consists of the component $C_{\rm hub}$ containing $v$, with $n(C_{\rm hub})\le54$, the member $K$ of $L^*$ of (e) (applied at $c$), and at most one further component, a single vertex of $H$-degree 1.
 (j) Consequently, unless $n(H)\le73$, the outer triple is *pendant*: $d_{G'}(a)=d_{G'}(b)=d_{G'}(c)=1$ and no two of $a,b,c$ are adjacent in $G'$.
 (k) (Outer vertices of degree 1.) Let $d_{G'}(a)=d_{G'}(b)=d_{G'}(c)=1$, with edges $g_a\ni a$, $g_b\ni b$, $g_c\ni c$ in $G'$, $R=G'-\{a,b,c\}$ and $W=(g_a\cup g_b\cup g_c)\setminus\{a,b,c\}$. (k1) If two of them, say $a,b$, share their edge, then $R$ is isomorphic to a $(17,13,7)$ member of $L^*$, $n(H)=24$, and $H$ is a connected tight member of $C_3$ with $(n,m,\tau)=(24,19,10)$ (excluded by the unrefereed computations quoted in (f)). (k2) If no two are adjacent, then $\tau(R)=\tau(G')-2=\tau(H)-4$, $A(R)=A(H)+94$, and every vertex of $W$ is avoided in $R$; moreover, for $Y=\{y_a,y_b,y_c\}$ with $y_t\in g_t\setminus\{t\}$, no two in an edge of $R$, either $R+Y\notin C_3$ or $\mathrm{DEF}(R+Y)\ge3-A(H)\ge4$; and if the component $J$ of $R+Y$ containing $Y$ has positive deficiency, then in every optimal family of $J$ the edge $Y$ lies inside a member.
*Case 1** is the configuration that remains: $G'$ connected, $\mathrm{DEF}(G')=0$, $8\le A(G')\le15$, $-8\le A(H)\le-1$, $a,b,c$ individually co-transversal and pairwise NC in $G'$, and (b)–(k) and Theorem 8(d),(e).

**Theorem 13 (I11 form, tight outer graph).** Let $G\in C$ with $s(G)=0$, and suppose every member of $C$ with $n(G)-2$ or $n(G)+2$ vertices satisfies I11. Then $G$ has no b-triple: for any three vertices $a,b,c$ with $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$, some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of them. In particular the I11 form of the E1-hub lemma holds whenever $s(G)=0$ (no hypothesis on degrees, connectivity or $n(G)$ is needed).

**Corollary 14 (pattern (b) in a minimal counterexample to I11).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to I11 on $C$ and $v$ a vertex of degree 3 whose pattern (L017 Theorem 4(iii)) is (b), with components $F_1,F_2$ of $H-v$. **Theorem 15 (I11 form, slack-1 outer graph: what is forced).** Let $G\in C$ with $s(G)=1$, $n=n(G)$, every member of $C$ with fewer than $n+4$ vertices satisfying I11, and $\{a,b,c\}$ a b-configuration of $G$ with $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C$. Then:

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 Step 0.) $n,m,\tau$ are additive over disjoint unions, and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts. For an induced subgraph $G-Z$: $\tau(G)-|Z|\le\tau(G-Z)\le\tau(G)$; $\tau(G-u)=\tau(G)-1$ if $u$ is co-transversal and $\tau(G-u)=\tau(G)$ if $u$ is avoided; a set $Z$ is co-transversal iff $\tau(G-Z)=\tau(G)-|Z|$ (L017 0.4). $C$ and $C_3$ are closed under deleting vertices and edges. ### Step 3. Proof of Lemma 3
3.1 Let $X$ be an $L^*$-family of the connected 3-graph $G$ with $p=|X|\ge1$ members, let $R=G-V(X)$ have $r$ components, and let $\Gamma$ be the hypergraph whose $N=p+r$ nodes are the members of $X$ and the components of $R$ (their vertex sets partition $V(G)$), with one hyperedge $\nu(f)$ for each $f\in E^*_G(X)$: the set of nodes whose vertex set meets $f$ ($1\le|\nu(f)|\le3$).
3.2 *$\Gamma$ is connected.* An edge $f$ of $G$ meeting two different nodes is in $E^*_G(X)$: it is not inside a member (it meets two nodes); if $f\subseteq V(R)$ then $f\in E(R)$ and its vertices lie in one component of $R$, contrary to meeting two nodes; so $f$ meets $V(X)$. Given two nodes, take a vertex in each and a path between them in $G$ (consecutive vertices lie in a common edge); each step between vertices of different nodes uses an edge meeting both nodes, hence a hyperedge of $\Gamma$ containing both. So the nodes are connected in $\Gamma$.
3.3 *Counting.* Adding the hyperedges of $\Gamma$ one at a time to the $N$ isolated nodes, each reduces the number of connected components by at most $|\nu(f)|-1\le2$. Hence $|E^*_G(X)|\ge\lceil(N-1)/2\rceil$ and, with 0.3,
$$\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)=\sum_{F\in X}W(F)-35|E^*_G(X)|\le16p-35\Big\lceil\frac{p+r-1}2\Big\rceil .$$
3.4 *Cases.* (i) $p=1$, $r=0$: $V(F)=V(G)$ and $G=G[V(F)]\cong F$, $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)=W(F)$. (ii) $p=1$, $r\ge1$: $\le16-35<0$. (iii) $p=2$: $\lceil(1+r)/2\rceil\ge1$, so $\le32-35<0$. (iv) $p=3$, $r=0$: $\le\sum W-35\le13$; if moreover $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\ge11$ then $|E^*_G(X)|=1$ (two edges give $\le48-70<0$), so the single hyperedge joins all three member nodes; all edges of $G$ lie inside members or in $E^*_G(X)$ ($R=\emptyset$), so $G$ is three disjoint members plus one edge meeting each of them; and $\sum W\ge46$ forces three weights 16 ($16+16+13=45$), i.e. three copies of $E_1$: $G\cong Y_9$. (v) $p=3$, $r\ge1$: $\lceil(2+r)/2\rceil\ge2$, $\le48-70<0$. (vi) $p=4$: $\ge2$ hyperedges, $\le64-70<0$. (vii) $p\ge5$: $\le16p-35(p-1)/2=(35-3p)/2\le10$.
3.5 So every family has $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\le16$ (also $X=\emptyset$: 0), and $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\ge11$ only in (i) with $W(F)\ge11$ — $G\cong E_1$ or a $(10,7,4)$ piece (0.3) — or in (iv) with $G\cong Y_9$. ### Step 15. Proof of Theorem 15
15.1 (a) Every proper induced subgraph of $G$ has fewer vertices, so satisfies I11; by 0.9 $G$ has no isolated vertex. For $u\in\{a,b,c\}$, $s(G)=1<5\le3+2d_G(u)$ and $s(G-u)\ge0$, so $u$ is co-transversal (0.8). For the NC pair $\{a,b\}$, 0.8 gives $\tau(G-a-b)=\tau(G)-1$ and $s(G-a-b)=6-2q_G(a,b)$, which is $\ge0$ by I11 for $n-2$ vertices; so $q_G(a,b)\le3$. If $q_G(a,b)=3$, $s(G-a-b)=0$, and its components, of slack $\ge0$ each, are all tight.
15.2 (b) If $d_G(a)=d_G(b)=2$, then $q_G(a,b)=4-\sigma_G(a,b)\le3$ forces $\sigma_G(a,b)=1$. If all three had degree 2, they would be pairwise adjacent through three distinct edges, which would be all the edges at $a,b,c$; then $c$ is isolated in $G-a-b$, while $q_G(a,b)=3$ makes every component of $G-a-b$ tight by (a), and an isolated vertex has slack 3: a contradiction.
15.3 (c) is Corollary 11.1 ($s(G)=1\le2$, I11 for $n-1$ vertices). (d) is Theorem 1(c) with 1.5: slack $s(G)-1=0$, $n+2$ vertices.
15.4 (e) $H\in C$ by hypothesis, $n(H)=n+4$, $s(H)=s(G)-2=-1$ (Theorem 1(b), 1.5), and every member of $C$ with fewer vertices satisfies I11: $H$ is a minimal counterexample. For the second claim (L022 Theorem 2(vii), re-proved): $c$ is co-transversal in $G$ (15.1) and in $H$ (L017 Theorem 1(b)), so $\tau(G-c)=\tau(G)-1$ and $\tau(H-c)=\tau(H)-1=\tau(G)+1$. For any minimum transversal $T'$ of $G-c$, $T=T'\cup\{v,x\}$ is a transversal of $H-c$ (the edges of $H-c$ are those of $G-c$ and $e_0,e_a,e_b$) with $\tau(G)+1$ vertices, i.e. a minimum one; if $T'$ met $\{p,q\}$, $T$ would meet both edges at $c$, $\{c,p,q\}$ and $e_c\ni v$, and would be a transversal of $H$ with $\tau(H)-1$ vertices. So no minimum transversal of $G-c$ meets $\{p,q\}$; a minimum transversal of $G$ containing $c$ and $p$ would give one ($T\setminus\{c\}$), so $\{c,p\}$ and likewise $\{c,q\}$ are NC pairs of $G$.
15.5 (f) The vertex $a$ has degree 3 in the minimal counterexample $H$, with co-vertex pairs $P_1=\{v,x\}$, $P_2=g_1\setminus\{a\}$, $P_3=g_2\setminus\{a\}$. If $H-a$ is disconnected, then by L022 Theorem 2 (summary) the pattern is (b) or (f); (b) requires every pair to be split between the two components, but $v$ and $x$ lie in one component (joined by $e_b$ and $e_0$). So it is (f), and in the naming of L022 Theorem 2 the pair inside $F_1$ is $P_1$, $s(F_1)=1$, $s(F_2)=0$ (L022 Theorem 2(iii)), $P_2$ and $P_3$ are split with one vertex each in $F_2$, and $F_2$ is a single edge or these two vertices are pendants of $F_2$ in different edges (L022 Theorem 2(iv)). $F_1\ni v$ contains $b,c$ ($e_b,e_c$), so $F_2$ is a component of $H-a$ inside $V(G)\setminus\{a\}$ avoiding $b,c$, i.e. a component of $G-a$. If $a$ is not a cut vertex of $G$, then $G-a$ (connected, containing $b,c$) together with $v,x,y,z$ is connected, so $H-a$ is connected and the pattern is (a). $\square$

16.3 (Size.) By L021 Theorem 7(ii) [unrefereed computation] a minimal counterexample has $n\ge25$, so the hypotheses $n(H)\ge16$ (Theorem 8) and $n(H)\ge22$ (Theorem 12) hold automatically.
16.4 (Why the reductions stop at $A(H)\ge-8$.) The exact reductions available, and their effect on $A$: a fold at a co-transversal vertex of degree 2, $+8$ (Theorem 1(c), Remark 11.6); deleting a co-transversal vertex of degree 3, $0$ (0.7); a merge of an NC pair, $-27$ (Lemma 11(d)); deleting an NC outer pair, $+94-35q$ (Theorem 8(b)).
