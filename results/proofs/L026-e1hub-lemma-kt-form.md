## Statement

**Conventions.** As in L021. $C$ is the class of linear $F_7$-free 3-graphs with $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ (property (P3)); $C_3=\{G\in C:\Delta(G)\le3\}$; $C$ and $C_3$ are closed under deleting vertices. $G-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(G)\setminus Z$; $\partial_G(Z)$ is the set of edges of $G$ meeting $Z$. A vertex set $S$ is *co-transversal* in $G$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains $S$; a vertex $u$ is *avoided* in $G$ if it lies in no minimum transversal of $G$; a vertex set $Q$ is *avoided* if no minimum transversal of $G$ meets $Q$. $A(G)=27n+35m-132\tau$, $\mathrm{DEF}(G)$ and $P(G)=A(G)+\mathrm{DEF}(G)$ are as in L021 Definition 2 with $L=L^*$ ($P(\emptyset)=0$, $P(K_1)=27$). $E_1$ is the single edge. $Y_9$ is the 3-graph with edges $\{1,2,3\},\{4,5,6\},\{7,8,9\},\{1,4,7\}$ ($A(Y_9)=243+140-396=-13$).

**Hub and fold.** For a 3-graph $G$ and distinct vertices $a,b,c$: $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,x,y,z$ and the four *hub edges* $e_0=\{x,y,z\}$, $e_a=\{v,x,a\}$, $e_b=\{v,y,b\}$, $e_c=\{v,z,c\}$; $\{a,b,c\}$ is the *outer triple*. $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,z$ and the edges $\{v,a,b\}$, $\{v,z,c\}$. A triple $\{a,b,c\}$ is a *b-configuration* of $G$ if $a,b,c$ have $G$-degree $\le2$, $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$, and no two of $a,b,c$ are co-transversal in $G$ (pairwise NC).

An *E1-hub* of a 3-graph $H$ is $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ with $E(H)\ni e_0,e_a,e_b,e_c$ as above and $d_H(x)=d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$; its *outer graph* is $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}$. If $\Delta(H)\le3$ the edges of $H$ meeting $\{v,x,y,z\}$ are exactly the four hub edges, so $H=G'\oplus(a,b,c)$.

**The KT form of the E1-hub lemma** (SYNTHESIS.md Section 3). *No minimal counterexample $H$ to KT$^{L^*}$ has an E1-hub whose outer triple is a b-configuration of $G'$.*

**Theorem 1 (hub arithmetic; no minimality).** Let $G\in C_3$ and let $a,b,c$ be distinct vertices of $G$-degree $\le2$ with $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$.
 (a) $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C_3$.
 (b) $\tau(G\oplus(a,b,c))=\tau(G)+1$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of $a,b,c$, and $\tau(G)+2$ otherwise. Hence $A$ changes by $+116$ or $-16$, and the I11 slack $s=3n+2m-11\tau$ by $+9$ or $-2$.
 (c) $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)\in C_3$ if and only if no edge of $G$ contains both $a$ and $b$. Its transversal number is $\tau(G)$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains $c$ and one of $a,b$, and $\tau(G)+1$ otherwise; in particular, if $\{a,b,c\}$ is a b-configuration, $A(G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c))=A(G)-8=A(G\oplus(a,b,c))+8$.

**Lemma 2 (families in induced subgraphs; no minimality).** Let $H_0$ be an induced subgraph of a 3-graph $H$ and $X$ an $L^*$-family of $H_0$. Then $X$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$, $E^*_H(X)=E^*_{H_0}(X)\sqcup\{f\in E(H)\setminus E(H_0):f\cap V(X)\ne\emptyset\}$, and so $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_{H_0}(X)-35\,|\{f\in E(H)\setminus E(H_0):f\cap V(X)\ne\emptyset\}|$.

**Lemma 3 (deficiency of a connected graph; no minimality).** Let $G$ be a connected 3-graph. Then $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le16$. If $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\ge11$, then $G$ is isomorphic to $E_1$, to one of the four $(10,7,4)$ members of $L^*$, or to $Y_9$. Consequently $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le13$ unless $G\cong E_1$, and $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le10$ if $n(G)\ge11$.

**Lemma 4 (avoided vertices; no minimality).** If $u$ is avoided in a 3-graph $G$, then $P(G)\ge P(G-u)+27$.

**Lemma 5 (bridge lemma).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$, $e\in E(H)$, and $V(H)=V_1\sqcup V_2$ with $V_1,V_2\ne\emptyset$, such that every edge of $H$ other than $e$ lies inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$, and $e$ meets both. Put $H_i=H[V_i]$ and $Q_i=e\cap V_i$. Then $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$; equivalently, $Q_1$ is avoided in $H_1$ and $Q_2$ is avoided in $H_2$.

**Lemma 6 (pendant-block lemma).** In the setting of Lemma 5, if $|Q_2|=1$ then $V_2=Q_2$.

**Corollary 7 (bridges are pendant).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ and $e\in E(H)$ such that $H\setminus e$ (delete the edge, keep the vertices) is disconnected. Then $H\setminus e$ has exactly two components, one of them is a single vertex $u$ (so $d_H(u)=1$), and $e\setminus\{u\}$ is avoided in $H-u$.

**Theorem 8 (KT form: reduction to Case 1).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to KT$^{L^*}$ with $n(H)\ge16$ and with an E1-hub $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ whose outer triple is a b-configuration of $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}$. Then:
 (a) $G'$ is connected.
 (b) Each of $a,b,c$ lies in a minimum transversal of $G'$. Hence, for each pair, say $\{a,b\}$: $\tau(G'-a-b)=\tau(G')-1$ and $A(G'-a-b)=A(H)+94-35\,q_{ab}$, where $q_{ab}=|\partial_{G'}(\{a,b\})|\le4$.
 (c) $A(G')=A(H)+16$ and $-10\le A(G')\le15$, so $-26\le A(H)\le-1$. If some pair of $a,b,c$ lies in no common edge of $G'$, then $A(H)\ge-18$.
 (d) For each pair, say $\{a,b\}$, every component $K$ of $G'-a-b$ with $c\notin V(K)$ either meets at least two edges of $\partial_{G'}(\{a,b\})$ or is a single vertex of $H$-degree 1; and $\sum_KA(K)=A(H)+94-35q_{ab}$, the sum over the components of $G'-a-b$.
 (e) If $q_{ab}=4$ for some pair $\{a,b\}$ (that is, $d_{G'}(a)=d_{G'}(b)=2$ and no edge of $G'$ contains $a$ and $b$), then $G'-a-b$ has exactly four components, none of them a single vertex, one of them contains $c$, and at least two of them are isomorphic to $E_1$, to a $(10,7,4)$ member of $L^*$, or to $Y_9$.

So in a minimal counterexample to the KT form (if one exists) the outer graph is connected, its outer vertices are individually co-transversal but pairwise not, the hub costs exactly 16 in $A$, and $-26\le A(H)\le-1$. This is **Case 1**; Cases 2 and 3 (disconnected $G'$) are impossible.

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 Step 0.) $n,m,\tau$ are additive over disjoint unions, and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts. For an induced subgraph $G-Z$: $\tau(G)-|Z|\le\tau(G-Z)\le\tau(G)$; $\tau(G-u)=\tau(G)-1$ if $u$ is co-transversal and $\tau(G-u)=\tau(G)$ if $u$ is avoided. $C$ and $C_3$ are closed under vertex deletion.
0.2 (L021 Step 0.6.) $\mathrm{DEF}$, hence $P$, is additive over disjoint unions (members of $L^*$ are connected, so a family of $H_1\sqcup H_2$ splits). In particular a minimal counterexample $H$ is connected (L021 Theorem 3(A)): otherwise $H=H_1\sqcup H_2$ with both parts in $C_3$ and smaller, and $P(H)=P(H_1)+P(H_2)\ge0$.
0.3 Every member of $L^*$ is connected and has at least 3 vertices; $W(F)\le16$ for all $F\in L^*$; $W(F)\ge11$ only for $E_1$ ($W=16$) and the four $(10,7,4)$ classes ($W=13$); the other weights are 5, 2, 10 (L021 Step 0.7; recomputed in C1).
0.4 $P(E_1)=0$: $A(E_1)=81+35-132=-16$, and the family $\{E_1\}$ gives $\mathrm{DEF}\ge16$, while $\mathrm{DEF}(E_1)\le16$ by Lemma 3 (or directly: the only member of a family of $E_1$ is $E_1$ itself). So $E_1$ is not a counterexample.
0.5 $A$ is an integer and $\mathrm{DEF}\ge0$ (the empty family), so a minimal counterexample has $A(H)\le P(H)\le-1$.

### Step 1. Proof of Theorem 1
1.1 (a) *Degrees:* $v,x,y,z$ have degree 3, 2, 2, 2, and $a,b,c$ gain one edge each, so $\Delta\le3$. *Linearity:* two hub edges meet in exactly one vertex ($e_a\cap e_b=\{v\}$, $e_a\cap e_0=\{x\}$, and so on, as $a,b,c$ are distinct); a hub edge and an edge $f$ of $G$ meet only in $V(G)$, and each hub edge has at most one vertex in $V(G)$. *$F_7$:* let $F$ be a copy of $F_7$ (7 vertices, 5 edges, every vertex of $F$-degree $\ge2$) in $G\oplus(a,b,c)$. If $F$ contains $x$, then both edges at $x$, namely $e_0$ and $e_a$, are in $F$; then $y,z\in V(F)$ and, as $y,z$ have degree 2, also $e_b,e_c\in F$. So $V(F)\supseteq\{v,x,y,z,a,b,c\}$, which has 7 elements, so equality holds, and the fifth edge of $F$ lies inside this set and is not a hub edge; as all edges at $v,x,y,z$ are hub edges, it is an edge of $G$ inside $\{a,b,c\}$, i.e. $\{a,b,c\}\in E(G)$: excluded. The same argument applies if $F$ contains $y$ or $z$. If $F$ contains $v$ but none of $x,y,z$, then $v$ has $F$-degree 0 (every edge at $v$ contains one of $x,y,z$): impossible. So $F\subseteq G$, impossible as $G\in C$. *(P3):* let $S\subseteq V(G\oplus)$, $|S|\ge2$, $S_0=S\cap V(G)$, $S_1=S\cap\{v,x,y,z\}$, and $h$ = number of hub edges inside $S$; $e(S)=e_G(S_0)+h$.
 - $S_1=\emptyset$: $e(S)=e_G(S_0)\le|S|-2$.
 - $\emptyset\ne S_1\ne\{v,x,y,z\}$: if $v\notin S_1$, only $e_0$ can be inside $S$, and only if $S_1=\{x,y,z\}$, so $h\le|S_1|-1$; if $v\in S_1$, then $e_0\not\subseteq S$ unless $S_1\supseteq\{x,y,z\}$ (excluded), and each of $e_a,e_b,e_c$ inside $S$ uses a distinct element of $S_1\setminus\{v\}$, so $h\le|S_1|-1$. If $|S_0|\ge2$: $e(S)\le|S_0|-2+|S_1|-1=|S|-3$. If $|S_0|\le1$: $e_G(S_0)=0$ and at most one hub edge is inside $S$ (each of $e_a,e_b,e_c$ needs its own vertex of $S_0$, and $e_0\subseteq S$ forces $v\notin S$, hence no other hub edge); an edge inside $S$ forces $|S|\ge3$, so $e(S)\le|S|-2$.
 - $S_1=\{v,x,y,z\}$: $h=1+|S_0\cap\{a,b,c\}|\le1+\min(3,|S_0|)$. If $|S_0|\ge2$: $e(S)\le|S_0|-2+4=|S|-2$. If $|S_0|\le1$: $e(S)\le2\le|S|-2$.
 So $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C_3$. $\square$
1.2 (b) Let $T$ be a transversal of $H:=G\oplus(a,b,c)$. $T\cap V(G)$ is a transversal of $G$ ($G$ is an induced subgraph: every edge of $G$ is an edge of $H$ inside $V(G)$), and $T$ meets $e_0\subseteq\{x,y,z\}$, so $|T|\ge\tau(G)+1$. If $|T|=\tau(G)+1$, then $T_0:=T\cap V(G)$ is a minimum transversal of $G$ and $T$ has exactly one vertex $w\in\{v,x,y,z\}$; $w=v$ misses $e_0$; $w=x$ forces $b,c\in T_0$ (to meet $e_b,e_c$); $w=y$ forces $a,c\in T_0$; $w=z$ forces $a,b\in T_0$. Conversely, if a minimum transversal $T_0$ of $G$ contains, say, $b$ and $c$, then $T_0\cup\{x\}$ is a transversal of $H$ (it meets $e_0,e_a$ in $x$, $e_b$ in $b$, $e_c$ in $c$, and the edges of $G$ in $T_0$). Finally $T_0\cup\{v,x\}$ is always a transversal. This proves the formula. With $\Delta n=\Delta m=4$: $\Delta A=108+140-132\Delta\tau\in\{116,-16\}$ and $\Delta s=12+8-11\Delta\tau\in\{9,-2\}$. $\square$
1.3 (c) Put $H^\circ=G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)$. *Linearity:* $\{v,a,b\}\cap\{v,z,c\}=\{v\}$; $\{v,z,c\}$ meets an edge of $G$ only in $c$; $\{v,a,b\}$ meets an edge $f$ of $G$ in $f\cap\{a,b\}$, which has 2 elements iff $f\supseteq\{a,b\}$. So $H^\circ$ is linear iff no edge of $G$ contains $a$ and $b$; assume this from now on. *Degrees:* $d(v)=2$, $d(z)=1$, and $a,b,c$ gain one edge each: $\Delta\le3$. *$F_7$:* $z$ has degree 1, so a copy $F$ of $F_7$ avoids $z$ and $\{v,z,c\}$; then $v$ has $F$-degree $\le1$, so $v\notin V(F)$ and $F\subseteq G$: impossible. *(P3):* for $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ and $S_0=S\cap V(G)$: $e(S)=e_G(S_0)+[\{v,a,b\}\subseteq S]+[\{v,z,c\}\subseteq S]$. If $v\notin S$: $e(S)=e_G(S_0)$, which is $\le|S_0|-2\le|S|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$ and 0 otherwise. If $v\in S\not\ni z$: $e(S)\le e_G(S_0)+1\le|S_0|-1=|S|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$, and $e(S)=0$ otherwise. If $v,z\in S$: $e(S)\le e_G(S_0)+2\le|S_0|=|S|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$; if $|S_0|\le1$, $e(S)\le1$ and $e(S)=1$ only for $S=\{v,z,c\}$. So $H^\circ\in C_3$. *Transversal number:* as in 1.2, a transversal $T$ of $H^\circ$ has $|T\cap V(G)|\ge\tau(G)$; $|T|=\tau(G)$ forces $T\cap\{v,z\}=\emptyset$, $T$ a minimum transversal of $G$ meeting $\{a,b\}$ (for $\{v,a,b\}$) and containing $c$ (for $\{v,z,c\}$); conversely such a $T$ is a transversal of $H^\circ$; and $T_0\cup\{v\}$ always is. If $\{a,b,c\}$ is a b-configuration no minimum transversal of $G$ contains $c$ together with $a$ or $b$, so $\tau(H^\circ)=\tau(G)+1$ and $A(H^\circ)=A(G)+54+70-132=A(G)-8$, which is $A(G\oplus(a,b,c))+8$ by (b). $\square$
1.4 *Remark.* $H^\circ$ is the fold $H^\circ_{x;y,a}$ of $H=G\oplus(a,b,c)$ at $x$ (L019 Theorem 5, merging $y$ with $a$): deleting $x$, its edges $e_0,e_a$, and identifying $y$ with $a$ turns $e_b$ into $\{v,a,b\}$ and keeps $e_c$. L019 is not needed: 1.3 proves everything used.

### Step 2. Proof of Lemma 2
2.1 A member $F$ of $X$ satisfies $H_0[V(F)]\cong$ a piece; as $H_0$ is induced in $H$, $H[V(F)]=H_0[V(F)]$. So $X$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$ (members stay pairwise disjoint). An edge $f$ of $H_0$ is in $E^*_H(X)$ iff it is in $E^*_{H_0}(X)$ (same vertex sets, same members). An edge $f\in E(H)\setminus E(H_0)$ is not inside $V(H_0)$ ($H_0$ is induced), hence inside no member, so $f\in E^*_H(X)$ iff $f$ meets $V(X)$. The inequality follows from $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)=\sum_XW-35|E^*_H(X)|$. $\square$

### Step 3. Proof of Lemma 3
3.1 Let $X$ be an $L^*$-family of the connected 3-graph $G$ with $p=|X|\ge1$ members, let $R=G-V(X)$ have $r$ components, and let $\Gamma$ be the hypergraph whose $N=p+r$ nodes are the members of $X$ and the components of $R$ (their vertex sets partition $V(G)$), with one hyperedge $\nu(f)$ for each $f\in E^*_G(X)$: the set of nodes whose vertex set meets $f$ ($1\le|\nu(f)|\le3$).
3.2 *$\Gamma$ is connected.* An edge $f$ of $G$ meeting two different nodes is in $E^*_G(X)$: it is not inside a member (it meets two nodes); if $f\subseteq V(R)$ then $f\in E(R)$ and its vertices lie in one component of $R$, contrary to meeting two nodes; so $f$ meets $V(X)$. Given two nodes, take a vertex in each and a path between them in $G$ (consecutive vertices lie in a common edge); each step between vertices of different nodes uses an edge meeting both nodes, hence a hyperedge of $\Gamma$ containing both. So the nodes are connected in $\Gamma$.
3.3 *Counting.* Adding the hyperedges of $\Gamma$ one at a time to the $N$ isolated nodes, each reduces the number of connected components by at most $|\nu(f)|-1\le2$. Hence $|E^*_G(X)|\ge\lceil(N-1)/2\rceil$ and, with 0.3,
$$\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)=\sum_{F\in X}W(F)-35|E^*_G(X)|\le16p-35\Big\lceil\frac{p+r-1}2\Big\rceil .$$
3.4 *Cases.* (i) $p=1$, $r=0$: $V(F)=V(G)$ and $G=G[V(F)]\cong F$, $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)=W(F)$. (ii) $p=1$, $r\ge1$: $\le16-35<0$. (iii) $p=2$: $\lceil(1+r)/2\rceil\ge1$, so $\le32-35<0$. (iv) $p=3$, $r=0$: $\le\sum W-35\le13$; if moreover $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\ge11$ then $|E^*_G(X)|=1$ (two edges give $\le48-70<0$), so the single hyperedge joins all three member nodes; all edges of $G$ lie inside members or in $E^*_G(X)$ ($R=\emptyset$), so $G$ is three disjoint members plus one edge meeting each of them; and $\sum W\ge46$ forces three weights 16 ($16+16+13=45$), i.e. three copies of $E_1$: $G\cong Y_9$. (v) $p=3$, $r\ge1$: $\lceil(2+r)/2\rceil\ge2$, $\le48-70<0$. (vi) $p=4$: $\ge2$ hyperedges, $\le64-70<0$. (vii) $p\ge5$: $\le16p-35(p-1)/2=(35-3p)/2\le10$.
3.5 So every family has $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\le16$ (also $X=\emptyset$: 0), and $\mathrm{DEF}_G(X)\ge11$ only in (i) with $W(F)\ge11$ — $G\cong E_1$ or a $(10,7,4)$ piece (0.3) — or in (iv) with $G\cong Y_9$. In (i) with $G\not\cong E_1$, and in (iv), the bound is $\le13$. As $n(E_1)=3$, $n(Y_9)=9$ and the $(10,7,4)$ pieces have 10 vertices, $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\le10$ if $n(G)\ge11$. $\square$

### Step 4. Proof of Lemma 4
4.1 $u$ avoided gives $\tau(G-u)=\tau(G)$ (0.1), so $A(G)=A(G-u)+27+35d_G(u)$. Let $X$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(G-u)$. The edges of $G$ not in $G-u$ are the $d_G(u)$ edges at $u$, so Lemma 2 gives $\mathrm{DEF}(G)\ge\mathrm{DEF}(G-u)-35d_G(u)$. Adding: $P(G)\ge P(G-u)+27$. $\square$

### Step 5. Proof of Lemma 5
5.1 $H_i=H[V_i]\in C_3$ (0.1) and $n(H_i)+m(H_i)<n(H)+m(H)$, because $n(H)+m(H)=n(H_1)+n(H_2)+m(H_1)+m(H_2)+1$ with $n(H_1),n(H_2)\ge1$. So $P(H_1),P(H_2)\ge0$.
5.2 A transversal of $H$ restricts to transversals of $H_1$ and $H_2$ (disjoint vertex sets), so $\tau(H)\ge\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$; adding one vertex of $e$ to $T_1\cup T_2$ ($T_i$ minimum transversals of $H_i$) shows $\tau(H)\le\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$.
5.3 Suppose $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$. Then $A(H)=A(H_1)+A(H_2)+35$ ($n$ and $\tau$ add, $m(H)=m(H_1)+m(H_2)+1$). Let $X_i$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(H_i)$; by Lemma 2 (twice) $X=X_1\cup X_2$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$ (members disjoint). An edge $f\ne e$ of $H$ lies in some $H_i$, say $H_1$; if $f$ meets $V(X)$ it meets $V(X_1)$, and it is inside a member of $X$ iff inside a member of $X_1$; so $f\in E^*_H(X)$ implies $f\in E^*_{H_1}(X_1)$. Hence $|E^*_H(X)|\le|E^*_{H_1}(X_1)|+|E^*_{H_2}(X_2)|+1$ and $\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}(H_1)+\mathrm{DEF}(H_2)-35$. Adding, $P(H)\ge P(H_1)+P(H_2)\ge0$, contradicting $P(H)<0$. So $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$.
5.4 (Equivalence.) If a minimum transversal $T_1$ of $H_1$ met $Q_1$, then $T_1\cup T_2$ would be a transversal of $H$ of size $\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$; similarly for $Q_2$. Conversely a transversal $T$ of $H$ with $\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$ elements has $T\cap V_i$ minimum transversals of $H_i$, one of which meets $e$. $\square$

### Step 6. Proof of Lemma 6
6.1 Write $Q_2=\{c\}$, $Q_1=\{p,q\}$ ($|e|=3$) and suppose $|V_2|\ge2$. Lemma 5: $\{p,q\}$ is avoided in $H_1$, $c$ is avoided in $H_2$, $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$.
6.2 Let $H^\#=H_1+w+\{p,q,w\}$ with a new vertex $w$. *$H^\#\in C_3$:* $d_{H^\#}(p)=d_{H_1}(p)+1=d_H(p)\le3$, the same for $q$, $d(w)=1$; no edge of $H_1$ contains $p$ and $q$ (they lie in $e$, $H$ is linear), so $H^\#$ is linear; a copy of $F_7$ avoids the degree-1 vertex $w$, hence lies in $H_1\in C$: impossible; for (P3), $e_{H^\#}(S)=e_{H_1}(S)$ if $w\notin S$, and $e_{H^\#}(S)\le e_{H_1}(S\setminus\{w\})+1\le|S|-2$ if $w\in S$ and $|S|\ge3$ ($e_{H^\#}(S)=0$ if $|S|=2$). *Smaller:* $n(H^\#)+m(H^\#)=n(H_1)+m(H_1)+2<n(H)+m(H)=n(H_1)+m(H_1)+n(H_2)+m(H_2)+1$, because $H_2$ is connected (every component of $H\setminus e$ inside $V_2$ meets $e$, and $e\cap V_2=\{c\}$) with $|V_2|\ge2$ vertices, hence $m(H_2)\ge1$ and $n(H_2)+m(H_2)\ge4$. So $P(H^\#)\ge0$, and also $P(H_2-c)\ge0$ ($H_2-c\in C_3$ is smaller).
6.3 *Potentials.* A minimum transversal of $H_1$ avoids $\{p,q\}$, so $\tau(H^\#)=\tau(H_1)+1$ (a transversal of $H^\#$ with $\tau(H_1)$ elements would be a minimum transversal of $H_1$ meeting $\{p,q\}$; $T_1\cup\{w\}$ works) and $A(H^\#)=A(H_1)+27+35-132=A(H_1)-70$. As $c$ is avoided in $H_2$, $A(H_2)=A(H_2-c)+27+35d_{H_2}(c)$ (0.1). And $A(H)=A(H_1)+A(H_2)+35-132$. Together:
$$A(H)=A(H^\#)+A(H_2-c)+35\,d_{H_2}(c).$$
6.4 *Families.* Let $X^\#$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(H^\#)$ and $X_2$ attain $\mathrm{DEF}(H_2-c)$. Let $\psi$ be the injection $V(H^\#)\to V(H)$ that is the identity on $V_1$ and sends $w$ to $c$. For a member $F$ of $X^\#$: if $w\notin V(F)$ then $V(F)\subseteq V_1$ and $H[V(F)]=H_1[V(F)]=H^\#[V(F)]$. If $w\in V(F)$, then, $F$ being connected with at least 3 vertices and $\{p,q,w\}$ the only edge at $w$, $p,q\in V(F)$; the edges of $H$ inside $\psi(V(F))=(V(F)\setminus\{w\})\cup\{c\}$ are the edges of $H_1$ inside $V(F)\setminus\{w\}$ and $e=\psi(\{p,q,w\})$ (an edge of $H_2$ at $c$ has a vertex in $V_2\setminus\{c\}$, outside $\psi(V(F))$); so $\psi$ is an isomorphism $H^\#[V(F)]\to H[\psi(V(F))]$. Hence $\psi(X^\#)$ is an $L^*$-family of $H$, and $X:=\psi(X^\#)\cup X_2$ is one too ($X_2$ lives in the induced subgraph $H[V_2\setminus\{c\}]$, disjoint from $\psi(V(H^\#))=V_1\cup\{c\}$).
6.5 *$E^*$.* Let $f\in E^*_H(X)$. (i) $f\in E(H_1)$: $f\subseteq V_1$ meets $V(X)\cap V_1=V(X^\#)\cap V_1$ and lies inside no member of $X^\#$ (on $V_1$, $\psi$ is the identity), so $f\in E^*_{H^\#}(X^\#)$. (ii) $f=e$: $e$ meets $V(X)$ in $p$, $q$ or $c$, and $c\in V(X)$ only through $\psi(X^\#)$, so $\{p,q,w\}$ meets $V(X^\#)$; it lies inside no member of $X^\#$ (else $e$ would lie inside its image); so $\{p,q,w\}\in E^*_{H^\#}(X^\#)$. (iii) $f\in E(H_2)$, $c\notin f$: $f\subseteq V_2\setminus\{c\}$, so $f\in E^*_{H_2-c}(X_2)$. (iv) $f\in E(H_2)$, $c\in f$: at most $d_{H_2}(c)$ edges. As (i), (ii) map injectively into $E^*_{H^\#}(X^\#)$:
$$\mathrm{DEF}(H)\ge\mathrm{DEF}_H(X)\ge\mathrm{DEF}(H^\#)+\mathrm{DEF}(H_2-c)-35\,d_{H_2}(c).$$
6.6 Adding 6.3 and 6.5: $P(H)\ge P(H^\#)+P(H_2-c)\ge0$, a contradiction. So $|V_2|=1$. $\square$

### Step 7. Proof of Corollary 7, and two remarks
7.1 $H$ is connected (0.2), so each component of $H\setminus e$ meets $e$, and there are 2 or 3 of them. If there are three, each meets $e$ in one vertex; Lemma 6 (with $V_2$ one component, $V_1$ the union of the other two) makes each a single vertex, so $H=E_1$, which is not a counterexample (0.4). If there are two, $e$ meets them in 2 and 1 vertices; Lemma 6 with $V_2$ the component met once gives $V_2=\{u\}$, $d_H(u)=1$; Lemma 5 with $H_1=H-u$ gives that $e\setminus\{u\}$ is avoided in $H-u$. $\square$
7.2 *Remark (what Lemma 6 replaces).* Lemma 6 is the KT analogue of replacing a pendant block by a pendant edge: the block $H_2$ with its attaching edge is worth at least $P(H_2-c)$ more than a single new vertex $w$. Its proof needs only $P\ge0$ on $H^\#$ and $H_2-c$, so the inequality $P(H)\ge P(H^\#)+P(H_2-c)$ holds for every $H$ with such an edge in which $\{p,q\}$ and $c$ are avoided (tested in C3).
7.3 *Remark (I11 analogue; not used).* The same two lemmas hold for a vertex-minimal I11 violator $H$ in $C$ (I11 for all members of $C$ with fewer vertices): in Lemma 5, $\tau(H)=\tau_1+\tau_2$ would give $s(H)=s(H_1)+s(H_2)+2\ge2$; in Lemma 6, $s(H)=s(H^\#)+s(H_2-c)+2d_{H_2}(c)\ge2$ (with $s(H^\#)=s(H_1)-6$ and $s(H_2)=s(H_2-c)+3+2d_{H_2}(c)$), as $H^\#$ and $H_2-c$ have fewer vertices. For the I11 form of the E1-hub lemma $G$ is connected by hypothesis, so this adds nothing there.

### Step 8. Proof of Theorem 8
8.1 *Set-up.* $\Delta(H)\le3$, so the edges at $v$ are $e_a,e_b,e_c$, those at $x,y,z$ are hub edges, and the edges meeting $\{v,x,y,z\}$ are exactly the four hub edges. $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}\in C_3$, $H=G'\oplus(a,b,c)$, $a,b,c$ are distinct ($e_a\cap e_b=\{v\}$ by linearity), $d_{G'}(a)=d_H(a)-1\le2$ (and so for $b,c$), and $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G')$ (the definition of a b-configuration; also, otherwise the hub edges and $\{a,b,c\}$ would form an $F_7$ with centre $v$). By Theorem 1(b), $\tau(H)=\tau(G')+2$ and $A(H)=A(G')-16$; by 0.5, $A(H)\le-1$, so $A(G')\le15$. $H$ is connected (0.2) and the only edges between $\{v,x,y,z\}$ and $V(G')$ are $e_a,e_b,e_c$, so every component of $G'$ contains one of $a,b,c$; $G'$ has 1, 2 or 3 components. $n(G')=n(H)-4\ge12$.
8.2 *(a), three components* $G_a\ni a$, $G_b\ni b$, $G_c\ni c$. The edge $e_a$ and the partition $V_2=V(G_a)$, $V_1=V(H)\setminus V(G_a)$ satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma 5 (the edges of $G_a$ lie inside $V_2$; all other edges of $H$ except $e_a$ — those of $G_b$, $G_c$ and $e_0,e_b,e_c$ — inside $V_1$; $e_a$ meets $V_2$ in $a$ and $V_1$ in $v,x$). So $\{v,x\}$ is avoided in $H_1=H[V_1]$. Likewise ($e_b$, $e_c$) $b$ is avoided in $G_b$ and $c$ in $G_c$. Now $H_1$ consists of $G_b$, $G_c$ and the edges $e_0,e_b,e_c$. A transversal $T$ of $H_1$ has at least $\tau(G_b)$ vertices in $V(G_b)$, $\tau(G_c)$ in $V(G_c)$ and one in $e_0$. If $|T|=\tau(G_b)+\tau(G_c)+1$, then $T\cap V(G_b)$, $T\cap V(G_c)$ are minimum transversals (so $b,c\notin T$) and $T$ has one vertex $w\in\{x,y,z\}$, which must lie in $e_b\cap e_c\cap\{x,y,z\}=\emptyset$: impossible. Hence $\tau(H_1)=\tau(G_b)+\tau(G_c)+2$ and $T_b\cup T_c\cup\{v,x\}$ is a minimum transversal of $H_1$ containing $v$: a contradiction.
8.3 *(a), two components*, one containing two outer vertices, say $G_1\ni a,b$, the other $G_2\ni c$. The edges meeting $V(G_2)$ are those of $G_2$ and $e_c$, with $e_c\cap V(G_2)=\{c\}$; Lemma 6 (with $e=e_c$, $V_2=V(G_2)$) gives $V(G_2)=\{c\}$. Then $G'=G_1\sqcup\{c\}$ with $G_1$ connected, $n(G_1)=n(H)-5\ge11$, $\tau(G')=\tau(G_1)$, $A(G')=A(G_1)+27$, so $A(G_1)=A(H)+16-27\le-12$. $G_1\in C_3$ is smaller than $H$, so $\mathrm{DEF}(G_1)\ge-A(G_1)\ge12$, contradicting Lemma 3 ($G_1$ connected with at least 11 vertices). Hence $G'$ is connected: (a).
8.4 *(b).* If $a$ were avoided in $G'$, Lemma 4 and $P(G'-a)\ge0$ ($G'-a\in C_3$ is smaller) would give $P(G')\ge27$; but $P(G')=A(G')+\mathrm{DEF}(G')\le15+10=25$ by 8.1 and Lemma 3 ($G'$ connected with $n(G')\ge12$). So $a$, and likewise $b$ and $c$, lies in a minimum transversal of $G'$. For the pair $\{a,b\}$: a minimum transversal $T_a\ni a$ gives the transversal $T_a\setminus\{a,b\}$ of $G'-a-b$, so $\tau(G'-a-b)\le\tau(G')-1$; and $\tau(G'-a-b)\ge\tau(G')-1$, since a transversal $T'$ of $G'-a-b$ with $\tau(G')-2$ elements would make $T'\cup\{a,b\}$ a minimum transversal of $G'$ containing $a$ and $b$. With $n$ down by 2 and $m$ down by $q_{ab}$: $A(G'-a-b)=A(G')-54-35q_{ab}+132=A(H)+94-35q_{ab}$. $q_{ab}\le d_{G'}(a)+d_{G'}(b)\le4$.
8.5 *(c).* $A(G')=A(H)+16\le15$ (8.1) and $A(G')\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(G')\ge-10$ ($P(G')\ge0$, Lemma 3). If no edge of $G'$ contains $a$ and $b$, then $H^\circ=G'\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)\in C_3$ with $A(H^\circ)=A(H)+8$ (Theorem 1(c)); $H^\circ$ is connected ($G'$ is connected, $v\in\{v,a,b\}$, $z\in\{v,z,c\}$), has $n(G')+2\ge14$ vertices, and $n(H^\circ)+m(H^\circ)=n(H)+m(H)-4$; so $A(H^\circ)\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(H^\circ)\ge-10$ and $A(H)\ge-18$. The same for the pairs $\{a,c\}$, $\{b,c\}$ (with the roles permuted).
8.6 *(d).* Let $R=G'-a-b$, $Q=\partial_{G'}(\{a,b\})$, $q=q_{ab}$, and $K$ a component of $R$ with $c\notin V(K)$. The vertices of $K$ lie on no hub edge ($K\cap\{a,b,c\}=\emptyset$), and an edge of $G'$ meeting $V(K)$ either lies inside $R$ — then inside $K$ — or meets $\{a,b\}$, i.e. lies in $Q$. So the edges of $H$ meeting $V(K)$ and not inside $K$ are exactly the edges of $Q$ meeting $V(K)$; there is at least one ($G'$ is connected and $a\notin V(K)$). Suppose there is exactly one, $f$. Then $f$ and the partition $V(K)\sqcup(V(H)\setminus V(K))$ satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma 5. If $|f\cap V(K)|=2$, then $f$ meets $V(H)\setminus V(K)$ in one vertex ($a$ or $b$) and Lemma 6 would give $V(H)\setminus V(K)=\{a\}$ or $\{b\}$, absurd (it contains $v$). So $|f\cap V(K)|=1$ and Lemma 6 (with $V_2=V(K)$) gives $|V(K)|=1$; that vertex has $H$-degree 1. The identity $\sum_KA(K)=A(R)=A(H)+94-35q$ is 0.1 and (b).
8.7 *(e).* Now $d_{G'}(a)=d_{G'}(b)=2$ and no edge contains $a,b$; $Q=\{f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4\}$ with $f_1,f_2\ni a$, $f_3,f_4\ni b$, and each $f_i$ has exactly two vertices in $V(R)$. Let $I(K)$ be the number of edges of $Q$ meeting the component $K$; $\sum_KI(K)\le\sum_i|f_i\cap V(R)|=8$; $I(K)\ge1$ for every $K$ (8.6), and $I(K)\ge2$ for every component $K\ne K_c$ with $|V(K)|\ge2$ (8.6), where $K_c\ni c$. By (b), $\sum_KA(K)=A(H)-46\le-47$. A single-vertex component has $A=27$. A component $K$ with at least two vertices is in $C_3$ and smaller than $H$, so $A(K)\ge-\mathrm{DEF}(K)$, and by Lemma 3: $A(K)\ge-16$, with equality only for $K\cong E_1$; $A(K)\ge-13$ if $K\cong Y_9$ or a $(10,7,4)$ piece; $A(K)\ge-10$ otherwise. Let $N$ be the number of components with at least two vertices, $t$ the number of those isomorphic to $E_1$, $Y_9$ or a $(10,7,4)$ piece, and $s_1$ the number of single-vertex components.
 - Incidences: if $K_c$ has at least two vertices, $1+2(N-1)+s_1\le8$, i.e. $2N+s_1\le9$; otherwise $s_1\ge1$ and $2N+s_1\le8$. In both cases $N\le4$.
 - $N\le2$: $\sum_KA(K)\ge-32>-47$. Impossible.
 - $N=3$: $\sum_KA(K)\ge-16t_{16}-13t_{13}-10(3-t)+27s_1$ ($t_{16}$, $t_{13}$ the numbers of $E_1$'s and of the other two types, $t=t_{16}+t_{13}$). $\le-47$ forces $s_1=0$ and $6t_{16}+3t_{13}\ge17$, i.e. $t_{16}=3$: three $E_1$ components and no other vertex, so $n(G')=2+9=11<12$: impossible.
 - $N=4$: if $K_c$ were a single vertex then $s_1\ge1$ and $2N+s_1\le8$ is violated; so $K_c$ has at least two vertices and $s_1\le1$; $s_1=1$ would need $\sum_{|V(K)|\ge2}A(K)\le-74<-64$; so $s_1=0$, and $-16t_{16}-13t_{13}-10(4-t)\le-47$, i.e. $6t_{16}+3t_{13}\ge7$, forces $t\ge2$.
 So $N=4$, $s_1=0$, $K_c$ is one of the four, and $t\ge2$. $\square$

### Step 9. Consequences and remarks
9.1 (Cases 2 and 3 of the synthesis are void.) In the notation of SYNTHESIS.md Section 3, Lemma A(b) of the judge reduces KT* (given GAP G2 of L021 and $n\ge67$) to E1-hubs at a minimal apex, where $G'=H-v-V(F_v)$ may a priori have up to three components (one per outer co-vertex). Theorem 8(a) shows that only the connected case can occur, for every $n\ge16$.
9.2 (Why Case 1 is not closed here.) In Case 1 every elementary reduction tried loses: the hub fold $H^\circ$ gains only $8$ (Theorem 1(c)), deleting $v$ is free ($A(H-v)=A(H)$, $P(H-v)=P(G')$), deleting an NC pair $\{a,b\}$ of $G'$ produces $G'-a-b$ with $A=A(H)+94-35q_{ab}$, but the optimal families of its components meet the boundary edges $Q\cup\{e_c\}$, and each boundary edge met costs 35.
