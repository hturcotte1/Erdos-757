## 0. Setting, notation and basic lemmas

0.1. A 3-graph $H=(V,E)$ has $n=|V|$ vertices and $m=|E|$ edges, each edge a 3-subset of $V$; $d(v)$ is the degree; a *pendant* vertex is a vertex of degree 1. For $S\subseteq V$, $H-S$ is the induced subhypergraph on $V\setminus S$ (the edges disjoint from $S$). For $Z\subseteq V$, $E_Z$ is the set of edges meeting $Z$ and $e(Z)=|E_Z|$. $\tau(F)$ for a set $F$ of edges is the minimum number of vertices meeting every edge of $F$; $\tau(H)=\tau(E)$. Distance is distance in the 2-section (two vertices are adjacent iff they lie in a common edge); $B_r(y)$ is the set of vertices at distance at most $r$ from $y$.

0.2. $C$ is the class of 3-graphs that are linear (two edges share at most one vertex), $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$ is not a subhypergraph) and satisfy $e_{\rm ind}(S)\le|S|-2$ for every $S\subseteq V$ with $|S|\ge2$, where $e_{\rm ind}(S)$ is the number of edges contained in $S$. Each of the three conditions passes to every subhypergraph (fewer edges, the same or fewer vertices in every set), so $C$ is closed under $H\mapsto H-S$ and under deleting edges.

0.3. $\mathrm{slack}(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$. I11 for $H$ means $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge0$. For $Z\subseteq V$ and a vertex set $T$ meeting every edge of $E_Z$ put $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=3|Z|+2e(Z)-11|T|$, and $\mathrm{value}(Z)=3|Z|+2e(Z)-11\tau(E_Z)$ (the maximum over $T$). A *(Red) pair* is $(Z,T)$ with $\emptyset\ne Z\ne V$, $T$ meeting every edge of $E_Z$, and $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)\ge0$. **Conjecture RU**: every connected $H\in C$ with $\Delta(H)\le3$ and $n\ge4$ has a (Red) pair.

0.4. A *minimal counterexample* is an $H\in C$ with $\mathrm{slack}(H)<0$ such that every member of $C$ with fewer vertices satisfies I11. Throughout, $s:=\mathrm{slack}(H)$.

**Lemma 0.1 (additivity).** If $H$ is the disjoint union of $H_1$ and $H_2$ then $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$ and $\mathrm{slack}(H)=\mathrm{slack}(H_1)+\mathrm{slack}(H_2)$. An isolated vertex has slack 3, a single edge has slack $9+2-11=0$.
*Proof.* A transversal of $H$ is the union of transversals of $H_1$ and $H_2$, and conversely; $n$ and $m$ add. The two values are direct evaluations. ∎

**Lemma 0.2 (slack identity).** For every $Z\subseteq V$ and every $T$ meeting all edges of $E_Z$,
$$\mathrm{slack}(H)=\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)+\mathrm{value}_T(Z)+11g,\qquad g:=\tau(H-Z)+|T|-\tau(H)\ \ge0 .$$
In particular $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)+\mathrm{value}_T(Z)$.
*Proof.* (1) $H-Z$ has $n-|Z|$ vertices and $m-e(Z)$ edges (the edges not meeting $Z$). (2) Hence $\mathrm{slack}(H)-\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)=3|Z|+2e(Z)-11\tau(H)+11\tau(H-Z)=\mathrm{value}_T(Z)+11(|T|+\tau(H-Z)-\tau(H))$. (3) $g\ge0$: every edge of $H$ either meets $Z$ (then it meets $T$) or is an edge of $H-Z$ (then it meets a minimum transversal of $H-Z$), so $T$ together with a minimum transversal of $H-Z$ is a transversal of $H$ of size $|T|+\tau(H-Z)$. ∎

**Lemma 0.3 (minimal counterexample, basic facts).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample. Then (a) $H$ is connected; (b) $H$ has at least two edges and $n\ge5$; (c) $H$ has no (Red) pair; (d) $\Delta(H)\le3$.
*Proof.* (a) If $H$ had two or more components, each would be a member of $C$ (0.2) with fewer vertices, hence of slack $\ge0$, and by Lemma 0.1 $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge0$. (b) A single vertex has slack 3 and a single edge slack 0 (Lemma 0.1); $H$ connected with at least two edges has two edges sharing exactly one vertex (connected and linear), so $n\ge5$. (c) If $(Z,T)$ were a (Red) pair, $H-Z$ is a member of $C$ with between 1 and $n-1$ vertices, so $\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)\ge0$ and Lemma 0.2 gives $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge0$. (d) If $d(v)\ge4$, then $(Z,T)=(\{v\},\{v\})$ has $\mathrm{value}=3+2d(v)-11\ge0$ and $\{v\}\ne V$ by (b): a (Red) pair, contradicting (c). ∎

**Lemma 0.4 (U1, U2: vertices in minimum transversals).** Let $F\in C$.
(U1) If $x$ is a vertex with $d_F(x)\ge1$, I11 holds for $F-x$, and $\mathrm{slack}(F)<3+2d_F(x)$, then some minimum transversal of $F$ contains $x$ (equivalently $\tau(F-x)=\tau(F)-1$).
(U2) If $x\ne x'$ are vertices, $E_{x,x'}$ is the set of edges of $F$ containing $x$ or $x'$, I11 holds for $F-\{x,x'\}$, and $\mathrm{slack}(F)<2|E_{x,x'}|-5$, then some minimum transversal of $F$ contains both $x$ and $x'$.
*Proof.* (U1) Always $\tau(F)-1\le\tau(F-x)\le\tau(F)$ (add $x$ to a transversal of $F-x$; restrict a transversal of $F$). If $\tau(F-x)=\tau(F)$ then $\mathrm{slack}(F-x)=3(n-1)+2(m-d_F(x))-11\tau(F)=\mathrm{slack}(F)-3-2d_F(x)<0$, contradicting I11 for $F-x$. So $\tau(F-x)=\tau(F)-1$, and a minimum transversal of $F-x$ plus $x$ is a minimum transversal of $F$ containing $x$. (U2) $\tau(F-\{x,x'\})\ge\tau(F)-2$. If equality holds, a minimum transversal of $F-\{x,x'\}$ plus $x,x'$ is a minimum transversal of $F$ containing both. Otherwise $\tau(F-\{x,x'\})\ge\tau(F)-1$ and $\mathrm{slack}(F-\{x,x'\})\le3(n-2)+2(m-|E_{x,x'}|)-11(\tau(F)-1)=\mathrm{slack}(F)+5-2|E_{x,x'}|<0$, contradicting I11 for $F-\{x,x'\}$. ∎

**Lemma 0.5 (TP: pendant vertices of tight graphs).** Let $K\in C$ be connected, not a single edge, with $\mathrm{slack}(K)=0$, and suppose I11 holds for every member of $C$ with fewer vertices than $K$. If $x$ is a pendant vertex of $K$ in the edge $\{x,p,q\}$, then $d_K(p)=d_K(q)=2$.
*Proof.* (1) If $d_K(p)=3$: $(Z,T)=(\{x,p\},\{p\})$; $E_Z$ is the set of the three edges at $p$ (the only edge at $x$ contains $p$), so $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=6+6-11=1$; $Z\ne V(K)$ since $p$ has neighbours outside $\{x,q\}$. Lemma 0.2 and I11 for $K-Z$ give $\mathrm{slack}(K)\ge1$, a contradiction. (2) If $d_K(p)=1$: if also $d_K(q)=1$, the edge $\{x,p,q\}$ is a component, so $K$ is a single edge (connected), excluded. If $d_K(q)\ge2$: $(Z,T)=(\{x,p,q\},\{q\})$, $E_Z$ = the edges at $q$, $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=9+2d_K(q)-11\ge2$, $Z\ne V(K)$ since $q$ has a second edge, and Lemma 0.2 gives $\mathrm{slack}(K)\ge2$, a contradiction. (3) So $d_K(p)=2$, and by symmetry $d_K(q)=2$. ∎

**Remark 0.6.** By L016 (K61), I11 holds on $C$ for $n\le27$, so a minimal counterexample has $n\ge28$. This is used only in Remark 5.4 and in Step 8.4 of Theorem 8.

**Lemma 0.7 ($F_7$ recognition).** A linear 3-graph with 7 vertices and 5 edges, one vertex $v$ of degree 3 and six vertices of degree 2, is isomorphic to $F_7$.
*Proof.* The three edges at $v$ are $\{v,a_1,a_2\},\{v,b_1,b_2\},\{v,c_1,c_2\}$ with the six other vertices distinct (linearity). Each of them has exactly one further edge, so the remaining two edges contain exactly these six vertices, each once: they partition $\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2,c_1,c_2\}$. By linearity neither contains two vertices of the same edge at $v$, so each takes one vertex from each pair; relabelling, they are $\{a_1,b_1,c_1\}$ and $\{a_2,b_2,c_2\}$. The map $v\mapsto0$, $a_1\mapsto1$, $a_2\mapsto2$, $b_1\mapsto3$, $b_2\mapsto4$, $c_1\mapsto5$, $c_2\mapsto6$ sends the five edges to $012,034,056,135,246$, i.e. to $F_7$. ∎ (This is also the test used by `hg.has_f7`, which looks for 5-edge subsets on 7 vertices with degrees $3,2^6$.)

## 1. Theorem 1 (the closure form of (Red))

**Theorem 1.** Let $H$ be a 3-graph without isolated vertices and with $n\ge2$. For $T\subseteq V$ put $\mathrm{cl}(T)=\{v\in V:\text{every edge at }v\text{ meets }T\}$ and $w(T)=3|\mathrm{cl}(T)|+2|E_T|-11|T|$. Then $H$ has a (Red) pair if and only if there is a nonempty $T$ with either (i) $\mathrm{cl}(T)\ne V$ and $w(T)\ge0$, or (ii) $\mathrm{cl}(T)=V$ and $w(T)\ge3$. Moreover (ii) holds for some $T$ if and only if $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge3$. In particular every $H$ with $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge3$ has a (Red) pair, and a (Red)-free $H$ has $\mathrm{slack}(H)\le2$.

*Proof.*
1.1. $T\subseteq\mathrm{cl}(T)$ (every edge at $t\in T$ contains $t$), and $E_{\mathrm{cl}(T)}=E_T$: an edge meeting $T$ meets $\mathrm{cl}(T)\supseteq T$, and an edge meeting $\mathrm{cl}(T)$ in $v$ is an edge at $v$, which meets $T$ by definition of $\mathrm{cl}$.
1.2. (i) ⇒ (Red): put $Z=\mathrm{cl}(T)$. $Z\supseteq T\ne\emptyset$ and $Z\ne V$. By 1.1, $T$ meets every edge of $E_Z=E_T$ and $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=w(T)\ge0$.
1.3. (ii) ⇒ (Red): every edge has a vertex, which lies in $\mathrm{cl}(T)=V$, so every edge meets $T$: $T$ is a transversal and $E_T=E$. Pick a vertex $u$ and put $Z=V\setminus\{u\}$ (nonempty since $n\ge2$). Every edge has 3 vertices, hence meets $Z$, so $E_Z=E$ and $T$ meets all of it; $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=3(n-1)+2m-11|T|=w(T)-3\ge0$.
1.4. (Red) ⇒ (i) or (ii): let $(Z,T)$ be a (Red) pair. Every edge at $z\in Z$ lies in $E_Z$ and so meets $T$; hence $Z\subseteq\mathrm{cl}(T)$ and $E_Z\subseteq E_T$. $Z$ is nonempty and has an edge (no isolated vertices), so $E_Z\ne\emptyset$ and $T\ne\emptyset$. If $\mathrm{cl}(T)\ne V$: $w(T)\ge3|Z|+2e(Z)-11|T|\ge0$, which is (i). If $\mathrm{cl}(T)=V$: $T$ is a transversal (as in 1.3), $|E_T|=m$, and since $|Z|\le n-1$ and $e(Z)\le m$, $w(T)=3n+2m-11|T|\ge3|Z|+2e(Z)+3-11|T|\ge3$, which is (ii).
1.5. (ii) ⇔ $\mathrm{slack}\ge3$: if $\mathrm{cl}(T)=V$ then $T$ is a transversal, $|T|\ge\tau(H)$ and $w(T)=3n+2m-11|T|\le\mathrm{slack}(H)$; so (ii) gives $\mathrm{slack}\ge3$. Conversely a minimum transversal $T$ has $\mathrm{cl}(T)=V$ and $w(T)=\mathrm{slack}(H)$. ∎

## 2. Theorem 2 (maximum degree 2: Lemma M)

**Theorem 2.** Let $H$ be a linear 3-graph with $\Delta(H)\le2$, with $n_0$ isolated vertices and $n_2$ vertices of degree 2. Then
$$7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)\ \ge\ 21n_0+n_2,\qquad\text{in particular }11\tau(H)\le3n+2m .$$
(No other hypothesis of class $C$ is needed.) The bound is attained, e.g. by the $(8,5,3)$ member of $C$ whose dual graph is $K_4$ with one edge subdivided (slack 1, $n_2=7$; Step 2.6).

*Proof.*
2.1 (dual graph). Let $G$ be the graph with vertex set $E(H)$ and, for every vertex $x$ of $H$ of degree 2, one edge joining the two edges of $H$ that contain $x$. $G$ has no loops (the two edges at $x$ are distinct) and no parallel edges (two edges of $H$ share at most one vertex). An edge $e$ of $H$ has $d_G(e)$ = number of degree-2 vertices of $H$ in $e\le3$. So $G$ is a simple graph with $\Delta(G)\le3$, $|V(G)|=m$ and $|E(G)|=n_2$.
2.2 ($\tau(H)=m-\nu(G)$, $\nu$ = matching number). (≤) Let $M$ be a maximum matching of $G$. Take, for each $G$-edge of $M$, the corresponding degree-2 vertex of $H$ (it meets both $H$-edges joined by it), and for each $H$-edge not incident with $M$ any one of its vertices. This is a transversal with $|M|+(m-2|M|)=m-\nu(G)$ vertices. (≥) Let $T$ be a minimum transversal; isolated vertices are not in $T$. Let $T_2\subseteq T$ be its degree-2 vertices and $F\subseteq E(G)$ the corresponding $G$-edges; $V(F)$ denotes the set of $G$-vertices incident with $F$. Each $H$-edge contains a vertex of $T$; if it contains no vertex of $T_2$ it contains a vertex of $T\setminus T_2$, and each vertex of $T\setminus T_2$ has degree 1, so lies in exactly one $H$-edge. Hence $m\le|V(F)|+|T|-|T_2|=|V(F)|+|T|-|F|$. In the graph $(V(F),F)$ every component with $k$ vertices has at least $k-1$ edges and at least one edge, so $|V(F)|-|F|\le$ number of components of $(V(F),F)\le\nu(G)$ (one edge from each component is a matching). So $\tau(H)=|T|\ge m-\nu(G)$.
2.3 (counting). Let $n_1$ be the number of pendant vertices. Then $n=n_0+n_1+n_2$ and $3m=n_1+2n_2$ (degree sum). So $3n=3n_0+3(3m-2n_2)+3n_2=3n_0+9m-3n_2$ and, by 2.2,
$\mathrm{slack}(H)=3n_0+9m-3n_2+2m-11m+11\nu(G)=3n_0+11\nu(G)-3n_2$.
2.4 (Edmonds). Let $x\in\mathbb R^{E(G)}$ be the constant vector $x_e=2/7$. (a) For every vertex $u$ of $G$: $\sum_{e\ni u}x_e=2d_G(u)/7\le6/7\le1$. (b) For every $S\subseteq V(G)$ with $|S|=2k+1$, $k\ge1$: the subgraph $G[S]$ has maximum degree $\le3$, so $2|E(G[S])|\le3|S|=6k+3$, and since $2|E(G[S])|$ is even, $|E(G[S])|\le3k+1$; for $k=1$ moreover $|E(G[S])|\le3$ (a simple graph on 3 vertices). Hence $\sum_{e\in E(G[S])}x_e\le\frac27\cdot3=\frac67\le1=k$ for $k=1$, and $\le\frac27(3k+1)\le k$ for $k\ge2$ (as $6k+2\le7k$). (c) By Edmonds' theorem the matching polytope of $G$ is exactly $\{x\ge0:\ x(\delta(u))\le1\ \forall u,\ x(E(G[S]))\le\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor\ \forall S\text{ odd}\}$; by (a), (b) our $x$ lies in it, so it is a convex combination of incidence vectors of matchings, and some matching $M$ has $|M|\ge\sum_ex_e=\frac27|E(G)|$. Thus $\nu(G)\ge\frac27n_2$.
2.5. By 2.3 and 2.4: $7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)=21n_0+77\nu(G)-21n_2\ge21n_0+22n_2-21n_2=21n_0+n_2\ge0$.
2.6 (equality). $G=K_4$ with one edge subdivided has 5 vertices, 7 edges, $\nu=2$; the corresponding $H$ (5 edges, 7 vertices of degree 2, one pendant vertex on the edge of $G$-degree 2) has $n=8$, $m=5$, $\tau=5-2=3$, slack $24+10-33=1$ and $n_2=7$, so $7\cdot1=7=n_2$. It is the $\Delta\le2$ class of cell $(8,5,3)$ (experiments/prover/L013/cells.tar.gz, cell_8_5_3_D3: degrees $1^12^7$). ∎

**Corollary 2.1.** A minimal counterexample has a vertex of degree 3, and $\Delta=3$.
*Proof.* $\Delta\le3$ by Lemma 0.3(d); $\Delta\le2$ is impossible by Theorem 2 ($H$ is linear and slack would be $\ge0$). ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (values when $\Delta\le2$).** Let $H$ be linear with $\Delta(H)\le2$, without isolated vertices, with dual graph $G$ (2.1). For a nonempty $Z\subseteq V(H)$ let $W=E_Z\subseteq V(G)$, let $E_G(W)$ be the set of $G$-edges with at least one end in $W$, and $G[W]$ the induced subgraph. Then $\mathrm{value}(Z)\le11\nu(G[W])-3|E_G(W)|$.
*Proof.* (1) The edge set $W$, viewed as a 3-graph on $V(H)$, has maximum degree $\le2$ and dual graph $G[W]$ (its degree-2 vertices are exactly the $G$-edges with both ends in $W$), so by 2.2, $\tau(E_Z)=|W|-\nu(G[W])$. (2) Every $z\in Z$ has all its edges in $W$: either $d(z)=2$ and $z$ is a $G$-edge with both ends in $W$, or $d(z)=1$ and $z$ is a pendant vertex of an $H$-edge of $W$. An $H$-edge $e$ has exactly $3-d_G(e)$ pendant vertices. So $|Z|\le|E(G[W])|+\sum_{e\in W}(3-d_G(e))$. (3) $\sum_{e\in W}d_G(e)=2|E(G[W])|+|\partial W|$, where $\partial W$ is the set of $G$-edges with exactly one end in $W$, and $|E_G(W)|=|E(G[W])|+|\partial W|$. (4) So $\mathrm{value}(Z)\le3|E(G[W])|+9|W|-6|E(G[W])|-3|\partial W|+2|W|-11|W|+11\nu(G[W])=11\nu(G[W])-3|E_G(W)|$. ∎

## 3. Theorem 3 (pendant vertices: the local catalogue) [computer-assisted]

Call a pendant vertex $y$ of $H$ *$r$-reducible* if $H$ has a (Red) pair $(Z,T)$ with $Z\subseteq B_r(y)$.

**Theorem 3.** Let $H$ be a connected 3-graph that is linear with $\Delta(H)\le3$ and $n\ge5$ (in particular any connected member of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$, $n\ge5$), and let $y$ be a pendant vertex, in the edge $e_0=\{y,a,b\}$.
(a) If $y$ is not 1-reducible, then $d(a)=d(b)=2$.
(b) If $y$ is not 2-reducible, let $A$, $B$ be the second edges at $a$ and $b$. Then exactly one of the following holds.
 (P-tree-2) $A\cap B=\emptyset$; the four vertices of $(A\cup B)\setminus\{a,b\}$ have degree 2; their four second edges are pairwise distinct and each of them meets $B_2(y)$ in exactly one vertex.
 (P-tri-2) $A\cap B=\{c\}$ with $d(c)=3$; the vertices $a'\in A\setminus\{a,c\}$ and $b'\in B\setminus\{b,c\}$ have degree 2; the third edge $C$ at $c$, the second edge $A'$ at $a'$ and the second edge $B'$ at $b'$ are pairwise distinct and each meets $B_2(y)$ in exactly one vertex.
(c) If $y$ is not 3-reducible, then either (P-tree-3): (P-tree-2) holds and the 8 further vertices of the four second edges are pairwise distinct, all of degree 2, and their 8 second edges are pairwise distinct and each meets $B_3(y)$ in exactly one vertex; or (P-tri-3): (P-tri-2) holds, the six vertices of $(C\cup A'\cup B')\setminus\{c,a',b'\}$ are pairwise distinct, the four in $A'\cup B'$ have degree 2, the two in $C$ have degree 2 or 3 (all four combinations occur), and all the further edges at these six vertices are pairwise distinct and each meets $B_3(y)$ in exactly one vertex.
(d) The lists cannot be shortened at radius 2: both types occur as pendants that are not 2-reducible in census graphs (Step 3.6: P-tree-2 92 times and P-tri-2 461 times at $n=13$). By Theorem 4, P-tree-$r$ occurs as a pendant that is not $r$-reducible for every $r$. Whether P-tri-3 occurs at a pendant that is not 3-reducible of an actual graph was not checked (it is a leaf of the enumeration, i.e. not excluded).

*Hand proof of (a) and (b)* (independent of the program; every (Red) pair below has $Z\subseteq B_2(y)$ and $Z\ne V$ because $|Z|\le5<n$ or because $B_2(y)$ has a vertex outside $Z$).
3.0.1 ((a)). If $d(a)=3$: $Z=\{y,a\}$, $T=\{a\}$, $E_Z$ = the three edges at $a$, value $6+6-11=1$. If $d(a)=1$: $Z=\{y,a,b\}$, $T=\{b\}$, $E_Z$ = the edges at $b$, value $9+2d(b)-11\ge0$. Both $Z\subseteq B_1(y)$. So $d(a)=d(b)=2$ if $y$ is not 1-reducible.
3.0.2 (setting for (b)). Let $y$ be not 2-reducible; by (a) $d(a)=d(b)=2$; $A=\{a,a_1,a_2\}$ and $B=\{b,b_1,b_2\}$ are the second edges at $a$ and $b$; $b\notin A$ and $a\notin B$ (else $A$ or $B$ would share two vertices with $e_0$), $y\notin A\cup B$ ($d(y)=1$), $A\ne B$ (else $a,b\in A$) and $|A\cap B|\le1$ (linearity). No vertex $u\in(A\cup B)\setminus\{a,b\}$ is pendant: for $u\in A$, $Z=\{y,a,u\}$, $T=\{a\}$, $E_Z=\{e_0,A\}$, value $9+4-11=2$ (symmetrically for $u\in B$).
3.0.3 (case $A\cap B=\emptyset$). (i) If $d(a_1)=3$, with edges $A,X,Y$: $Z=\{y,a,b,a_1\}$, $T=\{b,a_1\}$; $E_Z=\{e_0,A,B,X,Y\}$, five distinct edges ($X,Y\ne e_0$ as $a_1\notin e_0$; $X,Y\ne B$ as $a_1\notin B$); $T$ meets all of them; value $12+10-22=0$. 3.1 (split configuration). For $r\ge0$ let $K_r$ be the set of edges of $H$ meeting $B_r(y)$; its vertices are $B_r(y)$ and the *boundary*, the vertices at distance $r+1$ that lie in edges of $K_r$. The *split configuration* $K_r^{\rm sp}$ is obtained from $K_r$ by replacing each boundary vertex lying in $k\ge2$ edges of $K_r$ by $k$ distinct new vertices, one in each of those edges. In $K_r^{\rm sp}$ every vertex of $B_r(y)$ ("processed") has all its $H$-edges, every boundary vertex lies in exactly one edge, and $K_r^{\rm sp}$ is linear with $\Delta\le3$.
3.2 (monotonicity). For $\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq B_r(y)$: $\mathrm{value}_H(Z)\ge\mathrm{value}_{K_r^{\rm sp}}(Z)$, where the right side is computed in $K_r^{\rm sp}$. Proof: every edge at a vertex of $Z$ lies in $K_r$, so $E_Z$ is the same set of edges in $H$ and in $K_r^{\rm sp}$ (splitting renames boundary vertices and never makes two edges equal); $|Z|$ and $e(Z)$ agree; the map that re-identifies the split copies sends every transversal of $E_Z$ in $K_r^{\rm sp}$ to a transversal of $E_Z$ in $H$ of at most the same size, so $\tau_H(E_Z)\le\tau_{K^{\rm sp}_r}(E_Z)$. Moreover $Z\ne V(H)$ whenever $V(K_r^{\rm sp})\ne B_r(y)$ (a boundary vertex is a vertex of $H$ outside $B_r(y)$). Consequence: if some $\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq B_r(y)$ has $\mathrm{value}_{K_r^{\rm sp}}(Z)\ge0$, and either $K_r^{\rm sp}$ has a boundary vertex or $Z\ne B_r(y)$, then $y$ is $r$-reducible.

## 4. Theorem 4 (Conjecture RU is not a local statement)

**Theorem 4.** For every integer $R\ge1$ there is a connected $H\in C$ with $\Delta(H)=2$ and exactly one pendant vertex $p$ such that every (Red) pair $(Z,T)$ of $H$ has $|Z|>R$. In $H$, for every $r$ with $2r+4\le g$ ($g$ the girth chosen below), the split configuration $K_r^{\rm sp}(p)$ is the configuration P-tree-$r$ (the complete binary hypertree of depth $r$ with all processed vertices but the root of degree 2), so P-tree-$r$ is not $r$-reducible for any $r$. $H$ does have (Red) pairs (Theorem 1, since $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge n_2/7\ge3$), but only global ones.

*Proof.*
4.1. Fix $R\ge1$ and $r\ge0$ and let $g\ge\max(2R+2,\,2r+4,\,22)$. By Erdős–Sachs there is a 3-regular graph of girth $\ge g$; take a connected component $G_0$ (3-regular, girth $\ge g$). Subdivide one edge $uv$ of $G_0$ by a new vertex $g_0$. The result $G$ is connected and simple, $d_G(g_0)=2$, all other degrees are 3, and its girth is $\ge g$ (a cycle of $G$ avoiding $g_0$ is a cycle of $G_0$; a cycle through $g_0$ uses $u g_0 v$ and corresponds to a cycle of $G_0$ through $uv$, one longer).
4.2. Define $H$: vertex set $E(G)\cup\{p\}$ ($p$ new); for each vertex $w\ne g_0$ of $G$ the edge $\delta(w)$ (its three incident $G$-edges); for $g_0$ the edge $\delta(g_0)\cup\{p\}$. Then $H$ is 3-uniform; every $G$-edge lies in exactly the two $H$-edges of its endpoints (degree 2) and $p$ has degree 1; $H$ is connected since $G$ is; two $H$-edges share exactly the $G$-edges joining their two vertices, at most one (simple), so $H$ is linear. $G$ is the dual graph of $H$ in the sense of 2.1.
4.3 ($H\in C$). Linear: 4.2. $F_7$-free: $F_7$ contains three edges $012,034,135$ that pairwise meet in three distinct vertices; three $H$-edges pairwise meeting in distinct vertices correspond to three vertices of $G$ pairwise joined by distinct $G$-edges, a triangle, impossible as $g\ge4$. $e_{\rm ind}(S)\le|S|-2$: let $S\subseteq V(H)$, $|S|\ge2$, and $W$ the set of vertices of $G$ whose $H$-edge lies in $S$, so $e_{\rm ind}(S)=|W|$; if $W=\emptyset$ we are done. The union of these $|W|$ edges has $3|W|-|E(G[W])|$ vertices (a $G$-edge with both ends in $W$ lies in two of them, $p$ in at most one), all in $S$. So it suffices that $|E(G[W])|\le2|W|-2$ for every nonempty $W\subseteq V(G)$: for $|W|\le3$, $G[W]$ has no cycle (girth $\ge4$), so $|E(G[W])|\le|W|-1\le2|W|-2$; for $|W|\ge4$, $|E(G[W])|\le3|W|/2\le2|W|-2$.
4.4 (forest lemma; no small (Red) pair). Let $\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq V(H)$ and $W=E_Z$ (a nonempty set of vertices of $G$, since every vertex of $H$ has an edge). By Lemma 2.2, $\mathrm{value}(Z)\le11\nu(G[W])-3|E_G(W)|$. **If $G[W]$ is a forest, then $\mathrm{value}(Z)<0$**: indeed $|E(G[W])|\le|W|-1$ and $\nu(G[W])\le|W|/2$; since only $g_0$ has degree 2, $\sum_{w\in W}d_G(w)\ge3|W|-1$, and $|E_G(W)|=\sum_{w\in W}d_G(w)-|E(G[W])|\ge3|W|-1-(|W|-1)=2|W|$; so $\mathrm{value}(Z)\le\frac{11}2|W|-6|W|<0$. Now let $(Z,T)$ be a (Red) pair: $\mathrm{value}(Z)\ge\mathrm{value}_T(Z)\ge0$, so $G[W]$ contains a cycle, so $|W|\ge g$. Every vertex of $Z$ lies in at most 2 edges, so $|W|\le2|Z|$, and $|Z|\ge g/2>R$.
4.5 (the local configuration). Two $H$-vertices that are $G$-edges are adjacent in $H$ iff they share an endpoint in $G$, and $p$ is adjacent exactly to the two $G$-edges at $g_0$; by induction on $j$, a $G$-edge $f$ has $H$-distance $j\ge1$ from $p$ iff the nearer endpoint of $f$ has $G$-distance $j-1$ from $g_0$. Let $V_r$ be the set of vertices of $G$ at $G$-distance $\le r$ from $g_0$. The edges of $K_r$ (the $H$-edges meeting $B_r(p)$) are exactly the $H$-edges $\delta(w)$ (and $\delta(g_0)\cup\{p\}$) for $w\in V_r$: an $H$-vertex $f\in B_r(p)$ has an endpoint in $V_{r-1}$ and both endpoints in $V_r$. A cycle of $G$ all of whose vertices lie in $V_r$ has length $\le2r+1<g$; so $G[V_r]$ is a tree (the BFS tree from $g_0$), every $w\in V_r\setminus\{g_0\}$ has exactly one neighbour closer to $g_0$, and no $G$-edge joins two vertices at distance exactly $r$. Consequently $K_r^{\rm sp}(p)$ is the complete binary hypertree of depth $r$: $\delta(g_0)\cup\{p\}$ at the root, and every $\delta(w)$, $w\in V_{r-1}\setminus\{g_0\}$, consists of the $G$-edge towards $g_0$ and two $G$-edges away from it; every processed vertex other than $p$ has degree 2. This is P-tree-$r$. For $\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq B_r(p)$, $W=E_Z$ corresponds to a subset of $V_r$, so $G[W]$ is a subforest of the tree $G[V_r]$ and $\mathrm{value}(Z)<0$ by 4.4: $p$ is not $r$-reducible.
4.6 ((Red) pairs exist). $n_2(H)=|E(G)|\ge g$, so by Theorem 2, $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge n_2/7\ge3$ (as $g\ge21$) and Theorem 1 gives a (Red) pair ($Z=V\setminus\{u\}$, $T$ a minimum transversal). ∎

**Remark 4.1 (degree 3).** Adding to $H$ one edge $\{x_1,x_2,q\}$ with $q$ new and $x_1,x_2$ two degree-2 vertices at distance $>4$ from each other and from $p$ gives $H'\in C$ with $\Delta=3$ (linear; $e(S)\le|S|-2$: for $S\ni q$, $e_{H'}(S)\le e_H(S\setminus q)+1\le|S|-2$; $F_7$-free since $q$ has degree 1 and every vertex of $F_7$ has degree $\ge2$ in $F_7$) in which $K_r^{\rm sp}(p)$ is unchanged. So the non-locality at pendant vertices persists in graphs with degree-3 vertices. ($H'$ has the small (Red) pair $(\{q,x_1\},\{x_1\})$ elsewhere; the statement concerns the local analysis at $p$.)

## 5. Theorem 5 (pendant vertices in a minimal counterexample)

**Theorem 5.** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample with a pendant vertex $y$, in the edge $e_0=\{y,a,b\}$. Then:
(i) $d(a)=d(b)=2$; let $A=\{a,a_1,a_2\}$ and $B=\{b,b_1,b_2\}$ be the second edges at $a$ and $b$; no vertex of $(A\cup B)\setminus\{a,b\}$ is pendant.
(ii) $\tau(H-y)=\tau(H)-1$, and no minimum transversal of $H-y$ contains $a$ or $b$.
(iii) $s=\mathrm{slack}(H)=-1$. $K_a:=H-\{y,a\}$ and $K_b:=H-\{y,b\}$ are connected, $\tau(K_a)=\tau(K_b)=\tau(H)-1$, and $\mathrm{slack}(K_a)=\mathrm{slack}(K_b)=0$.
(iv) No minimum transversal of $K_a$ contains $b$ together with $a_1$ or $a_2$ (and symmetrically for $K_b$).
(v) If $A\cap B=\emptyset$, the four vertices $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$ have degree 2. If $A\cap B=\{c\}$, then $d(c)=3$ and the two other vertices of $(A\cup B)\setminus\{a,b\}$ have degree 2.
(vi) $K_a$ is a connected member of $C$ with $\mathrm{slack}=0$, $n-2$ vertices, at least 5 vertices and at least two pendant vertices ($b$, and every vertex of $A\setminus(\{a\}\cup B)$); likewise $K_b$.
(vii) If $A\cap B=\emptyset$, every vertex of the second edges $A_1,A_2,B_1,B_2$ at $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$ has degree 2. If $A\cap B=\{c\}$ with $A=\{a,c,a'\}$, $B=\{b,c,b'\}$, every vertex of the second edges at $a'$ and at $b'$ has degree 2.

*Proof.* $n\ge5$, $H$ connected, $\Delta\le3$ and (Red)-free by Lemma 0.3. Every member of $C$ with fewer than $n$ vertices satisfies I11 (0.4); this is used for $H-Z$ with $Z\neq\emptyset$ in each step.
5.1 ($d(a)\ne3$). If $d(a)=3$, $(Z,T)=(\{y,a\},\{a\})$: the only edge at $y$ contains $a$, so $E_Z$ = the three edges at $a$ and $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=6+6-11=1\ge0$; $Z\ne V$ as $n\ge5$. A (Red) pair, contradicting Lemma 0.3(c).
5.2 ($d(a)\ne1$). If $d(a)=1$, $(Z,T)=(\{y,a,b\},\{b\})$: $E_Z$ = the edges at $b$ (the edges at $y$ and at $a$ are $e_0\ni b$), $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)=9+2d(b)-11\ge0$; $Z\ne V$ as $n\ge5$. Contradiction. So $d(a)=2$, and by symmetry $d(b)=2$.
5.3 (structure of $A$, $B$). $A\ne e_0$; $b\notin A$ and $a\notin B$ (else $A$ or $B$ shares two vertices with $e_0$); $y\notin A\cup B$ ($d(y)=1$); $A\ne B$ (else $a,b\in A$); $|A\cap B|\le1$ (linearity). If $a_1$ were pendant: $(Z,T)=(\{y,a,a_1\},\{a\})$, $E_Z=\{e_0,A\}$, value $9+4-11=2\ge0$, $Z\ne V$: contradiction. Same for $a_2,b_1,b_2$. This proves (i).
5.4 ((ii)). $H-y\in C$ has $n-1$ vertices and $m-1$ edges ($e_0$ is the only edge at $y$). $\tau(H)-1\le\tau(H-y)\le\tau(H)$. If $\tau(H-y)=\tau(H)$ then $s=\mathrm{slack}(H-y)+3+2\ge5$ (I11 for $H-y$), a contradiction. So $\tau(H-y)=\tau(H)-1$. A minimum transversal of $H-y$ containing $a$ or $b$ meets $e_0$, hence is a transversal of $H$ of size $\tau(H)-1$: impossible.
5.5 ($\tau(K_a)$, $s=-1$). $K_a=(H-y)-a$. $\tau(H-y)-1\le\tau(K_a)\le\tau(H-y)$. If $\tau(K_a)=\tau(H-y)-1$, a minimum transversal of $K_a$ plus $a$ is a transversal of $H-y$ (the only edge of $H-y$ at $a$ is $A$) of size $\tau(H-y)$ containing $a$, contradicting (ii). So $\tau(K_a)=\tau(H)-1$. $K_a$ has $n-2$ vertices and $m-2$ edges ($e_0$, $A$ removed; no other edge contains $y$ or $a$), so $\mathrm{slack}(K_a)=3(n-2)+2(m-2)-11(\tau(H)-1)=s+1$. I11 for $K_a$ gives $s\ge-1$, so $s=-1$ and $\mathrm{slack}(K_a)=0$. Symmetrically for $K_b$.
5.6 ((iv)). If a minimum transversal $T$ of $K_a$ (size $\tau(H)-1$) contained $b$ and $a_i$, then $T$ meets $e_0$ (via $b$), $A$ (via $a_i$) and all edges of $K_a$; every edge of $H$ is $e_0$, $A$ or an edge of $K_a$; so $T$ would be a transversal of $H$ of size $\tau(H)-1$. Impossible.
5.7 ($K_a$ connected). Let $Q$ be a component of $K_a$. $H$ is connected, so some edge of $H$ meets both $V(Q)$ and $\{y,a\}$; the only edges meeting $\{y,a\}$ are $e_0$ and $A$, so $V(Q)$ contains $b$ or $a_1$ or $a_2$. In $K_a$: $d_{K_a}(b)=1$ (edge $B$) and $d_{K_a}(a_i)=d(a_i)-1\ge1$ (by (i), $d(a_i)\ge2$; the edges at $a_i$ other than $A$ avoid $y$ and $a$). Every component of $K_a$ has slack $\ge0$ (I11) and the slacks add to $0$ (Lemma 0.1), so every component has slack 0. Suppose $b$ and $a_i$ lie in different components $Q_b$, $Q_i$. By (U1) (slack $0<3+2\cdot1$; I11 for $Q-x$ by minimality) $Q_b$ has a minimum transversal containing $b$ and $Q_i$ one containing $a_i$; together with minimum transversals of the other components they form a minimum transversal of $K_a$ (Lemma 0.1) containing $b$ and $a_i$, contradicting (iv). So $b,a_1,a_2$ lie in one component, and since every component contains one of them, $K_a$ is connected. Same for $K_b$. This completes (iii).
5.8 (degree-3 vertices of $A\cup B$). Suppose $d(a_1)=3$ and $a_1\notin B$. In $K_a$, $d_{K_a}(b)=1$ (its only edge is $B$), $d_{K_a}(a_1)=2$, and $b,a_1$ share no edge of $K_a$ (the only edge at $b$ is $B\not\ni a_1$). So $|E_{b,a_1}|=3$ and $\mathrm{slack}(K_a)=0<2\cdot3-5$; by (U2) (I11 for $K_a-\{b,a_1\}$ by minimality) some minimum transversal of $K_a$ contains $b$ and $a_1$, contradicting (iv). Hence a degree-3 vertex among $a_1,a_2$ lies in $B$; symmetrically (using $K_b$) a degree-3 vertex among $b_1,b_2$ lies in $A$. With (i) (no pendants) this gives the degree statements of (v) except $d(c)$.
5.9 ($d(c)=3$). Let $A\cap B=\{c\}$, $A=\{a,c,a'\}$, $B=\{b,c,b'\}$, and suppose $d(c)=2$ (its edges are $A$, $B$). ## 6. Theorem 6 (degree-2 and degree-3 vertices in a minimal counterexample)

**Theorem 6.** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample, $s=\mathrm{slack}(H)$.
(a) For every vertex $v$: $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)-1$ and $\mathrm{slack}(H-v)=s+11-3-2d(v)$. In particular a degree-3 vertex gives $\mathrm{slack}(H-v)=s+2\ge0$, so $s\in\{-1,-2\}$ (a degree-3 vertex exists by Corollary 2.1).
(b) (degree 3) Let $d(v)=3$ with edges $\{v\}\cup P_i$, $P_i$ pairwise disjoint pairs ($i=1,2,3$). No vertex of $P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3$ is pendant; the components of $H-v$ have slack $\ge0$ summing to $s+2\le1$ (all tight except at most one of slack 1); every component contains a vertex of $P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3$; no minimum transversal of $H-v$ meets all three pairs; and the bipartite graph between the three edges at $v$ and the components of $H-v$ ($e$ adjacent to $Q$ when $e$ meets $V(Q)$) has no matching of size 3, so $H-v$ has at most 3 components.
(b') (gadget lemma) In (b), if $H-v$ is disconnected then no component contains two of the pairs $P_1,P_2,P_3$; consequently $H-v$ has at most 2 components, and if it has 2 components, each of them contains at most one of the pairs entirely.
(c) (degree 2) Let $d(x)=2$ with edges $\{x,a,b\}$, $\{x,c,d\}$. No minimum transversal of $H-x$ meets both $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$; $\mathrm{slack}(H-x)=s+4\in\{2,3\}$. If none of $a,b,c,d$ is pendant, $H-x$ is connected. If $s=-2$ and $z\in\{a,b\}$, $z'\in\{c,d\}$ both have degree 3, then $z$ and $z'$ lie in a common edge of $H-x$.

*Proof.*
6.1 ((a)). $\tau(H)-1\le\tau(H-v)\le\tau(H)$. If $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)$ then $s=\mathrm{slack}(H-v)+3+2d(v)\ge3$ (I11 for $H-v$), a contradiction. So $\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)-1$ and $\mathrm{slack}(H-v)=3(n-1)+2(m-d(v))-11(\tau(H)-1)=s+8-2d(v)$. For $d(v)=3$, I11 for $H-v$ gives $s+2\ge0$; with $s\le-1$, $s\in\{-1,-2\}$.
6.2 ((b), pendants). If $z\in P_1$ were pendant, $(\{z,v\},\{v\})$ has value $6+6-11=1$ and $\{z,v\}\ne V$: a (Red) pair, contradicting Lemma 0.3(c).
6.3 ((b), components). Slacks are $\ge0$ (I11, fewer vertices) and add to $\mathrm{slack}(H-v)=s+2\le1$ (Lemma 0.1, 6.1). Each component $Q$ is joined in $H$ to $v$ ($H$ connected), through an edge at $v$, so $V(Q)$ meets $P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3$.
6.4 ((b), no common transversal). A minimum transversal of $H-v$ meeting $P_1,P_2,P_3$ meets all three edges at $v$ and all edges of $H-v$, so $\tau(H)\le\tau(H-v)=\tau(H)-1$: impossible.
6.5 ((b), Hall). Suppose the three edges $e_1,e_2,e_3$ at $v$ are matched to distinct components $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$, with $x_i\in e_i\cap V(Q_i)$. $d_{Q_i}(x_i)=d(x_i)-1\ge1$ (6.2) and $\mathrm{slack}(Q_i)\le1<3+2$, so by (U1) $Q_i$ has a minimum transversal containing $x_i$; with minimum transversals of the other components this gives a minimum transversal of $H-v$ meeting $P_1,P_2,P_3$, contradicting 6.4. So no matching of size 3 exists. If $H-v$ had $\ge4$ components, each meeting $P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3$ (6.3): by Hall's theorem some set of $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ edges at $v$ meets fewer than $k$ components; $k=1$ is impossible (every edge meets a component); if $k=2$, two edges meet one component $Q$, so their four co-vertices lie in $Q$ and the remaining $\ge3$ components all meet the third pair, which has 2 vertices: impossible; if $k=3$, all six co-vertices lie in $\le2$ components: impossible with $\ge4$ components. So at most 3 components.
6.5' ((b')). Suppose $H-v$ has $r\ge2$ components and $P_1\cup P_2\subseteq V(F_1)$ for a component $F_1$. Then $P_3\not\subseteq V(F_1)$: otherwise the other components would meet no pair, contradicting 6.3. Let $H_1=H[V(F_1)\cup\{v\}]$ (induced). Its edges are the edges of $F_1$ (an edge of $H$ inside $V(F_1)$ is an edge of the component $F_1$) and $e_1,e_2$ ($e_3\not\subseteq V(F_1)\cup\{v\}$). $H_1\in C$ and $|V(H_1)|=|V(F_1)|+1\le n-1$, so I11 holds for $H_1$. If no minimum transversal of $F_1$ met both $P_1$ and $P_2$, then $\tau(H_1)=\tau(F_1)+1$: indeed $\tau(H_1)\le\tau(F_1)+1$ (add $v$), and a transversal $T$ of $H_1$ with $|T|\le\tau(F_1)$ would either avoid $v$ (then $T\subseteq V(F_1)$ is a minimum transversal of $F_1$ meeting $P_1$ and $P_2$, since it meets $e_1,e_2$) or contain $v$ (then $T\setminus\{v\}$ is a transversal of $F_1$ with fewer than $\tau(F_1)$ vertices). So $\mathrm{slack}(H_1)=\mathrm{slack}(F_1)+3+2\cdot2-11=\mathrm{slack}(F_1)-4\le1-4<0$ (6.3), a contradiction. Hence some minimum transversal $T_1$ of $F_1$ meets $P_1$ and $P_2$. Some other component $F_2$ meets $P_3$ (6.3), in a vertex $x_3$ with $d_{F_2}(x_3)\ge1$ (6.2); by (U1) ($\mathrm{slack}(F_2)\le1<5$) some minimum transversal of $F_2$ contains $x_3$. Together with minimum transversals of the remaining components this is a minimum transversal of $H-v$ meeting $P_1,P_2,P_3$, contradicting 6.4. Consequences: if $r=3$, every edge at $v$ meets a component, and two edges meeting only one common component would have both pairs inside it, which is excluded; so any two edges meet at least two components and all three meet three, Hall's condition holds and 6.5 gives a contradiction; so $r\le2$. If $r=2$, the statement about the pairs is the first sentence.
6.6 ((c)). The first two statements: as 6.4 (a transversal of $H-x$ meeting both pairs is a transversal of $H$) and 6.1 with $d=2$. Connectedness: every component $Q$ of $H-x$ meets $\{a,b,c,d\}$ (as 6.3). If none of $a,b,c,d$ is pendant, each has degree $\ge1$ in $H-x$; each component has slack $\le s+4\le3<5$; if $z\in\{a,b\}$ and $z'\in\{c,d\}$ were in different components, (U1) in each and Lemma 0.1 would give a minimum transversal of $H-x$ containing $z$ and $z'$, contradicting the first statement. So $a$ and $b$ are each in the component of $c$, and every component contains one of $a,b,c,d$: $H-x$ is connected. Last statement: if $s=-2$, $\mathrm{slack}(H-x)=2$; $d_{H-x}(z)=d_{H-x}(z')=2$; if they shared no edge of $H-x$, $|E_{z,z'}|=4$ and $2<2\cdot4-5$, so by (U2) a minimum transversal of $H-x$ contains $z$ and $z'$, contradicting the first statement. ∎

**Proposition 6.2 (the case $s=-2$ under Conjecture P2).** Assume Conjecture P2 (Corollary 5.2). If $H$ is a minimal counterexample with $s=-2$, then $H-v$ is connected (and tight) for every vertex $v$ of degree 3.

*Proof.* 6.7. By Theorem 6(a), (b), (b') and $s=-2$, $H-v$ has $r\le2$ components, all tight (slack $\le s+2=0$). Suppose $r=2$, components $F_1,F_2$, and classify each pair $P_i$ as type 1 or 2 (inside $F_1$ or $F_2$) or $s$ (split). By (b') the types form one of $\{1,2,s\}$, $\{1,s,s\}$, $\{2,s,s\}$, $\{s,s,s\}$.
6.8 (non-co-transversal pairs in a tight component). Let $F$ be a tight component with at least 5 vertices and $x\ne x'$ non-isolated vertices of $F$ lying in no common minimum transversal. By (U2) (I11 holds for $F-\{x,x'\}$; $\mathrm{slack}(F)=0$), $|E_{x,x'}|\le2$, i.e. $d_F(x)+d_F(x')-[x,x'\text{ share an edge}]\le2$. In every case a minimum transversal of $H-v$ meets $P_1,P_2,P_3$, contradicting Theorem 6(b). So $r=1$. ∎

## 7. Theorem 7 (a global bound through the matching method)

**Theorem 7.** Let $H$ be a linear 3-graph with $\Delta\le3$ and let $S$ be a vertex set such that $H-S$ has maximum degree $\le2$ (e.g. $S$ = all degree-3 vertices). Then
$$7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)\ \ge\ 14|E_S|-56|S|+21\,n_0(H-S)+n_2(H-S).$$
If no edge contains two degree-3 vertices and $S$ is the set of degree-3 vertices ($n_3=|S|$), then $7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge n_2(H)-20n_3$; so I11 holds whenever $n_2\ge20n_3$.

*Proof.* (1) $\tau(H)\le|S|+\tau(H-S)$ ($S$ meets every edge of $E_S$; Lemma 0.2 with $Z=T=S$, $g\ge0$), so $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge\mathrm{slack}(H-S)+3|S|+2|E_S|-11|S|$. (2) $H-S$ is linear with $\Delta\le2$; Theorem 2 gives $7\,\mathrm{slack}(H-S)\ge21n_0(H-S)+n_2(H-S)$. (3) Combine: $7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge14|E_S|-56|S|+21n_0(H-S)+n_2(H-S)$. (4) In the special case, $|E_S|=3n_3$ (the edge sets at degree-3 vertices are disjoint), so $14|E_S|-56|S|=-14n_3$; a degree-2 vertex of $H$ loses at most 2 edges, and it is still of degree 2 in $H-S$ unless one of its edges contains a vertex of $S$; each vertex of $S$ has 6 neighbours, so at most $6n_3$ degree-2 vertices are affected and $n_2(H-S)\ge n_2-6n_3$. Hence $7\,\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge n_2-6n_3-14n_3$. ∎

**Remark 7.1.** In a minimal counterexample, Theorem 7 must fail for every such $S$: $14|E_S|+21n_0(H-S)+n_2(H-S)<56|S|$ (as $s<0$). In the special case (no edge with two degree-3 vertices) it forces $n_2<20n_3$; the local reductions of Theorems 3, 5, 6 forbid dense local clusters of degree-3 vertices next to pendants. Balancing the two is the shape a discharging proof would have; it is not done here (GAP 2).

## 8. The dense core: what the census says (Propositions 8.1, 8.2)

**Proposition 8.1 (Red pairs certify slack; they do not exist in counterexamples of the same shape).** For every (Red) pair $(Z,T)$ of any 3-graph $H$: $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)$ (Lemma 0.2 with $\mathrm{value}_T(Z)\ge0$, $g\ge0$). Hence if $\mathrm{slack}(H)<0$, every (Red) pair of $H$ leaves a counterexample $H-Z$ with $\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)\le\mathrm{slack}(H)$. In particular a (Red) pair whose $H-Z$ satisfies I11 (for instance $H-Z$ a disjoint union of single edges, isolated vertices and 3-edge triangles, all of which satisfy I11) can exist only if $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge0$: such a pair is a certificate of the global inequality, not a local reduction. (Proof: immediate from Lemma 0.2.) ∎

**Proposition 8.2 (the hardest low-slack census graphs) [verified-here, `dense_core.py`, `dense_core2.py`].** Among all connected members of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$, $9\le n\le14$ and slack $\le5$, exactly 17 have no (Red) pair with $|Z|\le5$ (none at $n=14$): one $(11,7,4)$ (slack 3, $\Delta=2$), eight $(12,10,5)$ (slack 1, degrees $2^63^6$), seven $(13,10,5)$ (slack 4) and one $(13,9,5)$ (slack 2). For every one of them and for EVERY (Red) set $Z$ of minimum size, $H-Z$ is one of: two disjoint edges (7 of the $(12,10,5)$ graphs, $|Z|=6$; the $(13,9,5)$ graph), one edge (the eighth $(12,10,5)$ graph, $|Z|=9$, $Z=V\setminus e$ for a $\tau$-critical edge $e$), two edges plus an isolated vertex ($(13,10,5)$, $|Z|=6$), an edge plus an isolated vertex ($(11,7,4)$ and two $(13,10,5)$, $|Z|=7,9$), or a triangle of three edges on 6 vertices ($(13,10,5)$, $(13,9,5)$, $|Z|=7$); in all cases the reduction is exact ($g=0$, $\mathrm{value}(Z)=\mathrm{slack}(H)-\mathrm{slack}(H-Z)$). By Proposition 8.1 these are global certificates: a counterexample with the same local structure would have no (Red) pair at all.
Among all low-slack census graphs (slack $\le2$, $5\le n\le14$), the smallest (Red) set has size 2, 3 or 4 except for: the $\Delta\le2$ graph $(8,5,3)$ of Step 2.6 ($|Z|=5$), 7 graphs with $|Z|=6$ and 1 with $|Z|=9$ (all $(12,10,5)$), and 1 with $|Z|=7$ ($(13,9,5)$) (`red_*.tsv`, column 4 = slack, column 14 = smallest $|Z|$).

**Remark 8.3 (local catalogues at degree-2 and degree-3 roots) [verified-here, `localconf.py 2 1`, `localconf.py 3 1`].** At radius 1, the configuration enumeration of Theorem 3 leaves 71 irreducible leaves for a degree-2 root (384 nodes) and 9,316 for a degree-3 root (53,799 nodes; 9,134 of them in $C$), counted with isomorphic repetitions. By Theorem 4 (every vertex of the graph $H$ there other than $p$ is a degree-2 vertex in a tree-like region, and no (Red) pair of $H$ has $|Z|\le R$), no finite radius makes the degree-2 catalogue complete: a local proof of RU at degree-2 vertices is impossible, exactly as at pendant vertices. For degree-3 roots the radius-1 catalogue is large, but non-locality was NOT proved here (the graph $H'$ of Remark 4.1 has a small (Red) pair at its degree-3 vertices).

**Insight (dense core) [heuristic interpretation of Propositions 8.1, 8.2 and Theorem 4].** In every census graph, the (Red) pairs are of two kinds: *local* ones ($|Z|\le5$, determined by a bounded neighbourhood, valid in every graph containing the configuration, including counterexamples) and *global* ones ($H-Z$ consisting of at most three edges, valid only because $\mathrm{slack}(H)\ge0$). In the low-slack graphs with all degrees in $\{2,3\}$ that resist local reduction, only global pairs exist, and they are exact. Combined with Theorem 4, this shows that Conjecture RU cannot be proved by exhibiting (Red) pairs from local structure: in the remaining case the missing ingredient is a global upper bound on $\tau$ for locally irreducible graphs (Theorem 7 is the simplest instance), i.e. an I11-type statement itself.

## 9. What remains (GAPs), the smallest open sub-cases, and tests

GAP 1: Conjecture RU is not proved. By Theorem 4, RU fails to be local: for every $R$ there are members of $C$ (with $\Delta=2$, and configurations inside graphs with $\Delta=3$, Remark 4.1) whose (Red) pairs all have $|Z|>R$. Any proof of RU must contain a global argument. Moreover RU implies I11 (task statement), so it is at least as hard as I11; by Proposition 8.1, a proof of RU for a hypothetical counterexample amounts to showing it contains a smaller counterexample.

GAP 2: I11 is not proved; the remaining case is the following. A minimal counterexample $H$, if one exists, satisfies ALL of the following (proved above): connected, $\Delta(H)=3$ (Cor. 2.1), $n\ge28$ (Remark 0.6), $s\in\{-1,-2\}$ (Thm 6(a)) and $s=-1$ if Conjecture T' holds (Thm 8), no (Red) pair (Lemma 0.3); every pendant vertex has configuration P-tree-3 or P-tri-3 (Thm 3(c)), forces $s=-1$, and makes $H-\{y,a\}$ a connected tight graph with $\ge2$ pendant vertices (Thm 5); every degree-3 vertex $v$ satisfies Theorem 6(b), (b') ($H-v$ has at most 2 components, all tight but at most one of slack 1, no component contains two of the co-vertex pairs if there are two components, and no minimum transversal of $H-v$ meets the three co-vertex pairs), and if $s=-2$ and Conjecture P2 holds then $H-v$ is connected (Proposition 6.2); every degree-2 vertex satisfies Theorem 6(c); and $14|E_S|+21n_0(H-S)+n_2(H-S)<56|S|$ for every $S$ with $\Delta(H-S)\le2$ (Remark 7.1). What is missing to exclude such an $H$:
- (M1) Conjecture P2 (Corollary 5.2), or another argument excluding pendant vertices. Smallest open sub-case: a tight connected member of $C$, $\Delta\le3$, with two pendant vertices and at least 26 vertices (by Theorem 5(vi), a minimal counterexample with a pendant vertex produces one).
- (M2) The degree-3 step: an argument that some minimum transversal of $H-v$ meets the three pairs $P_1,P_2,P_3$ (Theorem 6(b)). By Theorem 6(a), the failure of this statement for a connected $H$ with $\mathrm{slack}(H-v)\le1$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{slack}(H)=\mathrm{slack}(H-v)-2<0$; so (M2) restated for general graphs is the inductive step of I11 itself and needs structural knowledge of the connected members of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$ and slack $\le1$ (a classification of tight graphs, Conjecture T' "tight connected members with $\Delta\le3$ have at most 17 vertices", plus a slack-1 analogue), or a global counting argument (Remark 7.1).
- The case $s=-2$ is closed modulo Conjecture T' (Theorem 8). Smallest open sub-case of (M2): $s=-1$, i.e. for every degree-3 vertex $v$ the graph $H-v$ consists of tight components and exactly one component $F^\ast$ of slack 1, and no minimum transversal of $H-v$ meets the three co-vertex pairs. What is needed: (a) Conjecture T' (so that the tight components are among the 13 classes, whose transversal data 8.3 is known) and (b) the corresponding transversal properties for connected members of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$ and slack 1, which are not known to be finitely many (the cell $(26,22,11)$ alone has 36 classes): for $F^\ast$ one needs that two disjoint admissible pairs are met by a common minimum transversal, and the analogues of 8.6 (ii)-(iv) (which pairs are not co-transversal). These properties hold for all slack-1 classes computed so far (experiments/20261027-0300-transversal-richness, unrefereed: `pp_all_cov = pp_all` and no admissible uncoverable triple for the slack-1 cells (8,5,3), (12,10,5), (15,11,6), (19,16,8), (22,17,9), (26,22,11)), but no proof for general slack-1 graphs is known.
