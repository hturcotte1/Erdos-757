## Statement

$C$ is the class of L007–L012: 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set $S$ with $|S|\ge2$. $\tau$ is the transversal number and $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$ the I11 slack.

**Theorem 1 (I11 up to 26 vertices).** Every $H\in C$ with $n\le26$ vertices satisfies $11\,\tau(H)\le3n+2m$.

**Corollary 2.** Every (4,5)-set $A$ with $|A|\le26$ satisfies $11\,\tau(H(A))\le3|A|+2m(A)$, where $m(A)$ is the number of 3-term arithmetic progressions in $A$; equivalently $h(A)\ge|A|-\lfloor(3|A|+2m(A))/11\rfloor$.

**Remark 3 (no new value of $f$).** With $m\le n-2$, Theorem 1 gives $h\ge n-\lfloor(5n-4)/11\rfloor$, which is $13,14,14,15$ for $n=23,24,25,26$: exactly the Henning–Yeo bound $n-\lfloor(8n-6)/17\rfloor$ at these four sizes (C1 (c)). So Theorem 1 decides no value of $f$: $f(24)=14$ and $f(26)=15$ were known (kb/00_START_HERE.md §5), and $f(23)\in\{13,14\}$ and $f(25)\in\{14,15\}$ ($f(25)\le f(11)+f(14)=15$ by K1) stay open, because the record cells $(23,21,10)$ (I11 slack 1) and $(25,23,11)$ (slack 0) satisfy I11; there is no record cell at $n=24,26$ (C1 (b)).

**Remark 4 (what Theorem 1 says about records at $n=23$ and $n=25$).** (a) A 23-point (4,5)-set with $h=13$ has $m=21$ ($110\le69+2m$); this is L012 Theorem 2 again (the cells (23,19,10) and (23,20,10) are violator cells, excluded in Steps 3 and 4). The maximum-degree-3 case of $(23,21,10)$ (Q14, L012 Remark 4) is **not** touched here. (b) A 25-point (4,5)-set with $h=14$ ($14/25<4/7$) has $m=23=n-2$ ($121\le75+2m$), so its AP hypergraph $H$ is saturated, hence connected (L012 0.6), I11-tight ($s=0$), and by Theorem 1 at $n=24$ every vertex $v$ has $\tau(H-v)=10$ and $d(v)\le4$: $11\tau(H-v)\le72+2(23-d)=118-2d<121$ forces $\tau(H-v)=10$, and then $110\le118-2d$ (C1 (d)).

**Remark 5 ($n=27$: where this engine stops).** The violator cells at $n=27$ that counting does not exclude are $(27,13,10)$ with $\Delta\le2$, $(27,14,10)$ $\Delta\le3$, $(27,18,11)$ $\Delta\le2$, $(27,19,11)$ $\Delta\le3$ and $(27,25,12)$ $\Delta\le3$ ($s=-1$); $(27,23,12)$ and $(27,24,12)$ are excluded by counting (C1 (a)). In $(27,25,12)$ every vertex has degree $\le3$ and $3m=75>54$, so a vertex $w$ of degree 3 exists; the engine's only branch is $d=3$, $t'=11$, $S'=1$ ($t'=12$ gives $S'=-10$), and the decompositions of $(26,22)$ with total slack 1 are exactly $[(26,22,11)]$, $[(12,10,5),(14,12,6)]$ and $[(3,1,1),(23,21,10)]$ (two generators, EQUAL; `n27_remark_output.txt`). The last one needs the complete list `cell(23,21,10,3)` of connected members of $C$ with $(23,21,10)$ and $\Delta\le3$, which is exactly the open computation of Q14 (L012 Step 8), and the first needs `cell(26,22,11,3)`, whose branch ($d=3$, $t'=10$, $(25,19)$ with total slack 3, 15 decompositions) contains $[(3,1,1),(22,18,9)]$, i.e. `cell(22,18,9,3)`, the first recursion level of Q14 (L012 8.1). ## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L007–L012 Step 0.1.) $n,m,\tau,s$ are additive over components; a single vertex has $s=3$; $C$ is hereditary (every subhypergraph, in particular $H-v$, lies in $C$); for $H\in C$: $\tau\le m$, $\tau(H)-1\le\tau(H-v)\le\tau(H)$, $m\le n-2$ ((P3) with $S=V$, $n\ge2$), $17\tau\le5n+3m$ (K8); a connected 3-graph with $m\ge1$ edges has $n\le2m+1$; deleting a vertex does not increase degrees; a component of a 3-graph is an isolated vertex or has at least 3 vertices.

0.2 I11 holds on $C$ for $n\le22$ (L011 Theorem 1).

0.3 (Component lemma, L011 0.3.) If I11 holds on $C$ for all graphs with fewer than $N$ vertices, then every $H\in C$ with $N$ vertices and $s(H)<0$ is connected: otherwise every component has fewer than $N$ vertices and slack $\ge0$, and $s(H)$ is their sum.

0.4 (Degree lemma, L011 0.4.) Under the same hypothesis let $H\in C$ have $N$ vertices, $m$ edges, $\tau(H)=\tau$, $s=s(H)<0$, and let $v$ have degree $d$. Then $H-v\in C$ has $N-1$ vertices, $m-d$ edges and $\tau'=\tau(H-v)\in\{\tau-1,\tau\}$, and I11 at $N-1$ gives $0\le3(N-1)+2(m-d)-11\tau'=s-3-2d+11(\tau-\tau')$. If $\tau'=\tau$ this says $s\ge3+2d>0$, false. Hence $\tau(H-v)=\tau-1$ and $d\le\lfloor(s+8)/2\rfloor$ for every vertex $v$.

0.5 (The engine for general $n$.) For a key $(n,m,t,D)$ let $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ be the set of connected $X\in C$ with $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, $\tau(X)=t$ and $\Delta(X)\le D$; `engine.cell(n,m,t,D)` writes a list of graphs to `out/cells/cell_n_m_t_D<D>.txt`.
**Lemma E′.** Let $n\le31$ and suppose I11 holds on $C$ for all graphs with fewer than $n$ vertices. Then every graph in the list `cell(n,m,t,D)` lies in $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$, and every member of $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ is isomorphic to a graph in the list.
*Proof.* This is L012 3.2–3.4, where the hypothesis was used only in the sentence "each component $K$ of $X-w$ … has at most $n-1\le21$ vertices, hence slack $\ge0$ (0.2)" of 3.2(ii); here it reads "at most $n-1$ vertices, hence slack $\ge0$ (hypothesis)". The code (`engine.py`, byte-identical to L012's) contains no other dependence on $n$ than the program limits of 0.6. In detail, by induction on $m$ (for all $n'\le n$ at once): (i) *Soundness.* Every graph that is saved passed `filt`: linear, $F_7$-free, (P3), connected on all $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, and $\tau=t$ (computed exactly on the direct route and in the branches with $t'=t$; in the branches with $t'=t-1$, `ext_d` guarantees $\tau\ge t$ and $\tau\le\tau(G)+1=t$). The degree bound holds: on the direct route by `enum_conn`'s bound $D$; in the recursion the input $G$ has $\Delta(G)\le d$ (its components come from cells with bound $d$), the new vertex has degree $d$ and its endpoints have $G$-degree $\le d-1$, so $\Delta\le d\le D$. (ii) *Completeness, $m\le\mathrm{MD}=10$.* `enum_conn n m t D` outputs a copy of every connected linear graph with $\tau\ge t$, $\Delta\le D$ (L007 Lemma D) and `filt` keeps exactly those in $C$ with $\tau=t$. (iii) *Completeness, $m\ge11$.* Let $X\in\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ and let $w$ have degree $d=\Delta(X)$; then $\lceil3m/n\rceil\le d\le D$. $X-w\in C$ has $n-1$ vertices, $m-d$ edges, $\Delta\le d$, $\tau(X-w)=t'\in\{t-1,t\}$, and its components lie in $C$, have at most $n-1$ vertices and hence slack $\ge0$, with slacks adding to $S'=3(n-1)+2(m-d)-11t'\ge0$; so their types $(n_K,m_K,\tau_K)$ form a decomposition of $(n-1,m-d)$ with total slack $S'$ in which every connected type has $n_K\ge3$, $\lceil(n_K-1)/2\rceil\le m_K\le n_K-2$, $1\le\tau_K\le m_K$, K8 and slack in $[0,S']$, and isolated vertices (type $(1,0,0)$, slack 3) occur only if $S'\ge3$; `engine.decompositions` lists all of these (checked against an independent generator in C4). A component of type $(n_K,m_K,\tau_K)$ lies in $\mathcal C(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)$, and $n_K\le n-1$, so the hypothesis of the lemma holds for it and, by induction on $m$ ($m_K<m$), it is isomorphic to a graph of `cell(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)`. Hence $X-w$ is isomorphic to one of the inputs (disjoint unions of one representative per component, on consecutive vertex blocks; multisets of representatives for repeated types). The $d$ edges at $w$ are $\{w,a_i,b_i\}$ with pairwise disjoint pairs, no pair inside an edge of $X-w$, endpoints of $(X-w)$-degree $\le d-1$ (L012 0.5), so by L012 0.4 $X$ is isomorphic to such an extension of an input, with $\tau=t$, connected and in $C$. `ext_d (n-1) t' t d (d-1)` lists every such extension with $\tau\ge t$ that is $F_7$-free and satisfies (P3) (L012 3.5: every hit exactly once; a branch is cut only when a new edge lies in a copy of $F_7$, which every superset then contains), and `filt` keeps it. (iv) *Reduction.* `canon` prints, for every input graph, a relabelling of it (the canonical labelling of nauty applied to the incidence graph), and `dedupe` keeps one printed graph per distinct output; so an isomorphic copy of every graph that reaches `dedupe` is kept, and two kept graphs are distinct canonical forms, hence non-isomorphic (nauty). $\square$

0.6 (Program limits.) `ext_d` stops with an error if $G$ has more than 31 vertices or $d>8$ (exit code 1), if an input has more than 262,144 minimum covers (exit code 3) or more than 256 admissible pairs (exit code 4); its edge array holds 40 edges (not checked at run time; here at most 24). `filt` stops if a graph has more than 40 edges (exit code 3) and works with up to 32 vertices (not checked; here at most 26). `canon` stops if $n+m>64$ (exit code 3; here at most 50). `enum_conn` stops if $N>31$ or $M>31$. In this certificate $n\le26$ and $m\le n-2\le24$. `engine.run` and `engine.ext_parallel` raise an exception on any non-zero exit code and `engine.recurse` on any `bad_tau`$\ne0$; every run of C3 ended normally, so no limit was reached.

0.7 (Order of the induction.) By Lemma E′ a cell list of size $n'$ is correct as soon as I11 holds on $C$ below $n'$: for $n'\le23$ by 0.2, for $n'=24$ after Step 4, for $n'=25$ after Step 5, for $n'=26$ after Step 6. The engine never uses a cell of size $\ge n$ inside a cell of size $n$ (components have at most $n-1$ vertices), so every list used in Step $k$ is covered by what is proved before Step $k$.
