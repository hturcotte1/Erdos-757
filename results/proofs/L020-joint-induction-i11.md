## Statement

**Conventions** (as in L017). A 3-graph $H$ has vertex set $V(H)$ and a set $E(H)$ of 3-subsets; $n,m,d_H(x),\Delta(H),n_i(H)$ as usual; $H-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(H)\setminus Z$; $\partial_H(x,y)$ is the set of edges meeting $\{x,y\}$, and $|\partial_H(x,y)|=d_H(x)+d_H(y)-\sigma_H(x,y)$ for linear $H$, where $\sigma_H(x,y)=1$ if $x,y$ lie in a common edge and $0$ otherwise; $\tau$ is the transversal number; a set $X$ is *co-transversal* in $H$ if some minimum transversal of $H$ contains $X$; $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$ is the slack; *tight* means $s=0$; $E_1$ is the single edge. For pairwise disjoint 2-sets $Q_1,\dots,Q_k\subseteq V(H)$, $H+w(Q_1,\dots,Q_k)$ adds a new vertex $w$ and the edges $\{w\}\cup Q_j$. $C$ is the class of 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every $S$ with $|S|\ge2$.

**Definitions.**
- $(\mathrm H_N)$: every member of $C$ with at most $N$ vertices satisfies I11 ($s\ge0$).
- A *near-tight graph* is a connected $K\in C$ with $\Delta(K)\le3$ and $s(K)\le1$. It is *big* if $n(K)\ge27$.
- An *NC pair* of a 3-graph $F$ is a set $\{x,y\}$ of two distinct vertices with $d_F(x),d_F(y)\le2$ that is not co-transversal in $F$.
- A *b-configuration* of $F$ is a set of three vertices of $F$-degree $\le2$ any two of which form an NC pair. An *e-configuration* $(z;x,y)$ consists of three distinct vertices of $F$-degree $\le2$ such that $\{z,x\}$ and $\{z,y\}$ are NC pairs and $x,y$ lie in no common edge of $F$.
- *Convention on b-configuration counts.* The definition allows the three vertices to form an edge. The counts of C3 for slack-2 cells and of C8 count only triples that are NOT an edge, unless "edges allowed" is said. Both counts, classes with a b-configuration (edges allowed / non-edge triples only), from the opus referee of round 1 (own code, `bcfg_slack2_output.txt`; its non-edge counts equal the prover's): $(9,4,3)$ 1/1 and 0; $(10,8,4)$ 1/63 and 0; $(13,9,5)$ 32/84 and 3; $(16,10,6)$ 16/16 and 0; $(20,15,8)$ 159/953 and 0. For the ten slack $\le1$ lists the full definition was used (`roles_b_any.py`). An edge-forming triple glued to $E_1$ creates an $F_7$, so the narrower notion loses nothing for the gluings with $E_1$.
- For a 3-graph $F$, an *admissible triple* is a triple of pairwise disjoint 2-sets $P_1,P_2,P_3$ of vertices of $F$-degree $\le2$ with $F+v(P_1,P_2,P_3)\in C$; it is *covered* if some minimum transversal of $F$ meets $P_1$, $P_2$ and $P_3$.

**Theorem 2 (maximum degree at most 2)** [published, re-proved here: Henning–Yeo, Electron. J. Combin. 24(2) (2017) P2.50, Proposition 9 ($\tau=|E|-\alpha'$ of the dual graph) with Chvátal–Hanson, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 20 (1976) 128–138 (edge/matching bound for maximum degree 3; extremal graph $K_4$ with one edge subdivided); only the packaging as the slack $s$ is ours]. Let $H$ be a linear 3-graph with $\Delta(H)\le2$, $n_0$ isolated vertices and $n_2$ vertices of degree 2. Then $7\,s(H)\ge21n_0+n_2$. If $H$ is connected with $m\ge2$ edges, then $s(H)\ge(m-1)/7$ and $n\le2m+1$; hence a connected linear 3-graph with $\Delta\le2$ and $s\le1$ has $m\le8$ and $n\le17$, and one with $s\le3$ has $m\le22$ and $n\le45$. In particular every near-tight graph with at least 18 vertices has a vertex of degree 3. (The inequality $7s\ge21n_0+n_2$ is L018 Theorem 2; Lemma 2.1 below is an independent proof of its matching part.)

**Theorem 3 (a vertex of degree 3 in a near-tight graph).** Let $K$ be a near-tight graph such that $(\mathrm H_{n(K)})$ holds, and let $v$ be a vertex of degree 3 with edges $\{v\}\cup P_j$, $P_1=\{a,b\}$, $P_2=\{c,d\}$, $P_3=\{e,f\}$. Put $G=K-v$, let $F_1,\dots,F_r$ be the components of $G$ with at least one edge and $Y$ the set of isolated vertices of $G$. Then:
(i) $a,\dots,f$ are distinct, $d_G(u)=d_K(u)-1\in\{0,1,2\}$ for each of them, no $P_j$ lies inside an edge of $G$; $\tau(G)=\tau(K)-1$ and $s(G)=s(K)+2$; and ($\star$) no minimum transversal of $G$ meets $P_1$, $P_2$ and $P_3$.
(ii) $Y$ is the set of co-vertices of $K$-degree 1; $|Y|\le1$; if $Y\ne\emptyset$ then $s(K)=1$ and every $F_i$ is tight, and otherwise $\sum_is(F_i)=s(K)+2\in\{2,3\}$. Every $F_i$ is a connected member of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$, at most $n(K)-1$ vertices and $0\le s(F_i)\le3$, and contains a co-vertex.
(iii) (*free choice*) every vertex of every $F_i$ lies in a minimum transversal of $F_i$; (*pair richness*) if $Q_1,Q_2\subseteq V(F_i)$ are disjoint 2-sets with $F_i+w(Q_1,Q_2)\in C$, some minimum transversal of $F_i$ meets $Q_1$ and $Q_2$; this applies whenever $Q_1,Q_2$ are two of $P_1,P_2,P_3$; (*pair lemma*) if $\{x,y\}\subseteq V(F_i)$ is not co-transversal in $F_i$, then $\tau(F_i-x-y)=\tau(F_i)-1$ and $s(F_i-x-y)=s(F_i)+5-2|\partial_{F_i}(x,y)|\ge0$.
(iv) If $Y=\emptyset$, then $r\le2$, a component contains two of the pairs only if $r=1$, and up to renaming the pairs, their vertices and the components the configuration is exactly one of
 (a) $r=1$: ($\star$) says that the admissible triple $P_1,P_2,P_3$ of $F_1=G$ is not covered;
 (b) $r=2$, $P_j=\{x_j,y_j\}$ with $x_j\in V(F_1)$, $y_j\in V(F_2)$: ($\star$) $\iff$ $\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}$ is a b-configuration of $F_1$ and $\{y_1,y_2,y_3\}$ one of $F_2$;
 (e) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2\subseteq V(F_2)$, $e\in V(F_1)$, $f\in V(F_2)$: ($\star$) $\iff$ $(e;a,b)$ is an e-configuration of $F_1$ and $(f;c,d)$ one of $F_2$;
 (f) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $c,e\in V(F_1)$, $d,f\in V(F_2)$: ($\star$) $\iff$ the four sets $\{x,y\}$ ($x\in P_1$, $y\in\{c,e\}$) are NC pairs of $F_1$ (so $(c;a,b)$ is an e-configuration of $F_1$) and $\{d,f\}$ is an NC pair of $F_2$; moreover $F_1+w(P_1,\{c,e\})\notin C$.
(v) If $Y=\{y\}$, say $P_3=\{y,f\}$, then $d_K(f)\in\{2,3\}$, $r\le3$, $P_1$ and $P_2$ do not lie in one component unless $r=1$, and up to renaming the configuration is exactly one of
 (i1) $r=1$: no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets $P_1$ and $P_2$ and contains $f$;
 (i2-1) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2\subseteq V(F_2)$, $f\in V(F_1)$: ($\star$) $\iff$ $\{a,f\},\{b,f\}$ are NC pairs of $F_1$;
 (i2-2) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $c,f\in V(F_1)$, $d\in V(F_2)$: ($\star$) $\iff$ $\{a,f\},\{b,f\}$ are NC pairs of $F_1$;

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 Facts 0.1–0.7, L013 Step 0.1.) Additivity of $n,m,\tau,s$ over components, and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts; an isolated vertex has $s=3$, $s(E_1)=0$; $C$ is closed under deleting vertices and edges; $\tau(H)-|Z|\le\tau(H-Z)\le\tau(H)$; $X$ is co-transversal iff $\tau(H-X)=\tau(H)-|X|$, and an isolated vertex is never co-transversal; $s(H-Z)=s(H)-3|Z|-2|\partial_H(Z)|+11(\tau(H)-\tau(H-Z))$; in a linear 3-graph the edges at a vertex meet pairwise only in it; if $H$ is connected and $\emptyset\ne Z\subsetneq V(H)$, every component of $H-Z$ contains a vertex of an edge meeting $Z$; in a connected linear $H$ with $n\ge2$, $u\ne v$ is isolated in $H-v$ iff $u$ is a co-vertex of $v$ of degree 1; a connected 3-graph with $m\ge1$ edges has $n\le2m+1$.
0.2 (L017 Theorem 2(U).) If $s(F-X)\ge0$ and $s(F)<2|\partial_F(X)|+11-8|X|$, then $X$ is co-transversal in $F$. For $X=\{x\}$ with $d_F(x)\ge1$ the condition is $s(F)<3+2d_F(x)$.
0.3 (Extension identity.) For a 3-graph $F$ and disjoint 2-sets $Q_1,\dots,Q_k\subseteq V(F)$, put $F^+=F+w(Q_1,\dots,Q_k)$. Then $\tau(F)\le\tau(F^+)\le\tau(F)+1$, and $\tau(F^+)=\tau(F)$ iff some minimum transversal of $F$ meets $Q_1,\dots,Q_k$. *Proof.* A transversal of $F^+$ restricted to $V(F)$ is a transversal of $F$; adding $w$ to a transversal of $F$ gives one of $F^+$. If $T$ is a transversal of $F^+$ with $\tau(F)$ vertices, then $w\notin T$ (else $T\setminus\{w\}$ would be a transversal of $F$ with $\tau(F)-1$ vertices), so $T$ is a minimum transversal of $F$ meeting every $Q_j$; the converse is clear. $\square$ Hence, if no minimum transversal of $F$ meets the $Q_j$, $s(F^+)=s(F)+3+2k-11=s(F)+2k-8$.

### Step 1. Proof of Theorem 1
1.1 All claims are exact computations; the programs, commands and outputs are C1, C2, C3, C5 below. Membership in $C$, $\tau$, connectivity and degrees were established twice, by two separately written codes (C1: `pattern_e_build.py` with L017's `hyp.py` and a Hall-type (P3) test; C2: `verify_K29.py` with brute-force $F_7$ search over all 5-edge subsets and vertex maps, (P3) through Hopcroft–Karp matchings, an explicit transversal with 12 vertices and an exhaustive search proving that none with 11 exists). The (P3) test used in both codes rests on the equivalence: $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ $\iff$ for every pair $\{x,y\}$ of vertices the edges have pairwise distinct representatives in $V\setminus\{x,y\}$. *Proof.* ($\Leftarrow$) For $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ choose $x,y\in S$; the edges inside $S$ have distinct representatives in $S\setminus\{x,y\}$, so $e(S)\le|S|-2$. ($\Rightarrow$) For a set $E'$ of edges put $S=\bigcup E'\cup\{x,y\}$; then $|E'|\le e(S)\le|S|-2=|\bigcup E'\setminus\{x,y\}|$, which is Hall's condition for the bipartite graph between edges and $V\setminus\{x,y\}$. $\square$

### Step 2. Proof of Theorem 2
**Lemma 2.1.** Every simple graph $G$ with $\Delta(G)\le3$ satisfies $7\nu(G)\ge2|E(G)|$.
*Proof.* Let $N=|V(G)|$, $M=|E(G)|$. By the Tutte–Berge formula there is $U\subseteq V(G)$ with $2\nu(G)=N+|U|-o(G-U)$, $o$ the number of odd components of $G-U$. Let $C_1,\dots,C_q$ be the components of $G-U$, with $c_i$ vertices and $e_i$ edges. At most $3|U|$ edges meet $U$, so $M\le3|U|+\sum_ie_i$, and $N=|U|+\sum_ic_i$. Hence
$$2\nu(G)-\tfrac47M\ \ge\ 2|U|+\sum_ic_i-o-\tfrac{12}7|U|-\tfrac47\sum_ie_i=\tfrac27|U|+\sum_it_i,\qquad t_i:=c_i-[c_i\ \mathrm{odd}]-\tfrac47e_i .$$
As $C_i$ is simple with maximum degree $\le3$, $e_i\le\min(\binom{c_i}2,\lfloor3c_i/2\rfloor)$, so: $c_i=1$: $t_i=0$; $c_i=2$: $t_i\ge2-\frac47>0$; $c_i=3$: $t_i\ge2-\frac{12}7=\frac27$; $c_i=4$: $t_i\ge4-\frac{24}7=\frac47$; $c_i=5$: $t_i\ge4-\frac{28}7=0$; even $c_i\ge6$: $t_i\ge c_i-\frac67c_i>0$; odd $c_i\ge7$: $t_i\ge c_i-1-\frac47\cdot\frac{3c_i-1}2=\frac{c_i-5}7>0$. So $2\nu\ge\frac47M$. $\square$ (Checked on all 5,389 graphs with $\Delta\le3$ and at most 10 vertices, C6; equality with $M>0$ only for disjoint unions of copies of $K_4$ with one edge subdivided, plus isolated vertices.)
2.2 Let $G^*$ be the dual graph of $H$ (vertices: the edges of $H$; one edge $ef$ for every vertex of degree 2 lying in the edges $e\ne f$). By L017 Theorem 7, Steps 4.1–4.3 (only linearity is used), $G^*$ is simple with $\Delta(G^*)\le3$, $|E(G^*)|=n_2$, and $s(H)=3n_0+11\nu(G^*)-3n_2$. By Lemma 2.1, $11\nu(G^*)\ge\frac{22}7n_2$, so $s(H)\ge3n_0+\frac17n_2$, i.e. $7s(H)\ge21n_0+n_2$.
2.3 Let $H$ be connected with $m\ge2$. Then $n\ge3$ and $H$ has no isolated vertex, $n_0=0$. $G^*$ is connected: for edges $e\ni x$ and $f\ni y$ of $H$ take a path $x=u_0,u_1,\dots,u_l=y$ in the 2-section of $H$ and edges $h_i\supseteq\{u_i,u_{i+1}\}$; in the sequence $e,h_0,\dots,h_{l-1},f$ consecutive edges share a vertex, and two distinct edges sharing a vertex $u$ have $d(u)=2$, so $u$ gives the $G^*$-edge between them. A connected graph on $m$ vertices has at least $m-1$ edges, so $n_2\ge m-1$ and $s(H)\ge(m-1)/7$; $n\le2m+1$ by 0.1. If $s\le1$: $m\le8$, $n\le17$; if $s\le3$: $m\le22$, $n\le45$. A near-tight graph with $n\ge18$ vertices is connected and linear with $m\ge(n-1)/2\ge2$, so it cannot have $\Delta\le2$. $\square$
