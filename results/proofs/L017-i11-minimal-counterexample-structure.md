## Statement

**Conventions.** A *3-graph* $H$ is a finite vertex set $V(H)$ with a set $E(H)$ of 3-element subsets (edges); $n=|V(H)|$, $m=|E(H)|$, $d_H(x)$ is the degree of $x$, $\Delta(H)$ the maximum degree, $n_i(H)$ the number of vertices of degree $i$. For $Z\subseteq V(H)$, $H-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(H)\setminus Z$ (all edges disjoint from $Z$), $H-x=H-\{x\}$, and $\partial_H(Z)$ is the set of edges meeting $Z$; $\partial_H(x,y)=\partial_H(\{x,y\})$. For a set $D$ of edges, $H\setminus D$ is $H$ with the edges of $D$ deleted and all vertices kept. A *transversal* is a vertex set meeting every edge, $\tau(H)$ is the least size of one ($\tau=0$ if $m=0$; the empty 3-graph has $n=m=\tau=0$), and a *minimum transversal* is one of size $\tau(H)$. A set $X\subseteq V(H)$ is *co-transversal in $H$* if some minimum transversal of $H$ contains $X$. Two vertices *share an edge* if some edge contains both; $\sigma_H(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ records this. The *co-vertices* of $v$ are the vertices $\ne v$ of the edges at $v$. $E_1$ is the 3-graph with 3 vertices and one edge. The I11 *slack* is $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$; *I11 holds for $H$* if $s(H)\ge0$; $H$ is *tight* if $s(H)=0$.

$C$ is the class of L007–L016: 3-graphs that are (P1) linear ($|e\cap f|\le1$ for distinct edges), (P2) $F_7$-free (no subhypergraph isomorphic to $F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every $S\subseteq V$ with $|S|\ge2$, where $e(S)$ is the number of edges inside $S$.

A *minimal counterexample* is an $H\in C$ with $s(H)<0$ such that every $H'\in C$ with fewer vertices satisfies $s(H')\ge0$. A *vertex-critical violator* is a 3-graph $H$ with $s(H)<0$ such that every proper induced subgraph $H-Z$ ($\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq V(H)$, including $Z=V(H)$) has $s(H-Z)\ge0$. Every minimal counterexample is a vertex-critical violator (its proper induced subgraphs lie in $C$, Fact 0.2, and have fewer vertices).

**Theorem 1 (basic structure).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample, $s=s(H)$, $\tau=\tau(H)$.
(a) $H$ is connected, $m\ge1$, $H$ has no isolated vertex, and $n\ge28$.
(b) For every vertex $v$: $\tau(H-v)=\tau-1$ (every vertex is co-transversal in $H$) and $s(H-v)=s+8-2d_H(v)\ge0$.
(c) $\Delta(H)\le3$.
(d) $\Delta(H)=3$ (by Theorem 7).
(e) $s\in\{-1,-2\}$.
(f) Every nonempty $X\subseteq V(H)$ with $2|\partial_H(X)|>s+8|X|-11$ is co-transversal in $H$. In particular any two vertices $x\ne y$ with $|\partial_H(x,y)|\ge3$ (for instance any two vertices of degree $\ge2$) lie in a common minimum transversal, and if $s=-2$ so do any two with $|\partial_H(x,y)|=2$.
(g) (Edge-criticality.) If, in addition, $m(H)$ is least among all minimal counterexamples (such an $H$ exists whenever a minimal counterexample exists), then $\tau(H\setminus D)\le\tau-\lceil(2|D|-s)/11\rceil$ for every nonempty set $D$ of edges; in particular $\tau(H\setminus e)=\tau-1$ for every edge $e$, and for every edge $e$ and every $x\in e$ some minimum transversal $T$ of $H$ has $T\cap e=\{x\}$.

**Theorem 2 (reduction lemmas).** Let $H$ be any 3-graph.
(R) If $\emptyset\ne Z\subseteq V(H)$ and $T\subseteq V(H)$ meets every edge of $\partial_H(Z)$, then $\tau(H)\le\tau(H-Z)+|T|$ and $s(H)\ge s(H-Z)+3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|-11|T|$.
(R+) Let $W_1,\dots,W_r\subseteq V(H)$ be nonempty and pairwise disjoint, $F_i=H[W_i]$, $W=\bigcup_iW_i$, $R=H-W$ and $E^*=\partial_H(W)\setminus\bigcup_iE(F_i)$. If $T_i$ is a minimum transversal of $F_i$ ($1\le i\le r$) and $T_0\subseteq V(H)$ meets every edge of $E^*$ disjoint from $T_1\cup\dots\cup T_r$, then $s(H)\ge s(R)+\sum_is(F_i)+2|E^*|-11|T_0|$.
(U) (Lemma U″ and its $k$-vertex form.) Let $F$ be a 3-graph and $\emptyset\ne X\subseteq V(F)$ with $s(F-X)\ge0$. If $s(F)<2|\partial_F(X)|+11-8|X|$, then $X$ is co-transversal in $F$. For $X=\{x\}$: if $s(F)<3+2d_F(x)$ then $x$ lies in a minimum transversal of $F$; in particular, if $x$ is isolated in $F$ and $s(F-x)\ge0$ then $s(F)\ge3$. For $X=\{x,y\}$ (two-vertex form): if $s(F)<2|\partial_F(x,y)|-5$ then $\{x,y\}$ is co-transversal in $F$; equivalently (*pair lemma*), if $\{x,y\}$ is not co-transversal in $F$ then $2|\partial_F(x,y)|\le s(F)+5$.
(H) (Hall lemma.) Let $H$ be a vertex-critical violator and $W_i,F_i,E^*$ as in (R+) with every $W_i\ne V(H)$. Let $B$ be the bipartite graph with parts $E^*$ and $\{1,\dots,r\}$ in which $e$ is adjacent to $i$ iff $e\cap W_i\ne\emptyset$. Then there is no injective map $\varphi:E^*\to\{1,\dots,r\}$ with vertices $x_e\in e\cap W_{\varphi(e)}$ such that $s(F_{\varphi(e)})<3+2d_{F_{\varphi(e)}}(x_e)$ for every $e\in E^*$. In particular, if every $F_i$ has $s(F_i)\le2$, or every $F_i$ has $s(F_i)\le4$ and no isolated vertex, then $B$ has no matching saturating $E^*$, so by Hall's theorem some nonempty $S\subseteq E^*$ has fewer than $|S|$ neighbours in $B$.
Consequences for a vertex-critical violator $H$ (in particular a minimal counterexample): $11|T|>3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|+s(H-Z)\ge3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|$ for $Z,T$ as in (R); $11|T_0|>s(R)+\sum_is(F_i)+2|E^*|\ge2|E^*|$ for $W_i,T_i,T_0$ as in (R+) with all $W_i\ne V(H)$, so no choice of minimum transversals $T_i$ of the $F_i$ meets every edge of $E^*$.
*No circularity:* every use of I11 in Theorems 1–6 is for a proper induced subgraph of $H$ ($H-Z$ with $Z\ne\emptyset$, or $F-X$ with $F$ an induced subgraph of $H$ and $X\ne\emptyset$), i.e. for a member of $C$ with fewer vertices than $H$, with one exception: Theorem 1(g) uses I11 for the graphs $H\setminus D$, which have as many vertices as $H$ and fewer edges, and this is why it assumes $m(H)$ least. Theorem 7 is unconditional; the only external input is L016 (for $n\ge28$).

**Theorem 3 (edges).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample.
(a) No edge contains two vertices of degree 1.
(b) Every edge has degree sum at least 5.
(c) If $d(y)=1$ and $\{y,a,b\}$ is the edge at $y$, then $d(a)=d(b)=2$: an edge with a vertex of degree 1 has degree pattern $(1,2,2)$.
(d) A vertex of degree 1 and a vertex of degree 3 never share an edge.
(e) No vertex lies in two edges that both contain a vertex of degree 1.
(f) If $H$ has a vertex of degree 1, then $s(H)=-1$.

**Theorem 4 (a vertex of degree 3).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample, $v$ a vertex of degree 3 with edges $\{v,a,b\},\{v,c,d\},\{v,e,f\}$, $P_1=\{a,b\}$, $P_2=\{c,d\}$, $P_3=\{e,f\}$, and $G=H-v$.
(i) $a,\dots,f$ are six distinct vertices of $H$-degree 2 or 3; their $G$-degrees are $d_H-1\in\{1,2\}$; no $P_j$ lies inside an edge of $G$.
(ii) $\tau(G)=\tau(H)-1$ and $s(G)=s(H)+2\in\{0,1\}$; $G$ has no isolated vertex; its components $F_1,\dots,F_r$ are connected members of $C$ with fewer vertices than $H$, maximum degree $\le3$ and $s(F_i)\ge0$, $\sum_is(F_i)=s(H)+2$; hence all $F_i$ are tight, except exactly one of slack 1 when $s(H)=-1$; every $F_i$ contains a vertex of $P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3$.
(iii) ($\star$) No minimum transversal of $G$ meets all three pairs. Every vertex of every $F_i$ is co-transversal in $F_i$. The bipartite graph between $\{P_1,P_2,P_3\}$ and $\{F_1,\dots,F_r\}$ ($P_j\sim F_i$ iff $P_j\cap V(F_i)\ne\emptyset$) has no matching saturating the three pairs. Consequently $r\le3$ (in particular $r\ge4$ is impossible), and the distributions of the six co-vertices that are Hall-deficient are exactly: every distribution with $r=1$; every distribution with $r=2$; and, for $r=3$, exactly those in which two pairs lie inside one component and the third pair has one vertex in each of the other two. Up to renaming pairs and components, these are the seven patterns
 (a) $r=1$;
 (b) $r=2$, every pair split: $P_j=\{x_j,y_j\}$, $x_j\in V(F_1)$, $y_j\in V(F_2)$;
 (c1) $r=2$, $P_1,P_2\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_3$ split ($e\in V(F_1)$, $f\in V(F_2)$);
 (c2) $r=2$, $P_1,P_2\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_3\subseteq V(F_2)$;
 (e) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2\subseteq V(F_2)$, $P_3$ split ($e\in V(F_1)$, $f\in V(F_2)$);
 (f) $r=2$, $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2,P_3$ split ($c,e\in V(F_1)$, $d,f\in V(F_2)$);
 (d) $r=3$, $P_1,P_2\subseteq V(F_1)$, $e\in V(F_2)$, $f\in V(F_3)$.
(iv) In each pattern ($\star$) is equivalent to the following condition on the components:
 (a) no minimum transversal of $G$ meets $P_1$, $P_2$ and $P_3$;
 (b) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ contains two of $x_1,x_2,x_3$, and no minimum transversal of $F_2$ contains two of $y_1,y_2,y_3$;
 (c1), (c2), (d) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets both $P_1$ and $P_2$;
 (e) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets $P_1$ and contains $e$, and no minimum transversal of $F_2$ meets $P_2$ and contains $f$;
 (f) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets both $P_1$ and $\{c,e\}$, and $\{d,f\}$ is not co-transversal in $F_2$.
(v) Every pair $\{x,y\}$ that (iv) forces to be non-co-transversal in a component $F$ — (b): any two of $x_1,x_2,x_3$ in $F_1$, any two of $y_1,y_2,y_3$ in $F_2$; (c1), (c2), (d): $x\in P_1$, $y\in P_2$ in $F_1$; (e): $\{a,e\},\{b,e\}$ in $F_1$ and $\{c,f\},\{d,f\}$ in $F_2$; (f): $x\in P_1$, $y\in\{c,e\}$ in $F_1$, and $\{d,f\}$ in $F_2$ — satisfies $|\partial_F(x,y)|\le2$ if $F$ is tight and $|\partial_F(x,y)|\le3$ if $s(F)=1$.
(vi) If the component carrying the attachment vertices named in (v) is tight, then at most one of these vertices has $H$-degree 3, and if one, $z$, has, then $z$ shares an edge of that component with each named vertex on the other side (the other two $x_j$ in (b); both vertices of $P_2$, resp. $P_1$, in (c1), (c2), (d); both of $\{c,e\}$, resp. both of $P_1$, in (f)). In (e), if $F_1$ is tight then either $d_H(e)=3$, $d_H(a)=d_H(b)=2$ and $e$ shares an edge of $F_1$ with $a$ and one with $b$, or $d_H(e)=2$ and $d_H(a)=2$ or $d_H(b)=2$; symmetrically for $F_2$ with $c,d,f$.

**Theorem 5 (vertices of degree 2 and 1).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample, $s=s(H)$.

 (i) $\tau(G)=\tau(H)-1$ and $s(G)=s+4\in\{2,3\}$;
 (ii) the isolated vertices of $G$ are exactly the co-vertices of $v$ of $H$-degree 1; there is at most one, and if there is one then $s=-1$;
 (iii) ($\star_2$) no minimum transversal of $G$ meets both $P_1$ and $P_2$;
 (iv) $G$ has exactly one component $F$ with an edge, and $F$ contains every co-vertex of $v$ of $H$-degree $\ge2$; so exactly one of the following holds:
  ($\alpha$) $G=F$ is connected, $s(F)=s+4$, no minimum transversal of $F$ meets both pairs, and $2|\partial_F(x,y)|\le s(F)+5$ (so $|\partial_F(x,y)|\le3$ if $s(F)=2$) for all $x\in P_1$, $y\in P_2$;
  ($\beta$) $G=F+\{y\}$ with $y$ a co-vertex of $H$-degree 1, say $P_1=\{y,b\}$: then $s=-1$, $F$ is connected and tight with $n-2$ vertices, $d_H(b)=2$, $d_H(c),d_H(d)\ge2$, no minimum transversal of $F$ contains $b$ and meets $\{c,d\}$, and $|\partial_F(b,c)|\le2$, $|\partial_F(b,d)|\le2$.
(B) Let $d(y)=1$ with edge $\{y,a,b\}$, and let $f=\{a,c,d\}$ and $g=\{b,p,q\}$ be the second edges at $a$ and $b$ (Theorem 3(c)). Then:

**Theorem 7 (maximum degree 2; Lemma M).** Let $H$ be a linear 3-graph with $\Delta(H)\le2$ and $n_0$ isolated vertices, and let $G$ be its dual graph: $V(G)=E(H)$, with one edge $ef$ of $G$ for every vertex of $H$ of degree 2, lying in the edges $e\ne f$. Then $G$ is a simple graph with $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $|E(G)|=n_2(H)$, $\tau(H)=m-\nu(G)$ ($\nu$ = matching number), and $s(H)=3n_0+11\nu(G)-3|E(G)|$. Hence $s(H)\ge0$, with equality if and only if $H$ is a disjoint union of edges (a matching without isolated vertices), and $s(H)\ge1$ if $H$ is connected with $m\ge2$. In particular I11 holds for every linear 3-graph of maximum degree at most 2; (P2) and (P3) are not needed.

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (Additivity; L013 Step 0.1.) $n$, $m$, $\tau$ and $s$ are additive over disjoint unions, and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are exactly the unions of minimum transversals of the parts. *Proof.* The restriction of a transversal of $H_1\sqcup H_2$ to $V(H_i)$ is a transversal of $H_i$, and a union of transversals of the parts is a transversal of the union; so $\tau(H_1\sqcup H_2)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$, and a transversal of size $\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)$ restricts to minimum transversals of both parts. $\square$ Values: an isolated vertex has $s=3$; $s(E_1)=9+2-11=0$; the empty 3-graph has $s=0$.

0.2 (Heredity; L013 Step 0.1.) Deleting vertices (with their edges) or edges preserves (P1), (P2) and (P3): an edge-subset of a linear system is linear, a copy of $F_7$ in a subhypergraph is a copy in $H$, and $e(S)$ can only decrease. So every induced subgraph $H-Z$, every component and every $H\setminus D$ of a member of $C$ lies in $C$.

0.3 For $Z\subseteq V(H)$: $\tau(H)-|Z|\le\tau(H-Z)\le\tau(H)$. *Proof.* If $T$ is a transversal of $H$, $T\setminus Z$ is a transversal of $H-Z$; if $T'$ is a transversal of $H-Z$, $T'\cup Z$ is a transversal of $H$. $\square$

0.4 $X\subseteq V(H)$ is co-transversal in $H$ iff $\tau(H-X)=\tau(H)-|X|$; an isolated vertex is never co-transversal. *Proof.* If $T\supseteq X$ is a minimum transversal, $T\setminus X$ is a transversal of $H-X$ of size $\tau(H)-|X|$, so equality holds by 0.3. Conversely a minimum transversal $T'$ of $H-X$ of size $\tau(H)-|X|$ is disjoint from $X$, and $T'\cup X$ is a transversal of $H$ of size $\tau(H)$. A minimum transversal containing an isolated vertex $x$ would remain a transversal without $x$. $\square$

0.5 (Slack under deletion.) $s(H-Z)=s(H)-3|Z|-2|\partial_H(Z)|+11\,(\tau(H)-\tau(H-Z))$, because $n(H-Z)=n-|Z|$ and $m(H-Z)=m-|\partial_H(Z)|$.

0.6 (Linearity.) In a linear 3-graph two distinct vertices lie in at most one common edge (two such edges would share two vertices); hence the edges at a vertex $v$ pairwise meet only in $v$, the co-vertices of $v$ in different edges are distinct, and $|\partial_H(x,y)|=d_H(x)+d_H(y)-\sigma_H(x,y)$.

0.7 (Components.) A component of a 3-graph is an isolated vertex or has at least 3 vertices and an edge. A connected 3-graph with at least 2 vertices has no isolated vertex. If $H$ is connected and $\emptyset\ne Z\subsetneq V(H)$, every component $K$ of $H-Z$ contains a vertex of an edge of $\partial_H(Z)$: some edge of $H$ meets both $V(K)$ and $V(H)\setminus V(K)$; it is not an edge of $H-Z$ (an edge of $H-Z$ meeting $V(K)$ lies in $V(K)$), so it meets $Z$. If $H$ is moreover linear and $n\ge2$, a vertex $u\ne v$ is isolated in $H-v$ iff $u$ is a co-vertex of $v$ with $d_H(u)=1$: $u$ is isolated in $H-v$ iff every edge at $u$ contains $v$; at most one edge contains $u$ and $v$ (0.6), and $d_H(u)\ge1$.

0.8 (L016 Theorem 1 [verified-here].) Every member of $C$ with at most 27 vertices satisfies I11.

0.9 (Hall's theorem [published].) A bipartite graph with parts $A$, $B'$ has a matching saturating $A$ iff $|N(S)|\ge|S|$ for every $S\subseteq A$.

### Step 1. Proof of Theorem 2 (R) and (R+)
1.1 (R). Let $T_R$ be a minimum transversal of $H-Z$. Every edge of $H$ either is an edge of $H-Z$ (met by $T_R$) or lies in $\partial_H(Z)$ (met by $T$), so $T_R\cup T$ is a transversal of $H$ and $\tau(H)\le\tau(H-Z)+|T|$. With 0.5: $s(H)=s(H-Z)+3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|-11(\tau(H)-\tau(H-Z))\ge s(H-Z)+3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|-11|T|$. $\square$

1.2 (R+). The edge set of $H$ is the disjoint union of $E(R)$, the $E(F_i)$ and $E^*$: an edge not in $R$ meets $W$; if it lies inside some $W_i$ it is an edge of the induced subgraph $F_i$, otherwise it is in $E^*$; edges inside $W_i$ and inside $W_j$ ($i\ne j$) are different, and an edge of $R$ misses $W$. Hence $m(H)=m(R)+\sum_im(F_i)+|E^*|$, and $n(H)=n(R)+\sum_in(F_i)$. Let $T_R$ be a minimum transversal of $R$. Then $T=T_R\cup T_0\cup T_1\cup\dots\cup T_r$ meets every edge (edges of $R$ by $T_R$, of $F_i$ by $T_i$, of $E^*$ by some $T_i$ or by $T_0$), so $\tau(H)\le\tau(R)+\sum_i\tau(F_i)+|T_0|$. Therefore $s(H)\ge\bigl(3n(R)+2m(R)-11\tau(R)\bigr)+\sum_i\bigl(3n(F_i)+2m(F_i)-11\tau(F_i)\bigr)+2|E^*|-11|T_0|$. $\square$

1.3 (Consequences for a vertex-critical violator.) If $Z\ne\emptyset$, then $H-Z$ is a proper induced subgraph, $s(H-Z)\ge0$, and (R) with $s(H)<0$ gives $11|T|>3|Z|+2|\partial_H(Z)|+s(H-Z)$. If all $W_i\ne V(H)$, then $R$ ($W\ne\emptyset$) and every $F_i$ are proper induced subgraphs, so $s(R),s(F_i)\ge0$, and (R+) gives $11|T_0|>s(R)+\sum s(F_i)+2|E^*|\ge0$; in particular $T_0=\emptyset$ is impossible, i.e. no choice of minimum transversals $T_i$ meets every edge of $E^*$. $\square$

### Step 2. Proof of Theorem 2 (U)
2.1 Suppose $X$ is not co-transversal in $F$. By 0.3 and 0.4, $\tau(F-X)\ge\tau(F)-|X|+1$, so by 0.5 $s(F-X)\le s(F)-3|X|-2|\partial_F(X)|+11(|X|-1)=s(F)+8|X|-11-2|\partial_F(X)|$, which is negative under the hypothesis $s(F)<2|\partial_F(X)|+11-8|X|$; this contradicts $s(F-X)\ge0$. $\square$

2.2 For $X=\{x\}$, $|\partial_F(X)|=d_F(x)$ and the condition reads $s(F)<3+2d_F(x)$. If $x$ is isolated and $s(F)<3$, 2.1 would make $x$ co-transversal, which is impossible (0.4); so $s(F-x)\ge0$ forces $s(F)\ge3$. For $X=\{x,y\}$ the condition reads $s(F)<2|\partial_F(x,y)|-5$; its contrapositive is the pair lemma. $\square$

### Step 3. Proof of Theorem 2 (H)
3.1 Suppose $\varphi$ and the $x_e$ exist. For $e\in E^*$, put $i=\varphi(e)$; $F_i-x_e$ is a proper induced subgraph of $H$ (as $F_i$ is an induced subgraph of $H$), so $s(F_i-x_e)\ge0$, and (U) with $s(F_i)<3+2d_{F_i}(x_e)$ gives a minimum transversal $T_i\ni x_e$ of $F_i$. As $\varphi$ is injective, each $i$ is used at most once; for $i$ outside the image take any minimum transversal $T_i$. Every $e\in E^*$ contains $x_e\in T_{\varphi(e)}$, so (R+) applies with $T_0=\emptyset$ and 1.3 gives a contradiction. $\square$

3.2 If $s(F_i)\le2$, then $F_i$ has no isolated vertex (an isolated $x$ would give $s(F_i)\ge3$ by 2.2, as $s(F_i-x)\ge0$), so $d_{F_i}(x)\ge1$ and $s(F_i)\le2<5\le3+2d_{F_i}(x)$ for every $x\in W_i$. If $s(F_i)\le4$ and $F_i$ has no isolated vertex, again $s(F_i)<5\le3+2d_{F_i}(x)$. In both cases any matching of $B$ saturating $E^*$ is an injective $\varphi$ with $\varphi(e)$ adjacent to $e$, and any $x_e\in e\cap W_{\varphi(e)}$ satisfies the condition of 3.1; so no such matching exists, and Hall's theorem (0.9) gives the deficient set $S$. $\square$

3.3 (No circularity.) In 1.3, 2.1–2.2 (applied with $F$ an induced subgraph of $H$) and 3.1–3.2 the graphs whose slack is assumed nonnegative are $H-Z$ ($Z\ne\emptyset$), $R$, the $F_i$ ($W_i\ne V(H)$) and $F-X$ ($X\ne\emptyset$): all are proper induced subgraphs of $H$. For a minimal counterexample they lie in $C$ (0.2) and have fewer vertices than $H$, so I11 holds for them by the definition of a minimal counterexample. Nothing else is assumed. $\square$

### Step 4. Proof of Theorem 7 (maximum degree 2)
4.1 ($G$ is simple and subcubic.) A vertex $x$ of degree 2 lies in exactly two edges $e\ne f$ and gives the edge $ef$ of $G$ (not a loop). Two distinct vertices giving the same pair $\{e,f\}$ would both lie in $e\cap f$, contradicting linearity; so $G$ is simple and $|E(G)|=n_2(H)$. The $G$-degree of $e$ is the number of degree-2 vertices in $e$, at most 3.

4.2 ($\tau(H)=m-\nu(G)$.) *Upper bound.* Let $M$ be a maximum matching of $G$. Take the vertex of $H$ that gives each edge of $M$, and one arbitrary vertex of each edge of $H$ not covered by $M$ (as a vertex of $G$). This set meets every edge of $H$ and has at most $|M|+(m-2|M|)=m-\nu(G)$ elements. *Lower bound.* Let $T$ be a minimum transversal of $H$; it has no isolated vertex (0.4). Let $F\subseteq E(G)$ be the set of $G$-edges given by the degree-2 vertices of $T$, $T_1$ the set of degree-1 vertices of $T$ (so $|T|=|F|+|T_1|$), $U\subseteq V(G)$ the set of ends of edges of $F$, and $k$ the number of components of the graph $(U,F)$. Then $|F|\ge|U|-k$, and one edge from each of these components forms a matching, so $k\le\nu(G)$. An edge $e$ of $H$ with $e\notin U$ contains no degree-2 vertex of $T$ (that vertex would put $e$ into $U$), so it contains a vertex of $T_1$; a degree-1 vertex lies in one edge only, so $|T_1|\ge m-|U|$. Hence $\tau(H)=|T|\ge(|U|-k)+(m-|U|)=m-k\ge m-\nu(G)$. $\square$
(No correction term is needed: an edge of $H$ all of whose vertices have degree 1 is an isolated vertex of $G$; it contributes 1 to $m$, 0 to $\nu(G)$, and needs one vertex of a transversal.)

4.3 (Slack.) $3m=\sum_xd_H(x)=n_1+2n_2$ and $n=n_0+n_1+n_2$, so $s(H)=3n+2m-11(m-\nu)=3n-9m+11\nu=3n_0+3n_1+3n_2-3n_1-6n_2+11\nu=3n_0+11\nu(G)-3|E(G)|$. $\square$

4.4 ($11\nu(G)\ge3|E(G)|$ for simple $G$ with $\Delta(G)\le3$.) Let $N=|V(G)|$, $M=|E(G)|$. By the Tutte–Berge formula there is $U\subseteq V(G)$ with $2\nu(G)=N+|U|-o(G-U)$, $o$ = number of components of $G-U$ with an odd number of vertices. Let $C_1,\dots,C_q$ be the components of $G-U$, $c_i=|V(C_i)|$, $e_i=|E(C_i)|$. Every edge of $G$ lies in some $C_i$ or meets $U$, and at most $3|U|$ edges meet $U$; so $M\le3|U|+\sum_ie_i$, while $N=|U|+\sum_ic_i$. Hence
$$2\nu(G)-\tfrac{6}{11}M\;\ge\;2|U|+\sum_ic_i-o-\tfrac{18}{11}|U|-\tfrac{6}{11}\sum_ie_i\;=\;\tfrac4{11}|U|+\sum_it_i,\qquad t_i:=c_i-[c_i\text{ odd}]-\tfrac6{11}e_i .$$
Since $C_i$ is simple with maximum degree $\le3$, $e_i\le\min\bigl(\binom{c_i}2,\lfloor3c_i/2\rfloor\bigr)$, and therefore: $c_i=1$: $t_i=0$; $c_i=2$: $t_i\ge2-\tfrac6{11}$; $c_i=3$: $t_i\ge2-\tfrac{18}{11}=\tfrac4{11}$; $c_i=4$: $t_i\ge4-\tfrac{36}{11}=\tfrac8{11}$; $c_i=5$: $t_i\ge4-\tfrac{42}{11}=\tfrac2{11}$; even $c_i\ge6$: $t_i\ge c_i-\tfrac{9c_i}{11}>0$; odd $c_i\ge7$: $t_i\ge c_i-1-\tfrac{9c_i-3}{11}=\tfrac{2c_i-8}{11}>0$. So $t_i\ge0$, with equality only for $c_i=1$, and $11\nu(G)\ge3M$. If $M\ge1$ the inequality is strict: if $U\ne\emptyset$ then $\tfrac4{11}|U|>0$; if $U=\emptyset$ then some component of $G$ has an edge, so $c_i\ge2$ and $t_i>0$. $\square$

4.5 (Conclusion.) By 4.3 and 4.4, $s(H)\ge3n_0\ge0$, with $s(H)=0$ iff $n_0=0$ and $E(G)=\emptyset$, i.e. no vertex has degree 0 or 2, i.e. $H$ is a disjoint union of edges. If $H$ is connected with $m\ge2$, then $n_0=0$ and some vertex has degree 2 (otherwise the edges would be pairwise disjoint and $H$ disconnected), so $E(G)\neq\emptyset$, $11\nu(G)-3|E(G)|\ge1$ (a positive integer) and $s(H)\ge1$. Only linearity was used. $\square$

4.6 (Remarks.) (a) K10 as recorded in kb/known_results.md ([published], Henning–Yeo EJC 24(2) P2.50: $18\tau\le4n+4m+2$ for connected linear 3-graphs with $\Delta\le2$) would also give I11 for such $H$ with $n\ge5$, since $3m\le2n$ gives $11(4n+4m+2)\le18(3n+2m)\iff8m+22\le10n$; Theorem 7 does not use it. (b) The bound of 4.4 is not tight for $M>0$: over all 52,662 graphs with $\Delta\le3$ on at most 12 vertices the least positive value of $11\nu-3M$ is 1, attained by $K_4$ with one edge subdivided ($N=5$, $M=7$, $\nu=2$; C1); its dual 3-graph (8 vertices, 5 edges, $\tau=3$) has $s=1$.

### Step 5. Proof of Theorem 1
5.1 (a) If $H=H_1\sqcup H_2$ with both parts nonempty, both are proper induced subgraphs, so $s(H)=s(H_1)+s(H_2)\ge0$ (0.1), a contradiction; so $H$ is connected (the empty 3-graph has $s=0$, so $n\ge1$). If $m=0$ then $s=3n\ge0$; so $m\ge1$, $n\ge3$, and $H$ has no isolated vertex (0.7). $n\ge28$ by 0.8, since $H\in C$ and $s(H)<0$.

5.2 (b) Apply (U) with $F=H$ and $X=\{v\}$: $H-v$ is a proper induced subgraph, so $s(H-v)\ge0$, and $s(H)<0<3+2d_H(v)$. So $v$ is co-transversal and $\tau(H-v)=\tau-1$ (0.4); then 0.5 gives $s(H-v)=s-3-2d_H(v)+11=s+8-2d_H(v)$, which is $\ge0$.

5.3 (c) From (b), $2d_H(v)\le s+8\le7$, so $d_H(v)\le3$.

5.4 (d) If $\Delta(H)\le2$, Theorem 7 ($H$ is linear) gives $s(H)\ge0$, a contradiction. With (c), $\Delta(H)=3$.

5.5 (e) For a vertex $v$ of degree 3 (it exists by (d)), (b) gives $0\le s(H-v)=s+2$, so $s\ge-2$; and $s\le-1$.

5.6 (f) Apply (U) with $F=H$ and $X$ ($H-X$ is a proper induced subgraph). For $X=\{x,y\}$ the condition is $2|\partial_H(x,y)|>s+5$, i.e. $|\partial_H(x,y)|\ge3$ when $s=-1$ and $|\partial_H(x,y)|\ge2$ when $s=-2$. Two vertices of degree $\ge2$ have $|\partial_H(x,y)|\ge2+2-1=3$ (0.6).

5.7 (g) Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample with $m(H)$ least, and $D$ a nonempty set of $k$ edges. $H\setminus D\in C$ (0.2) has $n$ vertices; every member of $C$ with fewer vertices satisfies I11, so if $s(H\setminus D)<0$ then $H\setminus D$ would be a minimal counterexample with fewer edges than $H$; hence $s(H\setminus D)\ge0$, i.e. $11\tau(H\setminus D)\le3n+2m-2k=11\tau+s-2k$, and as $\tau(H\setminus D)$ is an integer, $\tau(H\setminus D)\le\tau-\lceil(2k-s)/11\rceil$. For $k=1$, $\lceil(2-s)/11\rceil=1$ ($s\in\{-1,-2\}$), and $\tau(H\setminus e)\ge\tau-1$ (add a vertex of $e$ to a transversal of $H\setminus e$); so $\tau(H\setminus e)=\tau-1$. A minimum transversal $T'$ of $H\setminus e$ is disjoint from $e$ (otherwise it would be a transversal of $H$ of size $\tau-1$), so $T'\cup\{x\}$ is a minimum transversal of $H$ meeting $e$ exactly in $x$. $\square$

If $d'(a)=d'(b)=1$ and $d'(c)=2$: $\sigma_F(a,c)=\sigma_F(b,c)=1$, so the only $F$-edges at $a$ and at $b$ both contain $c$, and they are different edges by (i); if also $d'(e)=2$, both would also contain $e$, and two different edges would share $\{c,e\}$, contradicting linearity; so $d'(e)=1$. Hence at most one of $a,b,c,e$ has $d'=2$, and it shares an $F$-edge with both vertices of the other side.
(e) If $d'(e)=2$: $d'(a)=d'(b)=1$ and $\sigma_F(a,e)=\sigma_F(b,e)=1$. If $d'(e)=1$: $d'(a)\le1+\sigma_F(a,e)$, $d'(b)\le1+\sigma_F(b,e)$, and the only $F$-edge at $e$ contains at most one of $a,b$ (by (i)), so one of $a,b$ has $d'=1$, i.e. $H$-degree 2. ## Relation to the Henning–Yeo method
14.1 *The template* [published preprint, arXiv:1802.01825 §7; kb/literature/sources/henning_yeo/henning_yeo_1802.01825_sec7_deficiency.txt]. For 4-uniform linear hypergraphs with $\Delta\le3$, Henning and Yeo prove a key theorem $45\tau\le6n+13m+{\rm def}(H)$, where ${\rm def}(H)=\max_X\bigl(\sum_{F\in X}w(F)-13|E^*(X)|\bigr)$ over families $X$ of vertex-disjoint copies of fifteen special hypergraphs (each with a weight $w(F)$ compensating its violation of the plain inequality), by a minimal counterexample on $n+m$: Claim A (connected, $\delta\ge1$) is our Theorem 1(a); Claim B (no minimum $X$-transversal meets $E^*(X)$, else induct on $H-V(X)$) is our Theorem 2(R+)/1.3; Claims D–E (Hall's theorem on the bipartite graph $X$ versus $E^*(X)$, with $X$ minimising $|E^*(X)|-|X|$, give $|E^*(X)|\ge|X|+1$ for every nonempty $X$) correspond to our Lemma (H) and Theorem 4(iii); Claim H ($|E^*(X)|\ge|X|+2$) uses $X$-universal edges and a *replacement graph* $H'$ in which $V(X)$ is replaced by one new edge on the boundary $\partial(X)$ (allowed to be non-linear and treated separately); the rest is a long local case analysis resting on computer-verified transversal properties of the special hypergraphs (their Observation 1: (g) every vertex lies in a minimum transversal, (h), (k), (ℓ), (m), (n): two or more prescribed vertices or sets can be met simultaneously). Our Theorem 2(U) is the analogue of Observation 1(g) for every low-slack member of $C$ (proved, not computed), the pair lemma bounds the exceptions to the analogue of (h), and Remark 13.2 / C5 are the analogue of Observation 1(h),(k) for the known tight graphs.
14.2 *What does not transfer.* (a) The replacement step of Claim H adds an edge on boundary vertices; the result must stay in a class where minimality applies, and adding an edge to a member of $C$ can create $F_7$ or violate (P3). (b) For I11 on $C$ no deficiency is needed if I11 is true, but the minimal-counterexample analysis then never uses (P2), (P3) (Step 10), which makes a contradiction impossible from Theorems 1–5 alone.
14.3 *A deficiency form that would suffice* [derived-here; the key theorem itself is a conjecture]. Fix $0<t<3$ and a family $\mathcal S$ of connected 3-graphs; for a 3-graph $H$ let ${\rm def}_t(H)=\max\bigl(0,\max_X[\sum_{F\in X}w_t(F)-(2+t)|E^*(X)|]\bigr)$, the inner maximum over families $X$ of vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs isomorphic to members of $\mathcal S$, with $w_t(F)=11\tau(F)-(3-t)n(F)-(2+t)m(F)$. *Key theorem* (KT$_t$) [conjecture]: $11\tau(H)\le(3-t)n+(2+t)m+{\rm def}_t(H)$ for every $H$ in a class $\mathcal K\supseteq C$ (for instance linear $F_7$-free 3-graphs with $\Delta\le3$, a class in which the minimal counterexample may use replacement steps that leave $C$). *Claim:* if every member of $\mathcal S$ that occurs as an induced subgraph of a member of $C$ satisfies I11, then ${\rm def}_t(H)\le t(n-m)$ for every $H\in C$, and so KT$_t$ implies I11 on $C$. *Proof.* $X=\emptyset$ gives $0\le t(n-m)$ ($m\le n-2$ for $n\ge2$ by (P3), $m=0$ for $n\le1$). For $X\ne\emptyset$: each $F\in X$ lies in $C$ (0.2) and $w_t(F)=-s(F)+t(n(F)-m(F))\le t(n(F)-m(F))$. With $R=H-V(X)$, $n-m=\sum_F(n(F)-m(F))+(n(R)-m(R))-|E^*(X)|$ (the edge partition of 1.2) and $n(R)-m(R)\ge0$ ((P3) for $R\in C$), so $\sum_Fw_t(F)-(2+t)|E^*(X)|\le t(n-m)+t|E^*(X)|-(2+t)|E^*(X)|=t(n-m)-2|E^*(X)|\le t(n-m)$. Then $11\tau\le(3-t)n+(2+t)m+t(n-m)=3n+2m$. $\square$ *Remarks.* The (P3) "room" enters exactly through $n(R)-m(R)\ge0$ and $m\le n-2$. For KT$_t$ to be a finite framework, $\mathcal S$ must contain every connected $F$ in $\mathcal K$ with $s(F)<t(n(F)-m(F))$ that does not decompose: for every $t>0$ this includes $E_1$ ($w_t=2t$) and all tight graphs ($w_t=t(n-m)$: $3t$ for $(10,7,4)$, $2t$ for $(14,12,6)$, $4t$ for $(17,13,7)$), for $t>1/2$ also the slack-1 graphs $(12,10,5)$ ($w_t=2t-1$), and in $\mathcal K\setminus C$ the I11-violators such as $F_7$ and the $(9,8,4)$ graphs; so a finiteness statement of the type of Conjecture T is needed in any case.
