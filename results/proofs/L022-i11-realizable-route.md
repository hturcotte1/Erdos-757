## Statement

**Conventions.** As in L017: 3-graphs $H$, $n,m,\tau$, $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$ (I11 holds for $H$ iff $s(H)\ge0$; tight means $s=0$), $H-Z$ the induced subgraph on $V(H)\setminus Z$, $H\setminus D$ the deletion of a set $D$ of edges (all vertices kept), $H[Y]$ the induced subgraph on $Y$, $\partial_H(Z)$ the edges meeting $Z$, $\sigma_H(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ whether $x,y$ share an edge, *co-transversal* = contained in some minimum transversal, $E_1$ = a single edge on 3 vertices. A *pendant* is a vertex of degree 1. A *linear vertex-critical violator* is a linear 3-graph $H$ with $s(H)<0$ all of whose proper induced subgraphs have $s\ge0$. For a finite set $A\subset\mathbb R$, $H(A)$ is the 3-graph on $A$ whose edges are the 3-term APs of $A$; with $A=\{x_0<x_1<\dots<x_{n-1}\}$ we write an AP as a rank triple $(a,b,c)$, $a<b<c$, $x_b=(x_a+x_c)/2$; $\mu(e)=b$ is its *midpoint*, $x_b-x_a$ its *step*; $n_L(v),n_M(v),n_R(v)$ count the APs in which $v$ is the left endpoint, the midpoint, the right endpoint ($d(v)=n_L+n_M+n_R$). $R$ is the class of 3-graphs isomorphic to $H(A)$ for a finite (4,5)-set $A$; $C$ is the class of L007-L018 (linear, $F_7$-free, $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for $|S|\ge2$). A *minimal counterexample in $R$* is an $H\in R$ with $s(H)<0$ and the least number of vertices; *$(n,m)$-minimal* if moreover $m(H)$ is least among those.

**Theorem 1 ($R$ is closed under sub-hypergraphs).**
(a) (Heredity) If $A$ is a (4,5)-set and $B\subseteq A$, then $B$ is a (4,5)-set and $H(B)=H(A)[B]$. So $R$ is closed under deleting vertices, and every component $F$ of $H(A)-Z$ is $H(V(F))$ exactly (no extra APs).
(b) (Independence of the AP relations) Let $A=\{x_0<\dots<x_{n-1}\}$ be a finite set of reals in which every point is the midpoint of at most one 3-AP (true for every weak Sidon set, in particular every (4,5)-set). Then the $m$ linear forms $\ell_e=X_a+X_c-2X_b$, one per AP $e=(a,b,c)$ of $A$, are linearly independent over $\mathbb R$. Equivalently the AP equation system of $A$ has rank $m$, and its solution space has dimension $n-m$.
(b') (Rigidity of saturated sets) If $A$ is a (4,5)-set with $m=n-2$ APs, every set $A'$ of reals with the same labelled AP system (the same triples with the same midpoints, as equations on the ranks) is an affine image $qA+t$. (This explains the rigidity observed in all 54,243 extremal 12-point and all 2,910 saturated extremal 14-point classes, K41, K45.)
(c) (Edge deletion) If $A$ is a (4,5)-set and $D\subseteq E(H(A))$, then there is a (4,5)-set $A'$ of integers, with the same number of points in the same order, whose AP hypergraph is exactly $H(A)\setminus D$ (same rank labels and the same midpoints). So $R$ is closed under deleting edges.
(d) $R$ is closed under adding an isolated vertex and under disjoint unions.
(e) Consequently $R\subseteq C$, $R$ is closed under taking arbitrary sub-hypergraphs (delete any vertices and any edges), and $R$ is the class of 3-graphs containing no member of $\mathcal M_R$ as a sub-hypergraph, where $\mathcal M_R$ is the set of 3-graphs not in $R$ all of whose proper sub-hypergraphs are in $R$. Every member of $\mathcal M_R$ is connected, has no isolated vertex, and has at least 5 edges if it is linear (K33); $F_7\in\mathcal M_R$ (Step 1.5).
(f) Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample in $R$. Then $H$ is a linear vertex-critical violator with $n\ge28$, so L017 Theorems 1(a)-(f), 2, 3, 4, 5 hold for $H$ verbatim (L017 Step 10.1; "$n\ge28$" from $R\subseteq C$ and L016). If $H$ is $(n,m)$-minimal, then also L017 Theorem 1(g) holds: $\tau(H\setminus D)\le\tau(H)-\lceil(2|D|-s(H))/11\rceil$ for every nonempty $D\subseteq E(H)$; in particular $\tau(H\setminus e)=\tau(H)-1$ for every edge, and for every edge $e$ and $x\in e$ some minimum transversal meets $e$ exactly in $x$.

**Theorem 2 (sub-extension lemma; for every linear vertex-critical violator, hence for minimal counterexamples in $C$ and in $R$).** Let $H$ be a linear vertex-critical violator, $v$ a vertex of degree 3 with edges $\{v\}\cup P_j$ ($j=1,2,3$), $G=H-v$ with components $F_1,\dots,F_r$, and use the patterns (a), (b), (c1), (c2), (d), (e), (f) of L017 Theorem 4(iii) with its naming of vertices ((e): $P_1\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2\subseteq V(F_2)$, $P_3=\{e,f\}$, $e\in V(F_1)$, $f\in V(F_2)$; (f): $P_1=\{a,b\}\subseteq V(F_1)$, $P_2=\{c,d\}$, $P_3=\{e,f\}$, $c,e\in V(F_1)$, $d,f\in V(F_2)$; (b): $P_j=\{x_j,y_j\}$, $x_j\in V(F_1)$, $y_j\in V(F_2)$).
(i) (= L018 Theorem 6(b'), re-proved) If $r\ge2$, no component of $G$ contains two of the pairs. Hence (c1), (c2), (d) do not occur, and $r\le2$.
(ii) Pattern (e) does not occur.
(iii) In pattern (f): $s(F_1)=1$, $s(F_2)=0$ and $s(H)=-1$; the induced subgraphs $H[V(F_1)\cup\{v,d\}]$ and $H[V(F_1)\cup\{v,f\}]$ are tight.
(iv) In pattern (f): either $F_2$ is the single edge $\{d,f,z\}$ (for some vertex $z$), or $d$ and $f$ both have degree 1 in $F_2$ and lie in different edges of $F_2$ (so $F_2$ is a tight connected graph, not a single edge, with at least two pendant vertices).
(v) In pattern (b): if the three attachment vertices $y_1,y_2,y_3$ of $F_2$ form an edge of $F_2$, then $F_2$ is that single edge; symmetrically for $F_1$; and not both (else $H=F_7$).
(vi) (L017 Theorem 4(vi), restated) In pattern (b), a tight component that is not a single edge contains at least two attachment vertices of degree 1 in that component (so it has at least two pendant vertices).
(vii) (Degree-2 co-vertices; every pattern, including (a).) If a co-vertex $w$ of $v$ has $d_H(w)=2$, lies in the component $F$ of $G$, and $\{w,p,q\}$ is its edge in $F$, then neither $\{w,p\}$ nor $\{w,q\}$ is co-transversal in $F$; equivalently, no minimum transversal of $F-w$ contains $p$ or $q$. In particular (pair lemma) $d_F(p),d_F(q)\le2$ if $F$ is tight.
So for every vertex $v$ of degree 3 exactly one of the following holds: $H-v$ is connected (pattern (a)); or $r=2$ and the pattern is (b) with (v), (vi); or $r=2$, $s(H)=-1$ and the pattern is (f) with (iii), (iv); and (vii) holds in each case.

**Corollary 3 (degree-3 configurations of a minimal counterexample in $C$ or in $R$).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample in $C$ or in $R$ ($n\ge28$, connected, $\Delta(H)=3$, $s(H)\in\{-1,-2\}$ by L017 Theorem 1 and Theorem 1(f)). For every vertex $v$ of degree 3, $H-v$ is connected, or the pattern at $v$ is (b) or (f) as in Theorem 2. If $s(H)=-2$, the pattern at every vertex of degree 3 is (a), or (b) with both components tight, each of them a single edge or containing at least two pendant vertices, not both single edges. Under **Conjecture P2'** (every connected tight member of $C$ — resp. of $R$ — with maximum degree $\le3$, other than a single edge, has at most one vertex of degree 1): if $s(H)=-2$ every vertex of degree 3 has pattern (a) (this is L018 Proposition 6.2); if $s(H)=-1$, pattern (b) forces one component to be a single edge and the other to have slack 1, and pattern (f) forces $F_2$ to be a single edge.

**Theorem 4 (order facts for a (4,5)-set $A=\{x_0<\dots<x_{n-1}\}$; the section "What the order structure buys" discusses their use).**
(O1) $x_0$ and $x_{n-1}$ are never midpoints; $x_0$ is the left endpoint of all its APs and $x_{n-1}$ the right endpoint of all its APs.
(O2) $x_1$ is never a right endpoint, and it is a midpoint iff $2x_1-x_0\in A$ (of the AP $(x_0,x_1,2x_1-x_0)$); symmetrically $x_{n-2}$ is never a left endpoint and is a midpoint iff $2x_{n-2}-x_{n-1}\in A$.
(O3) (Distinct steps) The $m$ APs of $A$ have pairwise distinct steps. Equivalently every positive difference occurs at most twice in $A-A$, and exactly the $m$ steps occur twice.
(O4) (K30 at a vertex) The APs with left endpoint $v$, ordered by their right endpoints, have strictly increasing midpoints; likewise for right endpoint $v$. For the APs $(a_i,b_i,x_{n-1})$ at the maximum: $a_1<a_2<\dots$ iff $b_1<b_2<\dots$, and the steps $x_{n-1}-x_{b_i}$ are distinct (O3).
(O5) (Half-line) Every AP $(a,b,n-1)$ at the maximum has $x_b\ge(x_0+x_{n-1})/2$, with equality iff $a=0$; symmetrically at the minimum. At most one AP contains both $x_0$ and $x_{n-1}$.
(O6) (Reflection relations) A vertex $v$ is the midpoint of at most one of its APs; in every other AP $e\ni v$ one has $x_v=2x_{\mu(e)}-x_u$, $u$ the other endpoint. If $d(v)=3$ with pairs $P_1,P_2,P_3$, the points of $A\setminus\{v\}$ satisfy two linearly independent relations: $2x_{\mu_1}-x_{u_1}=2x_{\mu_2}-x_{u_2}=w_3$ with $w_3=2x_{\mu_3}-x_{u_3}$ if $v$ is a midpoint of none of its APs and $w_3=(x_p+x_q)/2$ if $v$ is the midpoint of $\{p,v,q\}$; and, by Theorem 1(b), the solution space of the AP equations of $H(A)$ has dimension exactly 2 less than that of $H(A)-v$.
(O7) (K31) No triangle of APs (three APs pairwise meeting in three distinct vertices) has all three of its non-shared vertices as midpoints. In particular, in pattern (f) with $F_2=\{d,f,z\}$ the APs $\{v,c,d\},\{v,e,f\},\{d,f,z\}$ do not have midpoints $c,e,z$; in pattern (b) with $F_2=\{y_1,y_2,y_3\}$ and $y_2$ its midpoint, $x_1$ and $x_3$ are not both the midpoints of their APs $\{v,x_1,y_1\}$, $\{v,x_3,y_3\}$.

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 Step 0.1-0.7.) $n,m,\tau,s$ are additive over disjoint unions and the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts; an isolated vertex has $s=3$, $E_1$ has $s=0$; $\tau(H)-|Z|\le\tau(H-Z)\le\tau(H)$; $X$ is co-transversal iff $\tau(H-X)=\tau(H)-|X|$; a component is an isolated vertex or has $\ge3$ vertices.
0.2 (Weak Sidon and (4,5).) For a finite set $Y$ of distinct reals: $Y$ is a (4,5)-set iff $Y$ is weak Sidon (the sums $y+y'$, $y\ne y'$, are pairwise distinct) and no two 3-APs of $Y$ share two points. *Proof.* For a 4-set $B$ the 6 positive differences are distinct except for coincidences $y_b-y_a=y_d-y_c$ with $(a,b)\ne(c,d)$, i.e. $y_b+y_c=y_a+y_d$. If $b=c$ this is the AP $(a,b,d)$; if $a=d$ it is the AP $(c,a,b)$ with midpoint $a$; if $b\ne c$ and $a\ne d$ it is a coincidence of two sums of distinct elements over different pairs ($\{b,c\}=\{a,d\}$ would force $b=d$, $c=a$, i.e. $(a,b)=(c,d)$, or $b=a$), i.e. a weak Sidon violation. So if $Y$ is weak Sidon, the coincidences inside a 4-set are exactly its 3-APs, and $|B-B|\ge11$ (at most one coincidence) fails iff $B$ contains two APs, which then share two points. Conversely a weak Sidon violation $y_a+y_b=y_c+y_d$ with $\{a,b\}\ne\{c,d\}$, $a\ne b$, $c\ne d$ has $\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}=\emptyset$ (a common element would give a repeated point), so $B=\{a,b,c,d\}$ has the coincidences $y_a-y_c=y_d-y_b$ and $y_a-y_d=y_c-y_b$, two coincidences; and two APs sharing two points lie in a 4-set (or are equal). [K3, K6; derived-here proof.] $\square$
0.3 (K5.) In a (4,5)-set, and more generally in a weak Sidon set, every point is the midpoint of at most one AP ($2x_b=x_a+x_c=x_{a'}+x_{c'}$ would be a weak Sidon violation), and no point is the midpoint of an AP inside a subset $S$ if it is $\min S$ or $\max S$. Hence $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for $|S|\ge2$.
0.4 (L017 Step 10.1.) L017 Theorems 1(a) (without "$n\ge28$"), 1(b)-(f), 2, 3, 4 and 5 (without "$n-2\ge26$") hold for every linear vertex-critical violator. In particular, for a vertex $v$ of degree 3 of such an $H$, L017 Theorem 4 gives: (i) the six co-vertices are distinct, have $H$-degree 2 or 3, and no $P_j$ lies inside an edge of $G=H-v$; (ii) $\tau(G)=\tau(H)-1$, $s(G)=s(H)+2\in\{0,1\}$, $G$ has no isolated vertex, every component $F_i$ has $s(F_i)\ge0$, $\sum_is(F_i)=s(H)+2$, and every $F_i$ contains a co-vertex of $v$; (iii) ($\star$) no minimum transversal of $G$ meets all three pairs, and every vertex of every $F_i$ is co-transversal in $F_i$; the patterns are (a), (b), (c1), (c2), (d), (e), (f); (iv) the equivalent conditions, of which we use: (b) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ contains two of $x_1,x_2,x_3$ (and likewise in $F_2$); (e) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets $P_1$ and contains $e$, and no minimum transversal of $F_2$ meets $P_2$ and contains $f$; (f) no minimum transversal of $F_1$ meets both $P_1$ and $\{c,e\}$, and $\{d,f\}$ is not co-transversal in $F_2$; (v) the pair lemma: a pair $\{x,y\}$ that is not co-transversal in a component $F$ has $|\partial_F(x,y)|\le2$ if $F$ is tight and $\le3$ if $s(F)=1$.
0.5 (Edges of $H$.) Every edge of $H$ is either an edge of exactly one component $F_i$ of $G$ or one of the three edges at $v$. Hence for $Y\subseteq V(G)$ the edges of $H[Y\cup\{v\}]$ are the edges of the $F_i$ lying inside $Y$ and those edges $\{v\}\cup P_j$ with $P_j\subseteq Y$.

### Step 1. Proof of Theorem 1
1.1 (a) Every 4-subset of $B$ is a 4-subset of $A$, so $B$ is a (4,5)-set. A triple of $B$ is an AP of $B$ iff it is an AP of $A$ (the defining equation does not depend on the ambient set). So $E(H(B))=\{e\in E(H(A)):e\subseteq B\}$, i.e. $H(B)=H(A)[B]$. A component $F$ of $H(A)-Z$ is an induced subgraph of $H(A)$, so $F=H(A)[V(F)]=H(V(F))$. $\square$

1.2 (b) Write $e=(a(e),\mu(e),c(e))$. Let $N$ be the $m\times m$ matrix with $N[e][f]$ = coefficient of $X_{\mu(f)}$ in $\ell_e$. It suffices to show that $N$ is nonsingular (then the restrictions of the $\ell_e$ to the $m$ coordinates $\{X_{\mu(f)}\}$ are linearly independent, hence so are the $\ell_e$).
1.2.1 By injectivity of $\mu$, $N[e][e]=-2$, and for $f\ne e$: $N[e][f]=1$ if $\mu(f)\in\{a(e),c(e)\}$ and $0$ otherwise. Again by injectivity there is at most one AP $e^-$ with $\mu(e^-)=a(e)$ and at most one AP $e^+$ with $\mu(e^+)=c(e)$; they are different from each other and from $e$.
1.2.2 Let $y\in\mathbb R^m$ (indexed by the APs) with $Ny=0$, and suppose $y\ne0$. Put $\Lambda=\max_e|y_e|>0$; replacing $y$ by $-y$ we may assume $y_e=\Lambda$ for some $e$; among the APs with $y_e=\Lambda$ choose $e_0$ with $c(e_0)$ largest. Row $e_0$ of $Ny=0$ reads $2y_{e_0}=[e_0^-\text{ exists}]\,y_{e_0^-}+[e_0^+\text{ exists}]\,y_{e_0^+}$, so $2\Lambda\le\Lambda+\Lambda$ with equality only if $e_0^+$ exists and $y_{e_0^+}=\Lambda$. Then $c(e_0^+)>\mu(e_0^+)=c(e_0)$, contradicting the choice of $e_0$. So $y=0$ and $N$ is nonsingular. The rank statement and $\dim=n-m$ follow. $\square$
(Only injectivity of $\mu$ and $a(e)<\mu(e)<c(e)$ in a total order were used.)
1.2.3 (b') The solution space $V$ of the labelled AP equations of $A$ has dimension $n-m=2$ by (b), and it contains the vector $x=(x_0,\dots,x_{n-1})$ and the all-one vector $\mathbf 1$ (each $\ell_e$ has coefficient sum 0), which are linearly independent since the $x_i$ are distinct. So $V=\{qx+t\mathbf 1\}$, and every $A'$ satisfying the same equations is $qA+t$. $\square$

1.3 (c) Let $x=(x_0,\dots,x_{n-1})$, $E=E(H(A))$ (rank labels), $E'=E\setminus D$, and $V'=\{y\in\mathbb R^n:\ell_f(y)=0\ \forall f\in E'\}$, a subspace defined over $\mathbb Q$ with $x\in V'$. Let $\Phi$ be the finite set of linear forms: $X_i-X_j$ ($i\ne j$); $X_i+X_j-X_k-X_l$ ($i\ne j$, $k\ne l$, $\{i,j\}\ne\{k,l\}$); $X_i+X_k-2X_j$ ($i,j,k$ distinct).
1.3.1 If $\varphi\in\Phi$ vanishes identically on $V'$, then $\varphi(x)=0$. At $x$: no $X_i-X_j$ vanishes (distinct points), no $X_i+X_j-X_k-X_l$ vanishes (weak Sidon, 0.2), and $X_i+X_k-2X_j$ vanishes iff $(i,j,k)$ is an AP of $A$ with midpoint $j$, i.e. $\varphi=\ell_e$, $e\in E$. For $e\in D$, $\ell_e$ does not vanish identically on $V'$: otherwise $\ell_e\in(V')^\perp=\mathrm{span}\{\ell_f:f\in E'\}$, contradicting (b). So the forms of $\Phi$ vanishing identically on $V'$ are exactly the $\ell_f$, $f\in E'$.
1.3.2 Every other $\varphi\in\Phi$ vanishes on a proper subspace of $V'$. A finite union of proper subspaces of $V'$ has empty interior in $V'$, and the rational points of $V'$ are dense in $V'$; so there is $y\in V'\cap\mathbb Q^n$ arbitrarily close to $x$ at which no such $\varphi$ vanishes. Choosing $y$ so close that $y_0<y_1<\dots<y_{n-1}$ (the order of $x$), $y$ has distinct coordinates, is weak Sidon, and its 3-APs are exactly the triples of $E'$, with the same midpoints. $E'\subseteq E$ is linear, so by 0.2 $y$ is a (4,5)-set; multiplying by a common denominator gives integers. $\square$

## GAPs: what is not proved

The following remain open; together they are exactly what separates Corollary 3 from I11 on $R$ (and on $C$).

GAP 1 (pattern (a)). For a minimal counterexample $H$ (in $C$ or $R$) and a vertex $v$ of degree 3 with $H-v$ connected: $G=H-v$ is a connected member of the class with $\Delta\le3$, $n-1\ge27$ vertices and $s(G)=s+2\le1$, and no minimum transversal of $G$ meets the three pairs. To exclude this one needs a statement of the type of Conjecture C1 (experiments/20261027-0300-transversal-richness, L019) for connected graphs of slack $\le1$ with at least 27 vertices, or a classification of such graphs (Conjecture T''; L020). Neither the order (W2-W6) nor realizability (W7) was found to give it. In $R$ the extension must in addition satisfy the two reflection relations (O6), and no consequence of these for transversals is known.

GAP 2 (pattern (b)). Two components, all pairs split, each with three attachment vertices that pairwise lie in no common minimum transversal; a tight component is a single edge or has at least two pendants (Theorem 2(vi)); a slack-1 component has the pair-lemma bound $|\partial(x_j,x_k)|\le3$. By L017 Corollary 13.3 [computation, unrefereed], at least one component has at least 27 vertices or is a tight graph in the cell $(25,23,11)$. Excluding (b) needs (1) Conjecture P2' for tight members with at least 27 vertices (for at most 26 vertices P2' holds: on the stored lists, and for the cell $(25,23,11)$ by the degree count of L018 Remark 7.3; L018 Remark 5.4 [computation, unrefereed]), and (2) a statement that no connected slack-1 graph with $\Delta\le3$ and at least 27 vertices has three such attachment vertices (for at most 26 vertices the stored slack-1 lists have none: role_b is non-zero only for $E_1$).

GAP 3 (pattern (f)). $s=-1$, $F_1$ of slack 1 containing $P_1,c,e$ with no minimum transversal meeting both $P_1$ and $\{c,e\}$, $F_2$ tight, either a single edge or with two non-adjacent pendants $d,f$ (Theorem 2(iii), (iv)). By the role counts (role_f1 = 0 on all stored pieces) $F_1$ has at least 27 vertices. Excluding (f) needs P2' for tight graphs with at least 27 vertices (to make $F_2$ a single edge) and a statement that no connected slack-1 graph with $\Delta\le3$ and at least 27 vertices has such a configuration $(P_1;c,e)$.

GAP 4 (the uncomputed cell). L017 Corollary 13.3 leaves components in the tight cell $(25,23,11)$ with $\Delta\le3$, not computed in $C$; its realizable part is not computed either.

None of GAP 1-4 is a finite statement; each is a statement about all sizes. Hence I11 on $R$ is NOT reduced here to a finite computation. Corollary 3 reduces the seven patterns of L017 Theorem 4 to three ((a), (b), (f)) for every $n$; this part is proved (Theorem 2) and holds in $C$ as well.
