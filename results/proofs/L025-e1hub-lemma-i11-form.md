## Statement

**Conventions.** As in L017 and L020. A 3-graph $G$ has vertex set $V(G)$ and edge set $E(G)$; $n,m,\tau,d_G(u),\Delta$ as usual; $s(G)=3n+2m-11\tau(G)$ is the I11 slack; $G-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(G)\setminus Z$; $\partial_G(Z)$ is the set of edges meeting $Z$ and $\partial_G(x,y)=\partial_G(\{x,y\})$; two vertices are *adjacent* if some edge contains both; a set $X$ is *co-transversal* in $G$ if some minimum transversal contains $X$; a vertex is *avoided* if it lies in no minimum transversal; an *NC pair* is a pair of distinct vertices that is not co-transversal; a *$k$-transversal* is a transversal with exactly $k$ vertices. $C$ is the class of 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set $S$ with $|S|\ge2$; a *tight set* of $G$ is a set $S$ with $|S|\ge2$ and $e_G(S)=|S|-2$. $(\mathrm H_N)$: every member of $C$ with at most $N$ vertices satisfies I11 ($s\ge0$). For a new vertex $w$ and disjoint 2-sets $Q_1,\dots,Q_k$, $G+w(Q_1,\dots,Q_k)$ adds $w$ and the edges $\{w\}\cup Q_j$. For a 3-set $Y\subseteq V(G)$, $G+Y$ adds the edge $Y$.

**Gadgets.** For distinct vertices $a,b,c$ of $G$:
- *hub:* $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,x,y,z$ and the edges $e_0=\{x,y,z\}$, $\{v,x,a\}$, $\{v,y,b\}$, $\{v,z,c\}$;
- *fold:* $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)$ is $G$ plus new vertices $v,z$ and the edges $\{v,a,b\}$, $\{v,z,c\}$;
- *pendant replacement:* $G^{\rm p}(c;a,b)=(G-c)+\{w,a,b\}$ with $w$ new;
- *identification:* $G_{x=y}$ replaces $y$ by $x$ in every edge and deletes $y$;
- *move* (for $d_G(x)=1$ with edge $\{x,x_1,x_2\}$): $M(x\to x_1;y,z)=(G-x)+\{x_1,y,z\}$.

A *b-configuration* of $G$ is a set $\{a,b,c\}$ of three vertices of $G$-degree $\le2$ that is not an edge of $G$ and in which any two vertices form an NC pair. It is *pendant* if $d_G(a)=d_G(b)=d_G(c)=1$ and no edge of $G$ contains two of $a,b,c$.

**Lemma 1 (hub; = L026 Theorem 1(a),(b), re-proved).** Let $G\in C$ and let $a,b,c$ be distinct vertices with $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$. Then $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C$ (and $\Delta\le3$ if $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $d(a),d(b),d(c)\le2$), and $\tau(G\oplus(a,b,c))=\tau(G)+1$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of $a,b,c$, and $\tau(G)+2$ otherwise. So the slack changes by $+9$ or by $-2$.

**Lemma 2 (fold).** Let $G\in C$ and let $a,b,c$ be distinct, with $a,b$ not adjacent. Then $G^\circ=G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)\in C$, and $\tau(G^\circ)=\tau(G)$ if some minimum transversal of $G$ contains $c$ and meets $\{a,b\}$, and $\tau(G)+1$ otherwise. In particular, if $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,c\}$ are NC pairs, then $s(G^\circ)=s(G)-1$.

**Lemma 3 (pendant replacement).** Let $G\in C$, let $a,b,c$ be distinct with $a,b$ not adjacent, $c$ co-transversal, and $\{a,c\},\{b,c\}$ NC pairs. Then $G^{\rm p}(c;a,b)\in C$, it has $n$ vertices, $m-d(c)+1$ edges and transversal number $\tau(G)$, and $s(G^{\rm p})=s(G)+2-2d(c)$.

**Lemma 4 (identification).** Let $G$ be a 3-graph and $\{x,y\}$ an NC pair with $x$ co-transversal, $x,y$ not adjacent and without a common neighbour. Then $G_{x=y}$ is a linear 3-graph if $G$ is, with $n-1$ vertices, $m$ edges and $\tau(G_{x=y})=\tau(G)$, so $s(G_{x=y})=s(G)-3$. If $G\in C$, then $G_{x=y}\in C$ unless $G_{x=y}$ contains a copy of $F_7$ through $x$ or $G$ has a tight set containing $x$ and $y$.

**Lemma 5 (move).** Let $G$ be a 3-graph, $x$ a co-transversal vertex of degree 1 with edge $\{x,x_1,x_2\}$, and $y,z\notin\{x,x_1\}$ distinct vertices such that $\{x,y\},\{x,z\}$ are NC pairs. Then $\tau(M(x\to x_1;y,z))=\tau(G)$; it has $n-1$ vertices and $m$ edges, so its slack is $s(G)-3$.

**Lemma 6 (an edge on avoided vertices).** If $Y$ is a set of three avoided vertices of a 3-graph $F$, then $\tau(F+Y)=\tau(F)+1$ and $s(F+Y)=s(F)-9$.

**Lemma 7 (pendant edges on pairs).** Let $F$ be a 3-graph and $P_1,\dots,P_k$ pairwise disjoint 2-sets of vertices, and let $F^+$ be $F$ plus new vertices $w_1,\dots,w_k$ and the edges $\{w_i\}\cup P_i$. Then $\tau(F^+)=\min_T\big(|T|+\#\{i:T\cap P_i=\emptyset\}\big)$ over all transversals $T$ of $F$. If $F\in C$ and no $P_i$ lies inside an edge of $F$, then $F^+\in C$.

**Lemma 8 (one vertex, two vertices; L017 Theorem 2(U), L020 Proposition 6).** Let $G$ be a 3-graph. (a) If $u$ is avoided, then $s(G-u)=s(G)-3-2d(u)$; hence if $s(G-u)\ge0$ and $s(G)<3+2d(u)$, then $u$ is co-transversal. (b) If $\{x,y\}$ is an NC pair and $x$ is co-transversal, then $\tau(G-x-y)=\tau(G)-1$ and $s(G-x-y)=s(G)+5-2|\partial_G(x,y)|$.

**Lemma 9 (deleting a pairwise NC triple).** Let $G$ be a 3-graph, $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$ a set of three vertices of degree $\ge1$, pairwise NC, with $a$ co-transversal. Then $\tau(G-a-b)=\tau(G)-1$ and $\tau(G-a-b-c)\in\{\tau(G)-2,\tau(G)-1\}$; if $\tau(G-a-b-c)=\tau(G)-1$ then $s(G-a-b-c)=s(G)+2-2|\partial_G(\{a,b,c\})|\le s(G)-2$. So if $s(G)\le1$ and $s(G-a-b-c)\ge0$, then $\tau(G-a-b-c)=\tau(G)-2$ and $s(G-a-b-c)=s(G)+13-2|\partial_G(\{a,b,c\})|$; if moreover all three of $a,b,c$ are co-transversal, none of them has all its edges meeting the other two.

**Theorem 10 (main reduction).** Let $G\in C$ be connected with $s(G)\le1$ and suppose $(\mathrm H_{n(G)+2})$. Then every b-configuration of $G$ is pendant, and if $G$ has one then $s(G)=1$.

**Corollary 11 (the I11 form, and the minimal-counterexample form).**
(a) Let $G\in C$ be connected with $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $s(G)\le1$, and suppose $(\mathrm H_{n(G)+2})$ (weaker than the hypothesis "I11 below $n(G)+4$" of SYNTHESIS.md Section 3; no lower bound on $n(G)$ is needed). Let $a,b,c$ have $G$-degree $\le2$, $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$ (then $G\oplus(a,b,c)\in C$ by Lemma 1). If $s(G)=0$, or if $s(G)=1$ and $\{a,b,c\}$ is not a pendant triple (some of $a,b,c$ has degree 2, or two of them are adjacent), then some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of $a,b,c$, i.e. $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ satisfies I11. In particular: no tight such $G$ has a b-configuration (statement B0 of SYNTHESIS.md Section 4 for triples that are not edges, for every $n$, given $(\mathrm H_{n+2})$; triples that are edges are handled by L022 Theorem 2(v)).
(b) Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to I11 on $C$ and $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ an E1-hub of $H$ (edges $\{x,y,z\},\{v,x,a\},\{v,y,b\},\{v,z,c\}$, $d_H(x)=d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$). Then $G'=H-\{v,x,y,z\}$ is connected, $H=G'\oplus(a,b,c)$, $\{a,b,c\}$ is a pendant b-configuration of $G'$, $s(H)=-1$, $s(G')=1$, and $a,b,c$ have $H$-degree 2; moreover $G'$ satisfies all conclusions of Theorem 12.

**Theorem 12 (the pendant case).** Let $G\in C$ be connected with $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $s(G)=1$ (by Theorem 10 this is forced when $(\mathrm H_{n(G)+2})$ holds), suppose $(\mathrm H_{n(G)})$, and let $\{a,b,c\}$ be a pendant b-configuration with edges $e_a=\{a\}\cup A$, $e_b=\{b\}\cup B$, $e_c=\{c\}\cup C$. Put $G''=G-\{a,b,c\}$, $\tau''=\tau(G'')$, $W=A\cup B\cup C$.
(a) $\tau''=\tau(G)-2$ and $s(G'')=8$. Every minimum transversal of $G''$ avoids $W$; every transversal of $G''$ that meets $A$, $B$ and $C$ has at least $\tau''+2$ vertices; and for each $X\in\{A,B,C\}$ some $(\tau''+1)$-transversal of $G''$ meets the other two pairs.
(b) $G''$ is connected.
(c) $A$, $B$, $C$ are pairwise disjoint, so $|W|=6$, and every vertex of $W$ has $G''$-degree 1 or 2.
(d) For every $X=\{x_A,x_B,x_C\}$ with $x_A\in A$, $x_B\in B$, $x_C\in C$: $\tau(G''-X)=\tau''-1$, $s(G''-X)=10-2|\partial_{G''}(X)|$, and $|\partial_{G''}(X)|\le5$.
(e) For every set $Y$ of three pairwise non-adjacent avoided vertices of $G''$ (for instance any pairwise non-adjacent $X$ as in (d)): $G''+Y\notin C$, so $G''+Y$ contains a copy of $F_7$ through the edge $Y$, or $G''$ has a tight set containing $Y$. A copy of $F_7$ through $Y=\{y_1,y_2,y_3\}$ means one of: some $o\in V(G'')$ has edges $\{o,y_i,p_i\}$ ($i=1,2,3$) and $\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}\in E(G'')$ (the four hub edges of an E1-hub with outer triple $Y$, without the degree conditions); or, for some $i$, $y_i$ has two edges $\{y_i,p,q\},\{y_i,r,s\}$ and the other two members of $Y$ lie in edges $\{\cdot,p,r\},\{\cdot,q,s\}$ of $G''$.
(f) The identifications $G_{a=b},G_{a=c},G_{b=c}$ and the six moves $M(a\to a_i;b,c)$ ($a_i\in A$) and their analogues have slack $-2$ and are linear, hence not in $C$. Precisely: $G_{a=b}\notin C$ iff some $a_i$ has edges $\{a_i,b_1,q\},\{a_i,b_2,s\}$ and $\{a_{3-i},q,s\}\in E(G'')$ (or the same with the roles of $A$ and $B$ exchanged), or $G''$ has a tight set containing $A\cup B$; and $M(a\to a_i;b,c)\notin C$ iff $a_i$ has edges $\{a_i,b_j,c_k\}$, $\{a_i,b_{3-j},c_{3-k}\}$ in $G''$, or $G''$ has a tight set containing $\{a_i\}\cup B\cup C$.
(g) For any two disjoint 2-sets $P,Q\subseteq W$ that lie inside no edge of $G''$, some $(\tau''+1)$-transversal of $G''$ meets both $P$ and $Q$.
(h) For any three pairwise disjoint 2-sets $P,Q,R\subseteq W$ inside no edge of $G''$, some $(\tau''+1)$-transversal meets two of them, or some $(\tau''+2)$-transversal meets all three.
(i) No edge of $G''$ lies inside $W$, and if an edge of $G''$ has two vertices in $W$, its third vertex lies in every minimum transversal of $G''$.
(j) (*Everything is inside a tight set.*) $G''$ has a tight set containing $W$. Let $S_0$ be the largest one (the union of all tight sets of $G''$ containing $W$). Then $S_0\cup\{a,b,c\}$ is a tight set of $G$ and $S_0\cup\{a,b,c,v,x,y,z\}$ is a tight set of $G\oplus(a,b,c)$; every edge of $G''$ that meets both $S_0$ and $V(G'')\setminus S_0$ has exactly one vertex in $S_0$; and every vertex of $W$ has an edge of $G''$ inside $S_0$. If $S_0=V(G'')$, then $G''$, $G$ and $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ are saturated ($m=n-2$), and $n(G)\equiv1\pmod{11}$.
(k) (*The saturated case.*) If $S_0=V(G'')$, then $a,b,c$ are the only vertices of $G$ of degree $\le2$ (all others have degree 3); the six vertices of $W$ have $G''$-degree 2 and are exactly the avoided vertices of $G''$; and every set $X=\{x_A,x_B,x_C\}$ as in (d) contains two vertices lying in a common edge of $G''$ (whose third vertex lies in every minimum transversal of $G''$, by (i)).

**Lemma 13 (pendant block; the I11 form of L026 Lemma 6, stated there in Remark 7.3).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to I11 on $C$, $e\in E(H)$, and $V(H)=V_1\sqcup V_2$ such that every edge other than $e$ lies inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$ and $|e\cap V_2|=1$. Then $|V_2|=1$. *Consequence (with Theorem 12(j)):* if $H=G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is a minimal counterexample as in Corollary 11(b) and the largest tight set $S_0\supseteq W$ of $G''$ is not all of $V(G'')$, then at least two edges of $G''$ leave $S_0$.

## Proof

*Overview.* Each lemma computes $\tau$ exactly for a small modification of $G$ in which the NC conditions force the transversal number; when the modification lies in $C$ and has at most $n(G)+2$ vertices, $(\mathrm H_N)$ bounds its slack. Theorem 10 combines three such bounds (R1: pendant replacement, Lemma 3; R2: the pair lemma, Lemma 8(b); R3: deleting the triple, Lemma 9) with a case analysis of the adjacencies inside the triple, and uses the fold (Lemma 2) to exclude slack 0. Theorem 12 deletes the three pendant vertices and studies the remainder $G''$: an edge on three avoided vertices (Lemma 6), identifications and moves (Lemmas 4, 5) and pendant pairs (Lemma 7) would all give smaller violators, which forces the structure (a)-(k), in particular a tight set containing all six attachment vertices.

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L017 0.1-0.6.) $n,m,\tau,s$ are additive over disjoint unions; the minimum transversals of a disjoint union are the unions of minimum transversals of the parts; an isolated vertex has $s=3$. $C$ is closed under deleting vertices and edges. $\tau(G)-|Z|\le\tau(G-Z)\le\tau(G)$. $X$ is co-transversal iff $\tau(G-X)=\tau(G)-|X|$; an isolated vertex is avoided. $s(G-Z)=s(G)-3|Z|-2|\partial_G(Z)|+11(\tau(G)-\tau(G-Z))$. In a linear 3-graph $|\partial(x,y)|=d(x)+d(y)-[x,y\ \text{adjacent}]$.
0.2 (L017 0.7.) If $G$ is connected and $\emptyset\ne Z\subsetneq V(G)$, every component of $G-Z$ contains a vertex of an edge of $\partial_G(Z)$. A connected 3-graph with at least 2 vertices has no isolated vertex.
0.3 (Free choice.) If $G\in C$ is connected with $n(G)\ge2$, $s(G)\le1$ and $(\mathrm H_{n(G)-1})$, then every vertex $u$ is co-transversal: $d(u)\ge1$ (0.2), $s(G-u)\ge0$, and $s(G)\le1<5\le3+2d(u)$; apply Lemma 8(a).

### Step 1. Proof of Lemma 1
1.1 Put $H=G\oplus(a,b,c)$, $e_a=\{v,x,a\}$, $e_b=\{v,y,b\}$, $e_c=\{v,z,c\}$. *Linear:* two hub edges meet in exactly one vertex ($e_0\cap e_a=\{x\}$, $e_a\cap e_b=\{v\}$, etc., as $a,b,c$ are distinct); a hub edge has at most one vertex in $V(G)$, so it meets every edge of $G$ in at most one vertex.
1.2 *$F_7$-free:* $F_7$ has 7 vertices, 5 edges and minimum degree 2. Let $F\subseteq H$ be a copy. If $x\in V(F)$, both edges at $x$ ($e_0,e_a$) are in $F$; then $y,z\in V(F)$, and as $d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$, also $e_b,e_c\in F$; so $V(F)=\{v,x,y,z,a,b,c\}$ and the fifth edge lies inside this set, is not a hub edge, and so avoids $v,x,y,z$ (all their edges are hub edges): it is $\{a,b,c\}\in E(G)$, excluded. Likewise if $y$ or $z\in V(F)$. If $v\in V(F)$ but $x,y,z\notin V(F)$, $v$ has $F$-degree 0. So $F\subseteq G$, impossible.
1.3 *(P3):* let $|S|\ge2$, $S_0=S\cap V(G)$, $S_1=S\cap\{v,x,y,z\}$, $h$ the number of hub edges inside $S$; $e_H(S)=e_G(S_0)+h$. If $S_1=\emptyset$: $e_H(S)=e_G(S_0)\le|S|-2$. If $S_1=\{v,x,y,z\}$: $h=1+|S_0\cap\{a,b,c\}|$; for $|S_0|\ge2$, $e_H(S)\le|S_0|-2+1+3=|S|-2$; for $|S_0|\le1$, $e_H(S)\le2\le|S|-2$. Otherwise $\emptyset\ne S_1\ne\{v,x,y,z\}$ and $h\le|S_1|-1$: if $v\notin S_1$ only $e_0$ can be inside $S$, and only when $S_1=\{x,y,z\}$; if $v\in S_1$ then $e_0\not\subseteq S$ and each of $e_a,e_b,e_c$ inside $S$ uses its own vertex of $S_1\setminus\{v\}$. For $|S_0|\ge2$: $e_H(S)\le|S_0|-2+|S_1|-1<|S|-2$. For $|S_0|\le1$: $e_G(S_0)=0$ and $h\le1$ (if $v\notin S_1$ only $e_0$; if $v\in S_1$, each of $e_a,e_b,e_c$ inside $S$ needs its own vertex $a,b,c$ of $S_0$), and $h=1$ forces $|S|\ge3$. So $H\in C$. Degrees: $v$ has degree 3, $x,y,z$ degree 2, and $a,b,c$ gain one edge each.
1.4 *$\tau$:* for a transversal $T$ of $H$, $T\cap V(G)$ is a transversal of $G$ and $T$ meets $e_0\subseteq\{x,y,z\}$, so $|T|\ge\tau(G)+1$. If $|T|=\tau(G)+1$, then $T_0=T\cap V(G)$ is a minimum transversal of $G$ and $T$ has one vertex $w$ in $\{v,x,y,z\}$; $w=v$ misses $e_0$; $w=x$ forces $b,c\in T_0$; $w=y$ forces $a,c\in T_0$; $w=z$ forces $a,b\in T_0$. Conversely if a minimum transversal $T_0$ contains (say) $b,c$, then $T_0\cup\{x\}$ is a transversal of $H$. And $T_0\cup\{v,x\}$ always is. With $\Delta n=\Delta m=4$ the slack changes by $12+8-11=+9$ when $\tau$ rises by 1 and by $12+8-22=-2$ when it rises by 2. $\square$

### Step 2. Proof of Lemma 2
2.1 *In $C$:* $\{v,a,b\}\cap\{v,z,c\}=\{v\}$; $\{v,z,c\}$ meets an edge of $G$ only in $c$; $\{v,a,b\}$ meets an edge $f$ of $G$ in $f\cap\{a,b\}$, of size $\le1$ as $a,b$ are not adjacent. $z$ has degree 1, so a copy of $F_7$ avoids $z$ and $\{v,z,c\}$, hence $v$ (which would have degree $\le1$ in it), hence lies in $G$: impossible. (P3): for $|S|\ge2$, $S_0=S\cap V(G)$, $e(S)=e_G(S_0)+[\{v,a,b\}\subseteq S]+[\{v,z,c\}\subseteq S]$. If $v\notin S$: $e(S)=e_G(S_0)$, which is $\le|S_0|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$ and 0 otherwise. If $v\in S$, $z\notin S$: $e(S)\le e_G(S_0)+1\le|S|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$, and $e(S)=0$ otherwise (the edge $\{v,a,b\}$ needs $a,b\in S_0$). If $v,z\in S$: $e(S)\le e_G(S_0)+2\le|S_0|=|S|-2$ if $|S_0|\ge2$; if $|S_0|\le1$, $e(S)\le1$ and $|S|\ge3$ when $e(S)=1$.
2.2 *$\tau$:* a transversal $T$ of $G^\circ$ has $|T\cap V(G)|\ge\tau(G)$; if $|T|=\tau(G)$ then $v,z\notin T$, so $T$ is a minimum transversal of $G$ meeting $\{a,b\}$ and containing $c$; conversely such a $T$ is a transversal of $G^\circ$; and $T_0\cup\{v\}$ always is. If $\{a,c\},\{b,c\}$ are NC pairs, no minimum transversal contains $c$ and meets $\{a,b\}$, so $\tau(G^\circ)=\tau(G)+1$ and $s(G^\circ)=s(G)+6+4-11=s(G)-1$. $\square$ (This is L019 Theorem 5 applied to $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ at $x$, merging $y$ with $a$; not needed.)

### Step 3. Proof of Lemma 3
3.1 *In $C$:* $G-c\in C$ (0.1). The new edge $\{w,a,b\}$ meets an edge of $G-c$ in at most one of $a,b$ ($a,b$ not adjacent) and never in $w$: linear. $w$ has degree 1, so no copy of $F_7$ uses the new edge. (P3): for $S\not\ni w$, $e(S)=e_{G-c}(S)\le|S|-2$; for $S\ni w$ with $|S\setminus\{w\}|\ge2$, $e(S)\le e_{G-c}(S\setminus\{w\})+1\le|S|-2$; for $S=\{w,u\}$, $e(S)=0$.
3.2 *$\tau$:* $c$ is co-transversal, so $\tau(G-c)=\tau(G)-1$ (0.1). If $T'$ is a minimum transversal of $G-c$, then $T'\cup\{c\}$ is a transversal of $G$ with $\tau(G)$ vertices, i.e. a minimum transversal containing $c$; it contains neither $a$ nor $b$ (NC). So no minimum transversal of $G-c$ meets $\{a,b\}$, and $\tau(G^{\rm p})=\tau(G-c)+1=\tau(G)$ (a transversal of $G^{\rm p}$ restricts to one of $G-c$ and must meet $\{w,a,b\}$; $T'\cup\{w\}$ works). ### Step 4. Proof of Lemma 4
4.1 *Shape:* an edge of $G$ containing $y$ does not contain $x$ ($x,y$ not adjacent), so $G_{x=y}$ has no degenerate edge; two edges $\{x,p,q\}$, $\{y,p,q\}$ would make $p$ a common neighbour, so no edge is doubled, and $G_{x=y}$ has $m$ edges and $n-1$ vertices. 4.2 *$\tau$:* a minimum transversal $T$ of $G$ containing $x$ does not contain $y$ (NC), and it is a transversal of $G_{x=y}$ (every edge that contained $y$ now contains $x$): $\tau(G_{x=y})\le\tau(G)$. Conversely let $T'$ be a transversal of $G_{x=y}$. If $x\notin T'$, $T'$ is a transversal of $G$, so $|T'|\ge\tau(G)$. If $x\in T'$, $T'\cup\{y\}$ is a transversal of $G$ containing $x,y$, which is not minimum, so $|T'|+1\ge\tau(G)+1$. Hence $\tau(G_{x=y})=\tau(G)$, and $s(G_{x=y})=s(G)-3$.
4.3 *Membership:* $G_{x=y}-x=G-x-y\in C$, so a copy of $F_7$ in $G_{x=y}$ contains $x$. For a set $S'\subseteq V(G_{x=y})$ with $|S'|\ge2$: if $x\notin S'$ then $e(S')=e_G(S')$; if $x\in S'$ then the edges of $G_{x=y}$ inside $S'$ are the images of the edges of $G$ inside $S=S'\cup\{y\}$, so $e(S')=e_G(S)$ with $|S|=|S'|+1$, and (P3) fails at $S'$ iff $e_G(S)\ge|S|-2$, i.e. (by (P3) in $G$) $S$ is a tight set of $G$ containing $x,y$. $\square$

### Step 8. Proof of Theorem 10

8.2 *Rule R1: if $\sigma_{ab}=0$ then $d(c)=1$.* Lemma 3 applies ($c$ co-transversal, $\{a,c\},\{b,c\}$ NC, $a,b$ not adjacent): $G^{\rm p}(c;a,b)\in C$ has $n$ vertices and slack $s(G)+2-2d(c)\ge0$ by $(\mathrm H_n)$, so $d(c)\le(s(G)+2)/2\le3/2$.
8.3 *Rule R2: if $d(a)=d(b)=2$ then $\sigma_{ab}=1$.* Lemma 8(b): $0\le s(G-a-b)=s(G)+5-2|\partial(a,b)|$ (by $(\mathrm H_{n-2})$), so $|\partial(a,b)|\le3$, while $|\partial(a,b)|=4-\sigma_{ab}$ (0.1).
8.4 *Rule R3: no vertex of the triple has all its edges meeting the other two.* Lemma 9 (all three vertices have degree $\ge1$ and are co-transversal by 8.1, $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$), with $s(G)\le1$ and $(\mathrm H_{n-3})$.
8.5 *The four adjacency patterns.* (i) $\sigma_{ab}=\sigma_{ac}=\sigma_{bc}=1$: let $e_{ab}\ni a,b$, $e_{ac}\ni a,c$, $e_{bc}\ni b,c$ be the edges; they are pairwise distinct (an edge containing two of the three pairs contains $a,b,c$ and would equal $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$). (ii) Exactly two adjacencies, say $\sigma_{ab}=\sigma_{ac}=1$, $\sigma_{bc}=0$: R1 for $\{b,c\}$ gives $d(a)=1$, but $a$ lies in the distinct edges $e_{ab},e_{ac}$. (iii) Exactly one, say $\sigma_{ab}=1$: R1 for $\{a,c\}$ gives $d(b)=1$ and for $\{b,c\}$ gives $d(a)=1$; then the only edge at $b$ is $e_{ab}$, which meets $\{a,c\}$: contradicts R3. 8.6 *$s(G)=1$.* In case (iv), $a,b$ are not adjacent and $\{a,c\},\{b,c\}$ are NC pairs, so Lemma 2 gives $G\oplus^\circ(a,b\,|\,c)\in C$ with $n+2$ vertices and slack $s(G)-1$, which is $\ge0$ by $(\mathrm H_{n+2})$. $\square$

11.8 *(j).* By (f) the move $M(a\to a_1;b,c)$ is linear and not in $C$. A copy of $F_7$ in it would need, by 11.4, the two edges $\{a_1,b_j,c_k\},\{a_1,b_{3-j},c_{3-k}\}$ of $G''$, which lie inside $W$: impossible by (i). So (11.4) $G''$ has a tight set $S_1\supseteq\{a_1\}\cup B\cup C$, and likewise (move $M(a\to a_2;b,c)$) a tight set $S_2\supseteq\{a_2\}\cup B\cup C$. Two tight sets $S,S'$ with $|S\cap S'|\ge2$ have a tight union: $e(S\cup S')\ge e(S)+e(S')-e(S\cap S')\ge(|S|-2)+(|S'|-2)-(|S\cap S'|-2)=|S\cup S'|-2$ (an edge inside $S$ and inside $S'$ is inside $S\cap S'$; (P3) for $S\cap S'$), and $\le$ holds by (P3). As $|S_1\cap S_2|\ge4$, $S_1\cup S_2\supseteq W$ is tight. All tight sets containing $W$ pairwise meet in $\ge6$ vertices, so their union $S_0$ is tight. $e_G(S_0\cup\{a,b,c\})=e_{G''}(S_0)+3$ (the pendant edges lie inside, $A,B,C\subseteq S_0$) $=|S_0\cup\{a,b,c\}|-2$; adding $v,x,y,z$ adds the four hub edges, all inside, so $S_0\cup\{a,b,c,v,x,y,z\}$ has $|S_0|+5$ edges on $|S_0|+7$ vertices. If an edge of $G''$ had two vertices in $S_0$ and its third vertex $r$ outside, then $e(S_0\cup\{r\})\ge|S_0\cup\{r\}|-2$, so $S_0\cup\{r\}$ would be a larger tight set containing $W$. If a vertex $u\in W$ had no edge inside $S_0$, then $e(S_0\setminus\{u\})=|S_0\setminus\{u\}|-1$, violating (P3). If $S_0=V(G'')$, then $m(G'')=n(G'')-2$, hence $m(G)=n(G)-2$ and $m(G\oplus(a,b,c))=n(G\oplus(a,b,c))-2$, and $s(G)=5n-4-11\tau(G)=1$ gives $5n\equiv5$, i.e. $n\equiv1\pmod{11}$.
11.9 *(k).* $G$ is saturated with $\Delta\le3$ and no isolated vertex, so $\sum_u(3-d_G(u))=3n-3m=6$, i.e. $2n_1+n_2=6$; the three degree-1 vertices $a,b,c$ give $n_1\ge3$, hence $n_1=3$, $n_2=0$. A vertex of $W$ lies in exactly one pendant edge (A, B, C are disjoint), so its $G''$-degree is $3-1=2$; a vertex of $G''$ outside $W$ has $G''$-degree 3, and as $s(G'')=8<3+2\cdot3$, Lemma 8(a) (with $(\mathrm H_{n-4})$) makes it co-transversal; $W$ is avoided by (a). For $X$ as in (d), every vertex of $X$ has $G''$-degree 2 and no edge contains three vertices of $X\subseteq W$ (by (i)), so $|\partial_{G''}(X)|=6-p$ where $p$ is the number of edges of $G''$ with two vertices in $X$; (d) gives $p\ge1$. $\square$

### Step 12. Proof of Lemma 13
12.1 Write $e=\{p,q,c\}$ with $p,q\in V_1$, $c\in V_2$, and $H_i=H[V_i]$ ($i=1,2$); both lie in $C$ and have fewer vertices than $H$, so $s(H_i)\ge0$. $n$ and $\tau$ are additive over $H_1\sqcup H_2$ and $m(H)=m(H_1)+m(H_2)+1$; a transversal of $H$ restricts to transversals of $H_1,H_2$, and $T_1\cup T_2\cup\{c\}$ is one; so $\tau(H)\in\{\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2),\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1\}$. The first value gives $s(H)=s(H_1)+s(H_2)+2\ge2$; so $\tau(H)=\tau(H_1)+\tau(H_2)+1$, i.e. no minimum transversal of $H_1$ meets $\{p,q\}$ and no minimum transversal of $H_2$ contains $c$ (otherwise $T_1\cup T_2$ would cover $e$).
12.2 Suppose $|V_2|\ge2$. $H$ is connected (L017 Theorem 1(a)), so every component of $H_2$ contains a vertex of $e$, i.e. contains $c$: $H_2$ is connected with $\ge2$ vertices and $d_{H_2}(c)\ge1$. $H^\#=H_1+\{p,q,w\}$ ($w$ new) lies in $C$ (Lemma 7: $p,q$ are not adjacent in $H_1$, as they lie in $e$) and has $|V_1|+1<n(H)$ vertices, and $\tau(H^\#)=\tau(H_1)+1$ by 12.1, so $0\le s(H^\#)=s(H_1)-6$. $c$ is avoided in $H_2$, so $s(H_2)=s(H_2-c)+3+2d_{H_2}(c)$ (Lemma 8(a)), with $s(H_2-c)\ge0$. Hence $s(H)=s(H_1)+s(H_2)+2-11=s(H^\#)+s(H_2-c)+2d_{H_2}(c)\ge2$, a contradiction. So $|V_2|=1$.
12.3 *Consequence.* In $H=G\oplus(a,b,c)$ the set $S_H=S_0\cup\{a,b,c,v,x,y,z\}$ is tight (Theorem 12(j)); the edges of $H$ leaving $S_H$ are the edges of $G''$ leaving $S_0$ (the hub and pendant edges lie inside $S_H$), and each meets $S_H$ in exactly one vertex (Theorem 12(j)). If $S_H\ne V(H)$, there is at least one such edge ($H$ is connected), and if there were exactly one, Lemma 13 with $V_2=S_H$ would give $|S_H|=1$, whereas $|S_H|\ge13$. $\square$

## What remains

**GAP: the pendant case (the I11 form of the E1-hub lemma is not proved).** It remains to exclude the configuration of Theorem 12: a connected $G''\in C$ with $\Delta\le3$, $s(G'')=8$ and (by Remark 10.3) $n(G'')\ge24$, with three pairwise disjoint 2-sets $A,B,C$, each inside no edge, whose six vertices $W$ have $G''$-degree 1 or 2, such that every minimum transversal of $G''$ avoids $W$ and every transversal meeting $A$, $B$ and $C$ has at least $\tau(G'')+2$ vertices (Theorem 12(a)); by Theorem 12(j) $G''$ then has a tight set $S_0\supseteq W$ (take the largest: edges leaving $S_0$ meet it in one vertex, every vertex of $W$ has an edge inside $S_0$), and (b)-(i), (k) hold. Given $(\mathrm H_{n(G'')+6})$ (I11 below $n(G)+4$, the hypothesis of SYNTHESIS.md), excluding this configuration is exactly what remains of the I11 form, and by Corollary 11(b) it is exactly what is needed to exclude E1-hubs from minimal counterexamples. Nothing proved here bounds $n(G'')$ from above, so this is not a finite computation.

*Why the gadgets of this certificate stop here.* Every gadget that adds edges among old vertices (Lemma 6 on avoided triples, the identifications, the moves) gives a graph of negative slack with fewer vertices; by Theorem 12(j) all of them are blocked by the single tight set $S_0$ (an edge added inside a tight set violates (P3)). The gadgets that never leave $C$ add new vertices with at most as many new edges (Lemmas 2, 7, the hub itself); with $s(G'')=8$ they must raise $\tau$ by about half the number of new vertices, which needs information about the $(\tau''+1)$- and $(\tau''+2)$-transversals of $G''$ beyond Theorem 12(a), (g), (h). Sub-problems:
- (R1a) $S_0=V(G'')$: $G''$, $G$ and the hub graph are saturated, $n(G)\equiv1\pmod{11}$, so $n(G)\in\{34,45,56,\dots\}$ (the saturated slack-1 cells $(34,32,15)$, $(45,43,20)$, ...), and by Theorem 12(k) all vertices of $G$ other than $a,b,c$ have degree 3, the avoided vertices of $G''$ are exactly the six degree-2 vertices $W$, and every transversal triple of $W$ contains an adjacent pair.
- (R1b) $S_0\ne V(G'')$: $G''$ consists of the saturated induced graph $M=G''[S_0]$, the rest $R''=G''-S_0$, and the edges between them, each of which meets $S_0$ in exactly one vertex; by Lemma 13 there are at least two of them when $H=G\oplus(a,b,c)$ is a minimal counterexample. A natural next step (not done) is to show that the triple restricts: that $\{a,b,c\}$ is a pendant b-configuration of $G[S_0\cup\{a,b,c\}]$ with slack $\le1$, which would allow an induction on $n$.
- (P) *A sufficient condition.* The GAP would follow from **Conjecture P2$^+$** [conjecture]: a connected member of $C$ with $\Delta\le3$ and slack 1 has at most two vertices of degree 1 (it suffices: no three degree-1 vertices in three different edges, pairwise non-co-transversal). Evidence: every stored near-tight graph other than $E_1$ has at most two vertices of degree 1, and every tight one at most one (pendants_output.txt: slack-1 cells $(8,5,3)$ $\{1{:}1,2{:}1\}$, $(12,10,5)$ $\{0{:}112,1{:}37\}$, $(15,11,6)$ $\{0{:}9,1{:}8,2{:}2\}$, $(19,16,8)$ $\{0{:}221,1{:}6\}$, $(22,17,9)$ $\{0{:}18,1{:}19\}$, $(26,22,11)$ $\{0{:}36\}$; the 45 classes $K_{29}$ have none). For saturated graphs P2$^+$ fails only at $n_1=3$, $n_2=0$, which is exactly (R1a).
- (R2) A multi-vertex form of Lemma 8(a) for avoided vertices of slack-8 graphs would be the natural tool for (a); note that Lemma 8(a) is not additive (the star $\{o,x_i,y_i\}$, $i=1,2,3$, has six avoided vertices and slack 16).
