## Statement

$C$ is the class of L007–L011: 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every vertex set with $|S|\ge2$; $s(H)=3n+2m-11\tau(H)$ is the I11 slack. A finite set $A\subset\mathbb R$ *realizes* a 3-graph $H$ if there is a bijection $V(H)\to A$ mapping the edges of $H$ onto the 3-term arithmetic progressions of $A$.

**Theorem 1.** Let $A$ be a 23-point (4,5)-set with $h(A)=13$ (so $h/n=13/23<4/7$; by L011, $13$ is the least possible value of $h$ at $n=23$). Then its AP hypergraph $H=H(A)$ has $\tau=10$, $m=21=n-2$ (it is saturated), it is connected, and **every vertex has AP-degree at most 3**; hence at least 17 of the 23 points have AP-degree exactly 3.

**Theorem 2 (the two violation cells with $h=13$).** No member of $C$ has $(n,m,\tau)=(23,19,10)$ or $(23,20,10)$.

**Theorem 3 (the degree-4 case).** (a) Up to isomorphism there are exactly 699 members $H$ of $C$ with $(n,m,\tau)=(23,21,10)$, $\Delta(H)\le4$ and a vertex of degree 4; all are connected with $\tau=10$ and $\Delta=4$ (`route4_classes.txt.gz`). (b) None of the 699 is realized by a finite set of reals whose midpoint map is injective; in particular none is the AP hypergraph of a (4,5)-set. So neither $C$ nor the ordered midpoint structure excludes a degree-4 point (each of the 699 has injective midpoint labellings compatible with a linear order, Step 6.3); exact realizability does.

**Remark 4 (open).** The maximum-degree-3 case is **not decided**: it is open whether some 23-point (4,5)-set has $h=13$, i.e. a saturated $(23,21,10)$ AP pattern with all degrees $\le3$ (Step 8 gives its structure and the cost data). If none exists, $f(23)=14$ and the first record cell left open by I11 is (25,23,11).

**Remark 5 (reference).** The I11-allowed record cells $(n,m,\tau)$ for $n\le40$ (L011 Remark 5, re-derived in C1): (23,21,10) $s=1$; (25,23,11) $s=0$; (30,27,13) $s=1$; (30,28,13) $s=3$; (32,29,14) $s=0$; (32,30,14) $s=2$; (34,32,15) $s=1$; (36,34,16) $s=0$; (37,33,16) $s=1$; (37,34,16) $s=3$; (37,35,16) $s=5$; (39,35,17) $s=0$; (39,36,17) $s=2$; (39,37,17) $s=4$.

## Proof

### Step 0. Facts used
0.1 (L007–L011 Step 0.) Additivity of $n,m,\tau,s$ over components; a single vertex has $s=3$; heredity of $C$; for $H\in C$: $\tau\le m$, $\tau(H)-1\le\tau(H-v)\le\tau(H)$, $m\le n-2$, $17\tau\le5n+3m$ (K8); a connected 3-graph with $m\ge1$ edges has $n\le2m+1$; deleting a vertex does not increase degrees.
0.2 I11 holds on $C$ for $n\le22$ (L011 Theorem 1).
0.3 (Degree bound at $n=23$, $\tau=10$.) Let $H\in C$ have 23 vertices, $m\le21$ edges, $\tau(H)=10$, and let $v$ have degree $d$. Then $H-v\in C$ has 22 vertices and $m-d$ edges, and $\tau(H-v)\in\{9,10\}$ (0.1). By 0.2, $11\tau(H-v)\le66+2(m-d)\le108<110$, so $\tau(H-v)=9$, and $99\le66+2(m-d)$, i.e. $d\le m-17$ (as $2d\le2m-33$). The slack of $H-v$ is $66+2(m-d)-99=2m-33-2d$.
0.4 (Enumeration and transfer, L008 Step 5, L011 0.5.) `enum_conn N M T0 D` outputs a labelled copy of every connected linear 3-graph with $N$ vertices, $M$ edges, $\tau\ge T_0$ and $\Delta\le D$, and nothing else (L007 Lemma D). If $G\cong G_0$, every extension of $G$ by a new vertex with $d$ edges whose pairs are pairwise disjoint, not inside an edge of $G$, with endpoints of $G$-degree $\le e$, is isomorphic to such an extension of $G_0$; $\tau$, connectivity, degrees and membership in $C$ are isomorphism invariants.
0.5 (Edges at a vertex of maximum degree.) If $X\in C$ has $\Delta(X)\le\Delta$ and $d(w)=d$, the $d$ edges at $w$ are $\{w,a_i,b_i\}$ with pairwise disjoint pairs (by (P1) two edges at $w$ meet only in $w$), no pair inside an edge of $X-w$ (that edge would meet $\{w,a_i,b_i\}$ in two vertices), and each $a_i,b_i$ of degree $\le\Delta-1$ in $X-w$ (it lies in exactly one edge at $w$). An isolated vertex of $X-w$ is an admissible endpoint of degree 0.
0.6 (Saturation forces connectivity.) If $H\in C$ has $m=n-2\ge1$ edges, $H$ is connected and has no isolated vertex: if $H$ had $c$ components with edges and $z$ isolated vertices, (P3) on each component with edges gives $m\le n-z-2c$, which is $\le n-3$ unless $c=1$ and $z=0$.

### Step 3. The engine (class lists of connected cells)
3.1 (Cells.) For $n\le22$ let $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ be the set of connected $X\in C$ with $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, $\tau(X)=t$ and $\Delta(X)\le D$; the isolated vertex is the type $(1,0,0)$. The engine computes, for each cell it needs, a list `cell(n,m,t,D)` of representatives, one per isomorphism class (`engine.py`; lists in `out/cells/`, tracked copy `cells.tar.gz`).
3.2 (Lemma E: one vertex removal.) Let $X\in\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$, $m\ge1$, $d=\Delta(X)$ (so $\lceil3m/n\rceil\le d\le D$), and $w$ a vertex of degree $d$. Then (i) $X-w\in C$ has $n-1$ vertices, $m-d$ edges, $\Delta(X-w)\le d$ and $\tau(X-w)=t'\in\{t-1,t\}$ (0.1); (ii) each component $K$ of $X-w$ is connected, lies in $C$, has $\Delta(K)\le d$ and at most $n-1\le21$ vertices, hence slack $\ge0$ (0.2); so the component types $(n_K,m_K,\tau_K)$ form a decomposition of $(n-1,m-d)$ into connected types and isolated vertices with total slack $S'=3(n-1)+2(m-d)-11t'$ (types with slack $>S'$ cannot occur), and each $K$ is isomorphic to a member of $\mathcal C(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)$; (iii) the edges at $w$ are as in 0.5 with $\Delta=d$ (endpoints of $(X-w)$-degree $\le d-1$). Hence (0.4) $X$ is isomorphic to a connected extension, as in (iii), of a disjoint union of one representative from each $\mathcal C(n_K,m_K,\tau_K,d)$ (a multiset when types repeat), with $\tau=t$. $\square$
3.3 (The algorithm.) If $m\le10$: `enum_conn n m t D` (0.4: a copy of every connected linear graph with $\tau\ge t$, $\Delta\le D$), then `filt` keeps the members of $C$ with $\tau=t$ exactly, then `canon` keeps one graph per canonical form. If $m\ge11$: for every $d$ with $\lceil3m/n\rceil\le d\le D$ and every $t'\in\{t-1,t\}$ with $S'\ge0$: every decomposition of $(n-1,m-d)$ of total slack $S'$ (`engine.decompositions`, isolated vertices included; checked against an independent brute force in C2), every multiset of representatives of the component cells (computed recursively with maximum degree $d$), placed on consecutive vertex blocks, is an input $G$; `ext_d (n-1) t' t d (d-1)` checks $\tau(G)=t'$ exactly and outputs every extension of $G$ as in 3.2(iii) with $\tau\ge t$ that lies in $C$; `filt` re-checks membership in $C$, keeps the extensions connected on all $n$ vertices, and (when $t'=t$) those with $\tau=t$ exactly (when $t'=t-1$, $\tau\le\tau(G)+1=t$ already); `canon` keeps one per class.
3.4 (Correctness.) By induction on $m$, `cell(n,m,t,D)` contains exactly one representative of every isomorphism class of $\mathcal C(n,m,t,D)$ and nothing else: every output is checked by `filt` to be connected, in $C$, with the right $n,m,\tau$ and degree bound; conversely every class is reached, for $m\le10$ by L007 Lemma D, for $m\ge11$ by Lemma E, the induction hypothesis on the component cells and the exhaustiveness of `ext_d` (C2); `canon` gives equal output exactly for isomorphic graphs (nauty; C6), so the reduction to representatives loses no class.
3.5 (`ext_d`, the extension test.) With $\tau(G)=t-1$ and $\mathcal T$ the set of all $(t-1)$-element vertex sets meeting every edge of $G$ (all of them, by enumeration of all subsets of that size), an extension $H=G+\{w,a_i,b_i\}_i$ has $\tau(H)\ge t$ iff every $T\in\mathcal T$ is disjoint from some pair $\{a_i,b_i\}$ (a cover of $H$ of size $\le t-1$ cannot contain $w$, its rest would cover $G$ with $t-2$ vertices; so it is a member of $\mathcal T$ meeting all pairs). `ext_d` enumerates these $d$-sets of pairs by a branching on the pairs disjoint from the first uncovered $T$, forbidding in each branch the earlier siblings, which produces every such $d$-set exactly once; a branch is cut when its new edge lies in a copy of $F_7$ (incremental test; $F_7$-containment is inherited by supersets), and (P3) is tested on the complete $d$-sets. With $\tau(G)\ge t$ every $d$-set is a hit.

### Step 6. Realizability (Theorem 3(b))
6.1 (Normal form of a realization.) Let $A$ realize a saturated $H$ ($m=n-2$) via $\varphi:V(H)\to A$, and suppose the midpoint map of $A$ is injective. For a (4,5)-set this holds: if $b$ were the midpoint of two 3-APs $\{a,b,c\}$ and $\{a',b,c'\}$ with $a<a'<b<c'<c$, then $c-c'=a'-a$ and $c'-a=c-a'$, so $\{a,a',c',c\}$ has at most 4 distinct differences. Let $\mu(e)=\varphi^{-1}(\text{midpoint of }\varphi(e))\in e$. Then $\mu$ is injective, so it misses exactly two vertices $u<v$; $\min A$ and $\max A$ are midpoints of nothing, so $\{u,v\}=\varphi^{-1}\{\min A,\max A\}$. The affine image $x_i=(\varphi(i)-\varphi(u))/(\varphi(v)-\varphi(u))$ has $x_u=0$, $x_v=1$, satisfies $2x_{\mu(e)}=x_a+x_c$ for every edge $e=\{a,\mu(e),c\}$, has $n$ distinct values, and its 3-APs are exactly the $m$ edges (affine maps preserve 3-APs).
6.2 (Lemma N: the system is nonsingular.) For saturated $H\in C$ and any injective $\mu$ with unmatched $u,v$, the system $2x_{\mu(e)}-x_a-x_c=0$ ($e\in E$), $x_u=0$, $x_v=1$, has exactly one solution. Proof: there are $n-2$ unknowns and $n-2$ equations ($\mu$ is a bijection $E\to V\setminus\{u,v\}$). Let $y$ solve the homogeneous system ($y_u=y_v=0$) and suppose $y\ne0$; replacing $y$ by $-y$, $M=\max y>0$. Let $S=\{w:y_w=M\}$; $u,v\notin S$. For $j\in S$ and its edge $e=\mu^{-1}(j)=\{a,j,c\}$: $2M=y_a+y_c\le2M$ forces $a,c\in S$. So the $|S|$ edges $\mu^{-1}(S)$ lie inside $S$, $|S|\ge3$, and $e(S)\ge|S|>|S|-2$, contradicting (P3). $\square$
6.3 (The test.) By 6.1–6.2, $H$ is realized by a set with injective midpoint map only if for some injective $\mu$ the unique solution has (i) $n$ distinct values and (ii) exactly $m$ three-term APs (each edge is one, by the equations and (i)). `realize.c` runs over all injective $\mu$ (a depth-first search over the edges, each edge choosing one of its unused vertices) and solves each system exactly: Gaussian elimination modulo $p=2^{61}-1$ gives $D=\det$ and $y=Dx$ modulo $p$; both are lifted to $(-p/2,p/2)$, and the lifted integers are then verified over $\mathbb Z$ ($D\ne0$ and $2y_{\mu(e)}-y_a-y_c=0$ for every edge, with $y_u=0$, $y_v=D$), so $x=y/D$ is the exact solution (unique by 6.2); (i) and (ii) are then tested on the integers $y$. C5: over the 699 classes, 27,206,877 assignments, none singular modulo $p$, none with a failed lift; 6,051,773 have distinct values (every class has at least 1,656 such; each of them orders the vertices so that every edge has its midpoint strictly between its endpoints, with distinct midpoints, so every class passes the ordered-labelling test, in agreement with the falsifier's SAT test F/post.py), and **none has exactly 21 three-term APs** (each distinct-valued solution has at least 4 extra APs; descriptive, `realize_extra.c`). So none of the 699 classes is realized by a set with injective midpoint map. $\square$

For Theorem 1: $H=H(A)\in C$ (K3–K7) with $\tau=23-13=10$ and $m\in\{19,20,21\}$ (Step 1); Theorem 2 leaves $m=21$, and $H$ is connected (0.6) with all degrees $\le4$ (Step 1).
