## Statement

**Conventions** (as in L025). A *3-graph* $F$ has vertex set $V(F)$ and edge set $E(F)$ (3-element sets); $n(F),m(F),\tau(F)$ are the numbers of vertices and edges and the transversal number; $d_F(u)$ is the degree, $\Delta(F)$ the maximum degree; $s(F)=3n(F)+2m(F)-11\tau(F)$ is the *I11 slack*; *I11 holds for $F$* if $s(F)\ge0$. For $Z\subseteq V(F)$, $F-Z$ is the induced subgraph on $V(F)\setminus Z$ and $\partial_F(Z)$ the set of edges meeting $Z$; $e_F(S)$ is the number of edges inside $S$. Two vertices are *adjacent* ($\sigma_F(u,w)=1$) if some edge contains both. A $k$-*transversal* is a transversal with exactly $k$ vertices. A set $X$ is *co-transversal* if some minimum transversal contains $X$; a vertex is *avoided* if it lies in no minimum transversal; an *NC pair* is a pair that is not co-transversal. $C$ is the class of 3-graphs that are (P1) linear, (P2) $F_7$-free ($F_7=\{012,034,056,135,246\}$) and (P3) satisfy $e(S)\le|S|-2$ for every $S$ with $|S|\ge2$. A *saturated set* of $F$ is a set $S$ with $|S|\ge3$ and $e_F(S)=|S|-2$ (L025 calls these "tight sets"; the size condition $|S|\ge3$ is the fix of SYNTHESIS.md Section 1, editorial point 1). $(\mathrm H_{<N})$: every member of $C$ with fewer than $N$ vertices satisfies I11.

A *pendant b-configuration* of $G$ is a set $\{a,b,c\}$ of three vertices of degree 1 lying in three different edges (so no edge contains two of them; in particular $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$) such that $\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{b,c\}$ are NC pairs. (This is the "pendant" case of the b-configurations of L025; the degree bound $\le2$ of a b-configuration holds.)

**Theorem 1 (the pendant-triple lemma PT).** Let $G\in C$ be connected with $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $s(G)\le1$, and suppose $(\mathrm H_{<n(G)})$. Then $G$ has no pendant b-configuration.

(The form asked for in SYNTHESIS.md Section 3 assumes $s(G)=1$ and I11 for all members of $C$ with at most $n(G)+3$ vertices; both hypotheses are stronger than those of Theorem 1. The proof uses I11 only for members of $C$ with $n(G)-1$, $n(G)-3$, $n(G)-4$ and $n(G)-5$ vertices or fewer; see "Hypotheses used" below.)

**Corollary 2 (residual form).** Let $G''\in C$ be connected with $\Delta(G'')\le3$ and $s(G'')\le8$, let $A,B,C$ be pairwise disjoint 2-sets of vertices of $G''$-degree $\le2$, none of them inside an edge of $G''$, such that (i) no minimum transversal of $G''$ meets $A\cup B\cup C$ and (ii) no $(\tau(G'')+1)$-transversal of $G''$ meets all of $A$, $B$ and $C$. Then some member of $C$ with at most $n(G'')+3$ vertices violates I11. In particular, under I11 for all members of $C$ with at most $n(G'')+3$ vertices (weaker than the hypothesis "fewer than $n(G'')+7$" of SYNTHESIS.md Section 3) no such $G''$ exists.

**Corollary 3 (the I11 form of the E1-hub lemma; uses L025 Theorem 10).** Let $G\in C$ be connected with $\Delta(G)\le3$ and $s(G)\le1$, and suppose every member of $C$ with at most $n(G)+2$ vertices satisfies I11. Then $G$ has no b-configuration: for any three vertices $a,b,c$ of $G$-degree $\le2$ with $\{a,b,c\}\notin E(G)$, some minimum transversal of $G$ contains two of them; equivalently (L025 Lemma 1) the hub $G\oplus(a,b,c)$ satisfies I11.

**Corollary 4 (minimal counterexamples; uses L025 Theorem 10, Corollary 11(b), L026 Corollary 14 and L022 Theorem 2).** Let $H$ be a minimal counterexample to I11 on $C$ (a member of $C$ with $s(H)<0$ all of whose members of $C$ with fewer vertices satisfy I11). Then
(a) $H$ has no E1-hub $(v;x,y,z;a,b,c)$ (edges $\{x,y,z\},\{v,x,a\},\{v,y,b\},\{v,z,c\}$, $d_H(x)=d_H(y)=d_H(z)=2$);
(b) no vertex of degree 3 of $H$ has pattern (b) of L017 Theorem 4(iii);
(c) hence (L022 Theorem 2(i),(ii)) for every vertex $v$ of degree 3, $H-v$ is connected (pattern (a)) or the pattern at $v$ is (f) (with $s(H)=-1$ and the structure of L022 Theorem 2(iii),(iv)).

**Remark 5 (what the proof says about L025 Theorem 12).** In the pendant case of L025 Theorem 12 the largest saturated set $S_0\supseteq W$ is always all of $V(G'')$ (Step 6): the sub-case (R1b) of L025 "What remains" ($S_0\ne V(G'')$, with $\ge2$ crossing edges) is void, the six attachment vertices always have $G''$-degree 2, and the configuration is the saturated case (R1a), which Step 7 excludes.

## Proof

*Overview.* Deleting the pendant triple leaves a graph $G''$ of slack $s(G)+7\le8$ in which the six attachment vertices $W$ are avoided and no $(\tau''+1)$-transversal meets all three attachment pairs (Step 2). The moves of L025 give a saturated set $S\supseteq W$ of $G''$ (Step 5). Since $\Delta\le3$, every saturated set $S$ satisfies $\sum_{t\in S}(3-d_{G''[S]}(t))=6$; the six vertices of $W$ have degree $\le2$, so they use up the whole budget: all of them have degree exactly 2 (Step 6). Two vertices of $W$ from different pairs that are not adjacent then give, after deleting both and putting a pendant edge on the third pair, a member of $C$ with $n(G)-4$ vertices and slack $\le s(G'')-9<0$ (Step 7). Such a pair exists because a vertex of degree 2 sees at most two other vertices of $W$.

### Step 0. Facts used (each with its one-line proof)
0.1 (Heredity.) If $F\in C$ and $Z\subseteq V(F)$, then $F-Z\in C$: linearity, $F_7$-freeness and (P3) pass to induced subgraphs.
0.2 (Deletion.) $\tau(F)-|Z|\le\tau(F-Z)\le\tau(F)$: a transversal of $F$ minus $Z$ is a transversal of $F-Z$, and a transversal of $F-Z$ plus $Z$ is a transversal of $F$. A vertex $u$ is co-transversal iff $\tau(F-u)=\tau(F)-1$ (a minimum transversal $T\ni u$ gives the transversal $T\setminus\{u\}$ of $F-u$; conversely a $(\tau(F)-1)$-transversal $T'$ of $F-u$ gives the minimum transversal $T'\cup\{u\}$); so an avoided $u$ has $\tau(F-u)=\tau(F)$.
0.3 (Slack under deletion.) $s(F-Z)=s(F)-3|Z|-2|\partial_F(Z)|+11(\tau(F)-\tau(F-Z))$, since $n(F-Z)=n(F)-|Z|$ and $m(F-Z)=m(F)-|\partial_F(Z)|$. In particular, for an avoided vertex $u$: $s(F-u)=s(F)-3-2d_F(u)$.
0.4 (Pairs.) In a linear 3-graph $|\partial_F(\{u,w\})|=d_F(u)+d_F(w)-\sigma_F(u,w)$ (at most one edge contains both).
0.5 (Components.) $n,m,\tau,s$ are additive over the components of $F$, and the minimum transversals of $F$ are exactly the unions of minimum transversals of its components; so a vertex avoided in $F$ is avoided in its component. A connected 3-graph with at least 2 vertices has no isolated vertex. If $F$ is connected and $\emptyset\ne Z\subsetneq V(F)$, every component $K$ of $F-Z$ contains a vertex of an edge of $\partial_F(Z)$: some edge meets both $V(K)$ and $V(F)\setminus V(K)$; it is not an edge of $F-Z$ (an edge of $F-Z$ meeting $V(K)$ lies inside $V(K)$), so it meets $Z$.
0.6 (Free choice.) If $G\in C$ is connected with $n(G)\ge2$, $s(G)\le1$ and $(\mathrm H_{<n(G)})$, every vertex $u$ of $G$ is co-transversal: $d_G(u)\ge1$ (0.5); if $u$ were avoided, 0.3 would give $s(G-u)=s(G)-3-2d_G(u)\le-4<0$, while $G-u\in C$ (0.1) has $n(G)-1$ vertices.
0.7 (Union of saturated sets.) If $F$ satisfies (P3) and $S_1,S_2$ are saturated sets with $|S_1\cap S_2|\ge2$, then $S_1\cup S_2$ is saturated. *Proof.* An edge inside $S_1$ or inside $S_2$ lies inside $S_1\cup S_2$, and an edge inside both lies inside $S_1\cap S_2$; so $e(S_1\cup S_2)\ge e(S_1)+e(S_2)-e(S_1\cap S_2)\ge(|S_1|-2)+(|S_2|-2)-(|S_1\cap S_2|-2)=|S_1\cup S_2|-2$, using (P3) for $S_1\cap S_2$. With (P3) for $S_1\cup S_2$ equality holds, and $|S_1\cup S_2|\ge|S_1|\ge3$. $\square$
0.8 (Degree budget of a saturated set.) If $\Delta(F)\le3$ and $S$ is a saturated set of $F$, then $\sum_{t\in S}\bigl(3-d_{F[S]}(t)\bigr)=6$, and every term is $\ge0$. *Proof.* $\sum_{t\in S}d_{F[S]}(t)=3e_F(S)=3|S|-6$, and $d_{F[S]}(t)\le d_F(t)\le3$. $\square$

### Step 1. Set-up
Let $G$ be as in Theorem 1, $n=n(G)$, and suppose, for a contradiction, that $\{a,b,c\}$ is a pendant b-configuration with edges $e_a=\{a\}\cup A$, $e_b=\{b\}\cup B$, $e_c=\{c\}\cup C$. Put $W=A\cup B\cup C$, $G''=G-\{a,b,c\}$, $N=n(G'')=n-3$, $m''=m(G'')$, $\tau''=\tau(G'')$.
1.1 $e_a,e_b,e_c$ are pairwise distinct and no edge contains two of $a,b,c$; hence $b,c\notin e_a$, $a,c\notin e_b$, $a,b\notin e_c$, so $W\subseteq V(G'')$, and the edges of $G$ meeting $\{a,b,c\}$ are exactly $e_a,e_b,e_c$ ($a,b,c$ have degree 1). So $m''=m(G)-3$ and $E(G)=E(G'')\cup\{e_a,e_b,e_c\}$.
1.2 By linearity of $G$, no edge other than $e_a$ contains $A$; in particular $A$ lies inside no edge of $G''$, and likewise $B$, $C$. Also $|A\cap B|\le|e_a\cap e_b|\le1$, and similarly for the other two intersections, so $|W|\ge3$.
1.3 Every $w\in W$ lies in at least one of $e_a,e_b,e_c$, which are not edges of $G''$, so $d_{G''}(w)\le d_G(w)-1\le\Delta(G)-1\le2$.
1.4 $G$ is connected with $n\ge4$ vertices ($\{a,b,c\}\cup A$), so by 0.6 every vertex of $G$ is co-transversal in $G$.
1.5 $G''\in C$ (0.1), and every member of $C$ with at most $n-1$ vertices satisfies I11 (hypothesis).

### Step 2. Transversals of $G''$
2.1 $\tau''\ge\tau(G)-2$: a transversal $T''$ of $G''$ with at most $\tau(G)-3$ vertices would make $T''\cup\{a,b,c\}$ a transversal of $G$ (1.1) with at most $\tau(G)$ vertices, hence a minimum transversal containing $a$ and $b$, contradicting the NC pair $\{a,b\}$.
2.2 By 0.2, $\tau(G)-3\le\tau''\le\tau(G)$, and by 0.3 with $|Z|=|\partial_G(Z)|=3$: $s(G'')=s(G)-15+11(\tau(G)-\tau'')$. If $\tau''\in\{\tau(G),\tau(G)-1\}$ this is $s(G)-15$ or $s(G)-4$, negative, contradicting 1.5 ($N=n-3$). With 2.1: $\tau''=\tau(G)-2$ and $s(G'')=s(G)+7\le8$.
2.3 (W is avoided.) No minimum transversal $T$ of $G''$ meets $W$: if $T$ met $A$, then $T\cup\{b,c\}$ would be a transversal of $G$ (it meets $e_a$ in $A$, and $e_b\ni b$, $e_c\ni c$) with $\tau''+2=\tau(G)$ vertices containing $b$ and $c$, contradicting the NC pair $\{b,c\}$; symmetrically for $B$ (use $\{a,c\}$) and $C$ (use $\{a,b\}$).
2.4 (No cheap triple cover.) Every transversal $T$ of $G''$ that meets $A$, $B$ and $C$ has $|T|\ge\tau''+2$: it is a transversal of $G$, so $|T|\ge\tau(G)$.
2.5 No edge of $G''$ lies inside $W$: every minimum transversal of $G''$ meets every edge and avoids $W$ (2.3). Hence every edge of $G''$ has at most two vertices in $W$.

### Step 3. $G''$ is connected (L025 Theorem 12(b), re-proved)
3.1 Every component $K$ of $G''$ contains a vertex of $W$: apply 0.5 to the connected $G$ with $Z=\{a,b,c\}$; the edges meeting $Z$ are $e_a,e_b,e_c$ (1.1), whose vertices outside $Z$ lie in $W$.
3.2 Each vertex of $W$ is avoided in its component (2.3 and 0.5).
3.3 For a component $K$: $s(K)\ge0$ (1.5). If $K$ contains an avoided vertex $u$ with $d_K(u)\ge1$, then $s(K)=s(K-u)+3+2d_K(u)\ge5$ (0.3; $K-u\in C$ has fewer vertices). If it contains an avoided vertex with $d_K(u)=0$, then $K=\{u\}$ and $s(K)=3$. By 3.1, 3.2 every component has slack $\ge3$.
3.4 $\sum_Ks(K)=s(G'')\le8$ (0.5, 2.2), so $G''$ has at most two components. Suppose it has two. They are not both single vertices ($|W|\ge3$), and not both of slack $\ge5$; so one is a single vertex $w_0\in W$ (slack 3) and the other, $K$, contains $W'=W\setminus\{w_0\}$, $|W'|\ge2$, and has $s(K)=s(G'')-3\le5$. Every $u\in W'$ is avoided in $K$ (3.2) and has $d_K(u)\ge1$ ($K$ is connected with at least 2 vertices, 0.5), so $5\ge s(K)\ge3+2d_K(u)$ (3.3), i.e. $d_K(u)=1$.
3.5 *Some two vertices $u_2,u_3$ of $W'$ are not adjacent.* Otherwise, fixing $u_1\in W'$ with its unique edge $g$, every other vertex of $W'$ shares an edge with $u_1$, which must be $g$; so $W'\subseteq g$. No pair among $A,B,C$ lies inside $g$ (1.2: $g\in E(G'')$), so each of $A,B,C$ has a vertex in $W\setminus W'=\{w_0\}$: $w_0\in A\cap B\cap C$. Then a minimum transversal of $G''$ plus $w_0$ is a $(\tau''+1)$-transversal meeting $A,B,C$, contradicting 2.4.
3.6 Let $Y=\{w_0,u_2,u_3\}$ and $G''+Y$ the 3-graph $G''$ with the extra edge $Y$. It is linear ($w_0$ is isolated in $G''$ and $u_2,u_3$ are not adjacent, so $Y$ meets every edge of $G''$ in at most one vertex); it is $F_7$-free ($w_0$ has degree 1 in $G''+Y$, $F_7$ has minimum degree 2, so a copy avoids $Y$ and lies in $G''\in C$); it satisfies (P3) (a set $S\supseteq Y$ has $e(S)=e_{G''}(S\setminus\{w_0\})+1\le|S|-2$, as $w_0$ is isolated in $G''$ and $|S\setminus\{w_0\}|\ge2$; other sets are unaffected). So $G''+Y\in C$. A $\tau''$-transversal of $G''+Y$ would be a minimum transversal of $G''$ meeting $Y\subseteq W$, impossible by 2.3; so $\tau(G''+Y)=\tau''+1$ and $s(G''+Y)=s(G'')+2-11\le-1$, with $N=n-3$ vertices: this contradicts 1.5. Hence $G''$ is connected, and as $N\ge|W|\ge3$, every vertex of $G''$ has $G''$-degree $\ge1$ (0.5).

### Step 4. $A$, $B$, $C$ are pairwise disjoint (L025 Theorem 12(c), re-proved)
4.1 Suppose $u\in A\cap B$. Then $u\notin C$: otherwise a minimum transversal of $G''$ plus $u$ would be a $(\tau''+1)$-transversal meeting $A,B,C$ (2.4).
4.2 Let $x\in C$ and $X=\{u,x\}$. For every transversal $T^*$ of $G''-X$, $T^*\cup X$ is a transversal of $G''$ meeting $A$, $B$ (in $u$) and $C$ (in $x$), so $|T^*|+2\ge\tau''+2$ (2.4). Hence $\tau(G''-X)\ge\tau''$, so $\tau(G''-X)=\tau''$ (0.2), and 0.3 gives $s(G''-X)=s(G'')-6-2|\partial_{G''}(X)|\le2-2|\partial_{G''}(X)|$. As $G''-X\in C$ has $n-5$ vertices, $|\partial_{G''}(X)|\le1$.
4.3 By Step 3, $d_{G''}(u),d_{G''}(x)\ge1$; with $|\partial_{G''}(X)|\le1$ this forces $d_{G''}(u)=d_{G''}(x)=1$ and a common edge. Applying this to both vertices $x$ of $C$: the unique edge $g$ of $u$ in $G''$ contains $C$, so $g=\{u\}\cup C$; but $C\subseteq e_c\ne g$ ($e_c\notin E(G'')$), contradicting 1.2. Hence $A\cap B=\emptyset$; by symmetry $A\cap C=B\cap C=\emptyset$, and $|W|=6$.

### Step 5. A saturated set of $G''$ containing $W$ (L025 Theorem 12(f),(j), re-proved)
Write $A=\{a_1,a_2\}$. For $i\in\{1,2\}$ let $M_i$ be the *move* $(G-a)+\{a_i,b,c\}$: delete $a$ (and $e_a$) and add the edge $\{a_i,b,c\}$, which is not an edge of $G-a$ (the only edge at $b$ is $e_b\not\ni c$). So $n(M_i)=n-1$ and $m(M_i)=m(G)$.
5.1 *$\tau(M_i)=\tau(G)$.* ($\le$) A minimum transversal $T$ of $G$ containing $b$ exists (1.4); it does not contain $a$ ($\{a,b\}$ is NC), so $T\subseteq V(M_i)$, and it meets every edge of $G-a$ and the new edge (at $b$). ($\ge$) Let $T$ be a transversal of $M_i$. It meets every edge of $G-a$, so $T\cup\{a\}$ is a transversal of $G$ and $|T|\ge\tau(G)-1$. If $|T|=\tau(G)-1$, then $T\cup\{a\}$ is a minimum transversal of $G$ containing $a$, so it contains neither $b$ nor $c$ (NC pairs); and $a_i\notin T$, since otherwise $T$ would also meet $e_a$ and be a transversal of $G$ with $\tau(G)-1$ vertices. Then $T$ misses the new edge $\{a_i,b,c\}$: a contradiction. So $|T|\ge\tau(G)$.
Hence $s(M_i)=3(n-1)+2m(G)-11\tau(G)=s(G)-3\le-2$, and by 1.5 $M_i\notin C$.
5.2 *$M_i$ is linear.* Two edges of $G-a$ meet in at most one vertex. The new edge meets $e_b$ only in $b$ ($a_i\notin B$ by Step 4, $c\notin e_b$ by 1.1) and $e_c$ only in $c$; every other edge $f$ of $G-a$ contains neither $b$ nor $c$ (degree 1 in $G$), so it meets the new edge at most in $a_i$.
5.3 *$M_i$ contains no copy of $F_7$.* A copy $F$ avoiding the new edge would lie in $G-a\in C$. So $F$ contains the new edge, hence $b$ and $c$. Every vertex of $F_7$ has degree $\ge2$ in $F_7$, and $b$ has exactly the two edges $e_b$ and $\{a_i,b,c\}$ in $M_i$, so $e_b\in F$; likewise $e_c\in F$. The set $\{a_i,b,c\}\cup B\cup C$ has 7 distinct elements (Step 4; $b,c\notin W$) and lies in $V(F)$, so it is $V(F)$. The two remaining edges $f_1,f_2$ of $F$ contain neither $b$ nor $c$ (their $M_i$-edges are already listed), so $f_j\subseteq\{a_i\}\cup B\cup C\subseteq W$, and $f_j\in E(G-a)$ avoids $a,b,c$, so $f_j\in E(G'')$: an edge of $G''$ inside $W$, contradicting 2.5.
5.4 *A saturated set $S_i\supseteq\{a_i\}\cup B\cup C$ of $G''$.* By 5.1-5.3, $M_i$ violates (P3): some $S'\subseteq V(M_i)$ with $|S'|\ge2$ has $e_{M_i}(S')\ge|S'|-1$. As $G-a\in C$ (0.1), $e_{G-a}(S')\le|S'|-2$; so the new edge lies inside $S'$ (hence $|S'|\ge3$) and $e_{G-a}(S')=|S'|-2$. If $e_b\not\subseteq S'$, then no edge of $G-a$ inside $S'$ contains $b$ (its only edge in $G-a$ is $e_b$), so $e_{G-a}(S'\setminus\{b\})=|S'|-2=|S'\setminus\{b\}|-1$ with $|S'\setminus\{b\}|\ge2$, violating (P3) in $G-a$; so $e_b\subseteq S'$, and likewise $e_c\subseteq S'$. Put $S_i=S'\setminus\{b,c\}\subseteq V(G'')$. The edges of $G-a$ are those of $G''$ together with $e_b,e_c$ (1.1), so $e_{G''}(S_i)=e_{G-a}(S')-2=|S'|-4=|S_i|-2$; and $S_i\supseteq\{a_i\}\cup B\cup C$, so $|S_i|\ge5$. Thus $S_i$ is a saturated set of $G''$.
5.5 $|S_1\cap S_2|\ge|B\cup C|=4$, so by 0.7 ($G''\in C$) $S:=S_1\cup S_2$ is a saturated set of $G''$, and $W=\{a_1,a_2\}\cup B\cup C\subseteq S$.

### Step 6. Every vertex of $W$ has $G''$-degree 2 (new)
6.1 Apply 0.8 to $F=G''$ ($\Delta(G'')\le\Delta(G)\le3$) and the saturated set $S$ of 5.5: $\sum_{t\in S}(3-d_{G''[S]}(t))=6$ with all terms $\ge0$.
6.2 For $w\in W\subseteq S$: $d_{G''[S]}(w)\le d_{G''}(w)\le2$ (1.3), so $3-d_{G''[S]}(w)\ge1$. As $|W|=6$ (Step 4), the six terms for $W$ already sum to at least 6; hence each equals 1 and all other terms vanish: $d_{G''[S]}(w)=2$ for every $w\in W$, and therefore $d_{G''}(w)=2$ for every $w\in W$ (by 1.3).
6.3 (For Remark 5; not needed below.) Every $t\in S\setminus W$ has $d_{G''[S]}(t)=3\ge d_{G''}(t)$, and every $w\in W$ has $d_{G''[S]}(w)=2=d_{G''}(w)$; so every edge of $G''$ meeting $S$ lies inside $S$. As $G''$ is connected (Step 3), $S=V(G'')$. So $G''$ is saturated ($m''=N-2$), all its vertices outside $W$ have degree 3, and in $G$ the only vertices of degree $\le2$ are $a,b,c$. In particular the largest saturated set $S_0\supseteq W$ of L025 Theorem 12(j) is $V(G'')$, and L025's sub-case (R1b) cannot occur.

### Step 7. The contradiction (new)
7.1 By 6.2, $d_{G''}(a_1)=2$. Each of the two edges of $a_1$ contains at most one vertex of $W$ besides $a_1$ (2.5), and $a_2$ is not adjacent to $a_1$ (1.2); so $a_1$ is adjacent to at most two of the four vertices of $B\cup C$. Choose $w\in B\cup C$ not adjacent to $a_1$; by the symmetry between $B$ and $C$ we may assume $w\in B$. By 6.2, $d_{G''}(w)=2$, so (0.4) $|\partial_{G''}(\{a_1,w\})|=2+2-0=4$.
7.2 Let $Q$ be the 3-graph $(G''-a_1-w)+\{t\}\cup C$, where $t$ is a new vertex: delete $a_1$ and $w$ (with their four edges) and add the *pendant edge* $\{t\}\cup C$ on the third pair. Then $n(Q)=N-1=n-4$ and $m(Q)=m''-4+1=m''-3$.
7.3 *$Q\in C$.* $G''-a_1-w\in C$ (0.1), and $C\subseteq V(G''-a_1-w)$ (Step 4). The new edge meets an old edge $f$ in $f\cap C$, of size $\le1$ since $C$ lies inside no edge of $G''$ (1.2): linear. $t$ has degree 1 and $F_7$ has minimum degree 2, so a copy of $F_7$ would avoid the new edge and lie in $G''-a_1-w\in C$: none. (P3): for $S\not\ni t$ nothing changes; $S=\{t,y\}$ has no edge; and for $S\ni t$ with $|S\setminus\{t\}|\ge2$, $e_Q(S)\le e_{G''-a_1-w}(S\setminus\{t\})+1\le|S|-3+1=|S|-2$.
7.4 *$\tau(Q)\ge\tau''$.* Let $T'$ be a transversal of $Q$ and $T=T'\setminus\{t\}$. Every edge of $G''-a_1-w$ is an edge of $Q$ not containing $t$, so $T$ is a transversal of $G''-a_1-w$, and $T\cup\{a_1,w\}$ is a transversal of $G''$ with $|T|+2$ vertices ($a_1,w\notin T$). So $|T|\ge\tau''-2$.
- If $|T|=\tau''-2$, then $T\cup\{a_1,w\}$ is a minimum transversal of $G''$ containing $a_1\in W$, contradicting 2.3.
- If $|T|=\tau''-1$, then $T\cup\{a_1,w\}$ is a $(\tau''+1)$-transversal of $G''$ meeting $A$ (in $a_1$) and $B$ (in $w$); by 2.4 it does not meet $C$, so $T\cap C=\emptyset$, the new edge $\{t\}\cup C$ forces $t\in T'$, and $|T'|=\tau''$.
- If $|T|\ge\tau''$, then $|T'|\ge\tau''$.
7.5 Hence $s(Q)=3(N-1)+2(m''-3)-11\tau(Q)\le3N+2m''-11\tau''-9=s(G'')-9\le-1$ (2.2). By 7.3, $Q$ is a member of $C$ with $n-4$ vertices violating I11, contradicting 1.5. This proves Theorem 1. $\square$

*Remark 7.6 (what Step 7 is).* $Q$ is the pendant replacement of L025 Lemma 3, $G^{\rm p}(w;c_1,c_2)$, applied inside $K=G''-a_1$ (slack $s(G'')-7\le1$ by 0.3, as $a_1$ is avoided with degree 2): in $K$ the vertex $w$ is NC with both vertices of $C$ (a minimum transversal of $K$ containing $w$ and $c_j$, plus $a_1$, would be a $(\tau''+1)$-transversal of $G''$ meeting $A,B,C$). ### Step 8. Proof of Corollary 2
8.1 Let $G''$, $A,B,C$ be as in Corollary 2 and suppose every member of $C$ with at most $n(G'')+3$ vertices satisfies I11. Let $G$ be $G''$ plus three new vertices $a,b,c$ and the pendant edges $\{a\}\cup A$, $\{b\}\cup B$, $\{c\}\cup C$. $G\in C$: the new edges are pairwise disjoint (the pairs are disjoint), each meets an edge of $G''$ in at most one vertex (no pair lies inside an edge of $G''$), and each contains a vertex of degree 1, so linearity holds, no copy of $F_7$ (minimum degree 2) uses a new edge, and (P3) follows by adding the edges one at a time as in 7.3. $G$ is connected ($G''$ is, and each new edge meets it), $\Delta(G)\le3$ (the vertices of $W$ have $G''$-degree $\le2$ and gain one edge each), and $n(G)=n(G'')+3$.
8.2 $\tau(G)\ge\tau''+2$: for a transversal $T$ of $G$, $T_0=T\cap V(G'')$ is a transversal of $G''$; if $|T_0|=\tau''$ it misses $A,B,C$ by (i), and $T$ needs $a,b,c$, so $|T|\ge\tau''+3$; if $|T_0|=\tau''+1$ it misses one of the pairs by (ii) and $|T|\ge\tau''+2$; otherwise $|T|\ge|T_0|\ge\tau''+2$. If $\tau(G)\ge\tau''+3$, then $s(G)\le s(G'')+9+6-33<0$, a violator with $n(G'')+3$ vertices, excluded. So $\tau(G)=\tau''+2$ and $s(G)=s(G'')+15-22\le1$.
8.3 $\{a,b,c\}$ is a pendant b-configuration of $G$: $a,b,c$ have degree 1 and lie in three different edges; a minimum transversal $T$ of $G$ containing $a$ and $b$ would have $|T\cap V(G'')|\le\tau''$, so $T\cap V(G'')$ would be a minimum transversal of $G''$, which misses $C$ by (i); then $c\in T$ and $|T|\ge\tau''+3$, a contradiction; symmetrically for the other pairs.
8.4 Theorem 1 applies to $G$ (connected, $\Delta\le3$, $s(G)\le1$, and I11 below $n(G)=n(G'')+3$ by assumption) and gives a contradiction. $\square$
