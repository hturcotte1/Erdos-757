3.1    The Family Hk
Definition 5. Let Hk be the class of all connected, linear, k-uniform hypergraphs with
maximum degree 2.
    For a hypergraph H ∈ Hk we define a graph GH as follows. Let the vertices of GH be
the edges of H and let the edges of GH correspond to the n2 (H) vertices of degree 2 in
H: if a vertex of H is contained in the edges e and f of H, then the corresponding edge
of the multigraph GH joins vertices e and f of GH . Thus, V (GH ) = E(H) and for every
v ∈ V2 (H), contained in the two edges e and f , add an edge between e and f in GH . By
the linearity of H, the multigraph GH is indeed a graph, called the dual graph of H. Since
H is k-uniform and ∆(H) = 2, the maximum degree, ∆(GH ), in GH is at most k. Since
H is connected, so too is GH . By construction, n(GH ) = m(H) and m(GH ) = n2 (H).
We note that if H ∈ Hk is 2-regular, then the dual graph, GH , of H is k-regular.
