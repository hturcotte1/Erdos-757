## Problems found (in order of severity)

None that affects the claims. Minor remarks:

1. (Cosmetic, certificate text.) Step 2.1 says the unchanged engine keeps "every labelled graph" in Python lists; the actual memory consumer on the direct route is `run()`'s `capture_output` of `enum_conn`'s whole output plus the list of FOUND lines, which is what the streamed driver avoids. No effect on the proof.
2. (Cosmetic.) The table in Step 3.2 mixes two meanings of the column "in $C$": for direct rows it is the number of labelled graphs in $C$ with $\tau=t$ (`in_C_tau_exact`), for recursion rows the number of HIT lines (`hits_in_C`, before the connectivity filter). The headings say so, but a reader may miss it.
3. (Scope note, not a gap.) Theorem 1 depends on the completeness of the lists `cell(22,18,9,3)` (94,513) and `cell(19,17,8,3)` (29,898), which are produced by the recursion and therefore by the full induction chain of Lemma E''. The certificate does not (and cannot) check them against a direct enumeration; its evidence for them is the agreement with the worker's run (same binaries, different driver), C7/C8 on smaller cells, and the audit C9. My own one-level recomputations ((19,17,8,3) from the prover's lower lists, below; (21,18,9,3), (17,14,7,3), (20,15,8,3)), the genbg re-enumeration of all 86 direct lists and of (14,12,6,3), and round 1's full bottom-up recomputation (per the STATUS line) cover this. A genbg enumeration of (20,12,7,3) (310,263 graphs for (20,11); the (20,12) class is much larger) was started and is reported below only if it finished within the budget.
4. (Process, no effect.) The STATUS line of the certificate changed on disk during this review (the round-1/round-2/3 bookkeeping was added); the mathematical content read at the start and at the end is the same.

## Independence statement

All programs used for the decisions above were written by me during this review and share no code with `experiments/prover/*`, `e757/` or `judge/`: the decompositions (partition-first), the tree dry run, the input generator, the extension search (`refext`: plain enumeration of all disjoint pair-triples with an own cover enumeration by branching; `refext2`: with a complete restriction, used only where the brute force was too slow), the $F_7$ test (explicit embedding, not Lemma F's degree pattern), the (P3) test (Hall with an explicitly verified violator), the exact $\tau$ (branching, and brute force over all subsets for $C_{23}$), the graph6 filter for genbg output, and the witness check. The only shared tools are unmodified nauty 2.8.8 (`genbg`, `labelg`, built by me from the tarball) and the prover's own cell lists, which are the *inputs* of the one-level recomputations (their completeness is what the recursion chain and round 1 establish; their soundness is irrelevant for Theorem 1). The prover's `engine.decompositions` was imported for a comparison only. I did not read the round-1 review before writing the verdict and the checks above.
